Meta changed direction on AI in 2026. Its Superintelligence Labs, led by Alexandr Wang, released Muse Spark in April, the first new frontier model since Llama 4 a year earlier. Unlike Llama, Muse Spark is closed. Artificial Analysis scored it 52 on its Intelligence Index, well above Llama 4 Maverick at 18. Meta says it hopes to open-source future versions but has not given a date.

Llama is still around. The Llama 3 and 4 models remain widely used by developers who want weights they can run themselves, and Meta has said it will keep supporting them. The Muse line now powers the Meta AI app, with versions for code and image generation, and reports describe a second model aimed at generating media.

Meta's research group also keeps releasing work outside language models. Segment Anything (SAM) and DINOv2 are common building blocks in computer vision, and Seamless and Audiocraft cover translation and audio. On the hardware side, Meta designs its own MTIA chips for ranking and recommendation workloads.
