Intel is behind NVIDIA and AMD in AI accelerators, and its own plans have shifted more than once. Gaudi 3 is still sold for training and inference through cloud partners, but reports say sales were weak and a planned successor, Falcon Shores, was cancelled for commercial release. The company's current bet is Crescent Island, an inference-only data center GPU on the Xe3P architecture with 160 GB of LPDDR5X memory instead of HBM, which lowers cost at the expense of bandwidth.

Intel targeted customer sampling in the second half of 2026. Some reports say the launch may slip into 2027, and analysts note it trails AMD's MI355X and NVIDIA's B200 on raw memory bandwidth and matrix throughput. Intel is betting that inference cost matters more than peak speed.

On the client side, Panther Lake is the first chip on the 18A process and puts a stronger GPU and NPU in laptops. OpenVINO, Intel's toolkit for optimizing models to run on its hardware, is the software side of the strategy and works across CPUs, GPUs and NPUs.
