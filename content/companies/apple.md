Apple's AI strategy is built around on-device processing and Private Cloud Compute. Apple Intelligence runs a small foundation model on the device and sends heavier requests to Apple's own servers. The Foundation Models framework lets third-party apps use the on-device model, and the current version also supports models from other providers.

The biggest change is Siri. Apple and Google agreed to a multi-year deal in which the next generation of Apple Foundation Models is built on Gemini. At WWDC on June 8, 2026, Apple showed Siri AI for iOS 27, iPadOS 27 and macOS 27, with a public release planned for the fall. Reports say it will not launch at first in the EU or China. A figure of about $1 billion a year for the deal circulates in the press but is unconfirmed.

Apple makes its own silicon for this work, from the M-series chips in Macs to the A-series in iPhones, and the Neural Engine in each handles much of the local inference.
