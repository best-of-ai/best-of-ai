NVIDIA is best known for the GPUs that train and serve most large models, and the Vera Rubin platform shown at GTC in March 2026 is its next step. It combines seven chips, including the Vera CPU, Rubin GPU, NVLink 6 switch and a Groq-designed inference processor. NVIDIA's own figures claim a large drop in cost per token compared with Blackwell, though those numbers come from the company and have not been independently tested.

The company also ships software and models of its own. NIM packages models as ready-to-deploy microservices, and Nemotron is its family of open models. Nemotron 3 Super, released on March 11, 2026, has 120 billion parameters with about 12 billion active at a time. At GTC NVIDIA also announced NemoClaw, an open platform for running enterprise AI agents with security controls, along with a Nemotron Coalition that includes Mistral, Cursor, Perplexity and Black Forest Labs.

Cosmos, NVIDIA's world model for robotics and autonomous driving, covers the physical AI side. Keynote demos leaned heavily on robots, and the company also announced a plan for a data center in space.
