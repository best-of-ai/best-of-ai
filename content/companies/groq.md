Groq makes the LPU, a chip built for fast inference that keeps model weights in on-chip SRAM instead of external memory. The company says this lets it generate tokens with far lower latency and, by its claim, about a tenth of the power of GPUs. GroqCloud sells access to open models running on that hardware through an API.

In December 2025, NVIDIA agreed to a non-exclusive license of Groq's inference technology. Reports put the deal around $20 billion. It was not a full acquisition: founder Jonathan Ross and president Sunny Madra joined NVIDIA, while Groq stayed independent under CEO Simon Edwards and GroqCloud kept running. Commentators called it a reverse acquihire, a structure that avoids the antitrust review of a standard merger.

The technology has since shown up in NVIDIA's roadmap. A Groq-derived LPU is one of the seven chips in NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform from GTC 2026. We found no 2026 reporting on regulatory review or changes to GroqCloud.
