Todoist is a clean, fast task manager. You add tasks by typing in plain language, like "send report every Friday at 3pm," and it turns that into a recurring due date. Tasks can be organized into projects and sections, given priorities and labels, and viewed as a list, board, or calendar. Filters let you build views such as everything due today across all projects. It syncs across phones, computers, browsers, and email clients.

The AI assistant helps with planning. It can break a large task into smaller steps, suggest how to word a task, help make a project template, and answer questions about what is on your plate. Other features include reminders, comments, file attachments, and shared projects with assignments for teams or families. Karma and productivity charts reward consistent completion, and integrations connect it with Google Calendar, Slack, Gmail, and automation tools like Zapier.

Todoist has a free plan with limits on projects and features, and paid plans add reminders, more projects, filters, and AI access, with a team tier. It aims for simplicity, so it has fewer project management features than Asana or ClickUp, such as dependencies or detailed reporting. That simplicity is the reason many people stay with it for years. Alternatives include TickTick, Things (Apple only), Microsoft To Do, and Notion for people who want tasks and notes together.
