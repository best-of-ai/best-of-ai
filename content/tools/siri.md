Siri is the assistant that comes with every Apple device. You say "Hey Siri" or hold a button, then ask it to send a message, set a timer, call someone, play music, adjust a setting, or control HomeKit devices. It works across Apple's hardware, so the same assistant is on your wrist, in your car through CarPlay, and on a smart speaker. Its biggest advantage is how deeply it ties into the system, since it can act on messages, calendars, reminders, and photos without extra setup.

Apple has spent years trying to make Siri less rigid. With Apple Intelligence, requests can be phrased more naturally, Siri can follow a conversation, and it can take action inside apps that support the right developer interfaces. You can type to it instead of speaking, and it can hand some questions to ChatGPT when you allow it. Privacy is a central part of Apple's pitch, with many requests handled on the device and heavier ones sent to Apple's Private Cloud Compute servers.

Siri is free and cannot be installed separately from Apple's devices. Its weak point has long been how it handles open-ended questions and multi-step requests, where it has fallen behind assistants built on large language models, and Apple has been reworking it to close that gap. Which features you get depends on your device, language, and software version. If you live in Apple's ecosystem it is the most convenient assistant for controlling your phone and home. For research and writing, most people still reach for ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude.
