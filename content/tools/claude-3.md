The Claude 4 family is structured around three tiers that cover different cost and capability trade-offs. Opus 4.7 is the most capable for complex reasoning, extended analysis, and tasks that benefit from deep thinking. Sonnet 4.6 hits the practical sweet spot for most production applications — strong performance with response speeds and costs that work at scale. Haiku 4.5 is optimised for speed and cost, making it the right choice for high-volume classification, extraction, and triage tasks.

All three models share the 200,000-token context window that Claude has become known for, which means they can process entire codebases, legal documents, research papers, or lengthy conversation histories in a single API call without chunking or summarising the input. The Constitutional AI training approach means the models are unusually reliable about staying within boundaries, declining problematic requests without being unnecessarily restrictive, and being honest about uncertainty. This predictability matters for enterprise applications where model behaviour needs to be consistent across millions of interactions.

The API is available through Anthropic directly or through AWS Bedrock and Google Cloud Vertex AI for teams already operating on those platforms. Prompt caching reduces costs for applications with repeated context (system prompts, reference documents, few-shot examples). Extended thinking mode in Opus lets the model work through complex problems step-by-step before responding. Claude is increasingly the API choice for writing-heavy applications — legal tech, content platforms, documentation tools — where the quality of the prose output matters as much as the accuracy of the reasoning.
