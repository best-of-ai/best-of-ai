DreamBox Math is designed to give every student lessons at the right level. As a child works through interactive, game-based problems, the software tracks not only whether answers are correct but also how the child solves them: the strategies used, the time taken, and the kinds of mistakes made. It uses that information to adjust the next activity, moving ahead when the student is ready and revisiting ideas when they are shaky.

The curriculum covers number sense, operations, fractions, and algebra readiness across the grade levels, with an emphasis on conceptual understanding over memorizing procedures. Teachers see dashboards showing time spent, lessons completed, and skills mastered, and they can assign practice or identify students who need extra help. A reading program follows a similar adaptive approach. The company is now part of Discovery Education.

DreamBox is sold to schools and districts, with some options for families, under paid licenses. Studies of adaptive math programs show benefits when students use them regularly for the recommended time, and little effect when use is sporadic. It supplements teaching but does not replace it. Competitors include IXL, Khan Academy, Carnegie Learning, ST Math, and Zearn, some of which are free. District buyers usually compare evidence of results, alignment to standards, and cost per student.
