Darktrace takes a different approach from signature-based security. Instead of matching known threats, it learns the usual patterns of each organization, such as which devices talk to which systems and how people normally log in, and then notices when something deviates. This lets it detect novel attacks and insider threats that do not match a known signature. The learning happens inside the customer's environment, and Darktrace says it does not need to share data with a central model to do it.

The company sells a platform that covers several areas. Products protect networks, email, cloud services, endpoints, identity, and industrial control systems, and an autonomous response capability can take action such as blocking a connection or interrupting an attack in progress. Investigation tools use AI to group related alerts into incidents and write summaries for analysts, which helps teams that are short on staff.

Darktrace is sold by subscription to organizations, with custom pricing, and has been taken private by an investment firm. Critics point to alert volume and to the difficulty of verifying how well anomaly detection performs compared with other approaches, and buyers often run trials to test it. Competitors include CrowdStrike, Vectra AI, Microsoft Defender, Palo Alto Networks, and ExtraHop. It suits organizations that want AI-based detection and automated containment with limited security staff.
