Synthesis grew out of a school experiment. The idea is that children learn best by working together on puzzles, simulations, and strategic games that require reasoning, instead of by memorizing procedures. Sessions are live and collaborative: a small group of children plays through challenges guided by a facilitator, which builds skills in math, logic, teamwork, and communication.

A more recent product, the Synthesis Tutor, uses AI to give each child personalized practice. It adapts to what a child knows, presents math concepts through games and stories, and gives feedback that encourages persistence. Parents get information about progress. The program is designed for elementary and middle school ages and is used by homeschoolers and others looking for enrichment.

Synthesis is a paid subscription with different offerings for live sessions and the tutor, and a trial. Research evidence on its outcomes is limited compared with more established curricula, so parents should weigh it as enrichment instead of a full replacement. Alternatives include Khan Academy and Khanmigo, which are free, DreamBox, Prodigy, Beast Academy, and Art of Problem Solving for challenging math. Synthesis suits families who want collaborative, game-based learning.
