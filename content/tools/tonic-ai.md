Tonic.ai solves a common development problem: engineers need realistic data to test software, but production data contains sensitive information. Tonic Structural connects to a database, identifies sensitive fields, and replaces them with fake but realistic values, preserving relationships and formats so applications behave as they would with real data. This allows teams to build and test with representative data without exposing customers' details.

Other products broaden the range. Tonic Fabricate generates synthetic data from scratch for new applications or demos, and Tonic Textual finds and redacts sensitive information in unstructured data such as documents and text, which helps prepare data for training or retrieval in AI systems. Integrations support popular databases and data warehouses, and subsetting tools cut large databases down to smaller, usable test sets.

Tonic is sold to companies under subscription agreements, with deployment options including on-premises environments, and a free trial. Setting up requires understanding the data model and sensitive fields, and careful review to avoid leaks. Competitors include Gretel, which is now part of NVIDIA, MOSTLY AI, Delphix, K2view, and open-source libraries. Tonic suits engineering and data teams working with regulated or sensitive data.
