Deepgram sells speech AI to developers. Its speech-to-text API transcribes audio in real time or from recordings, and it is designed for accuracy at low latency and low cost, which matters for call centers, meeting tools, and voice assistants that process large volumes. Features include speaker labeling, punctuation, keyword boosting, and support for many languages, and models can be customized to a company's vocabulary.

The platform has widened to cover the whole voice stack. A text-to-speech product generates natural voices with low latency, and a voice agent API combines speech recognition, a language model, and speech synthesis into a single service for building conversational agents that can hold phone calls. Enterprises can run Deepgram in their own cloud or on-premises when data cannot leave their control.

Pricing is pay-as-you-go by audio duration, with free starting credit and volume discounts, plus enterprise agreements. Deepgram is aimed at engineers and not at end users who want a ready-made note taker. Competitors include AssemblyAI, OpenAI's Whisper and speech models, Google Cloud Speech-to-Text, and AWS Transcribe, as well as ElevenLabs for voice. Testing on your own audio is essential, since accuracy varies with accents, background noise, and domain terms.
