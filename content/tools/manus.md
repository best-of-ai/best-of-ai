Manus gained attention as an agent that completes whole tasks instead of answering questions. You describe a goal, such as researching a market, comparing products, building a small site, or analyzing a spreadsheet, and Manus plans the work, opens a browser, writes and runs code in its own cloud environment, and produces deliverables like reports, slides, and files. You can watch what it does and step in, and it continues running in the background.

It pulls together tools: a browser for research and actions, a code environment for data work, and file creation for documents and presentations. Newer versions allow connections to outside apps and scheduled tasks. The agent runs on top of language models from various providers and uses multi-agent orchestration to split larger jobs. It is available on the web and in apps, and it is used by professionals and small teams for research and content tasks.

Manus has a free allowance and paid subscriptions based on credits that tasks consume, so complex jobs can cost a lot. Like all agents, it can make mistakes or get stuck, and anything it produces should be checked. Meta announced an acquisition of Manus and said it would continue the product while bringing its technology into Meta AI, so details may evolve. Alternatives include ChatGPT Work and agent mode, Claude Cowork, Genspark, and Perplexity's agents.
