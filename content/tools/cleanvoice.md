Cleanvoice does one job: it cleans up speech. You upload a podcast episode, interview, or video, and it finds the places where a speaker said "um" or "uh," paused for too long, made lip smacks or breath sounds, or repeated a word. It removes them and returns a tidier file. Editing these by hand takes a long time, and a one-hour recording can contain hundreds of them, so automating it saves real hours for podcasters and video creators.

You decide what gets removed. Settings let you keep natural pauses, target particular fillers, or remove only the loudest mouth noises, and a noise reduction and voice enhancement option cleans up the background. Cleanvoice accepts common audio and video formats, and instead of a flattened file it can export the edits to editors such as Audacity, Adobe Audition, DaVinci Resolve, and Reaper, so you can adjust them. A Python, JavaScript, and REST API allows batch processing.

New users get a block of free minutes, and after that you pay by subscription or by usage. Results are good on clear speech but can cut too aggressively or miss fillers in noisy, overlapping, or accented recordings, so a quick listen is still needed. It supports many languages, though accuracy differs by language. Descript, Auphonic, and Adobe Podcast cover related ground, with Descript offering full transcript-based editing instead of just cleanup.
