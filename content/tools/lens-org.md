The Lens aims to make innovation data open. It combines a very large database of patents from patent offices worldwide with scholarly works from sources like Crossref, PubMed, and others. Users can search by keywords, inventors, institutions, or classifications, and the results show patent families, legal status, citations, and links to the full text where available. The free access sets it apart from commercial patent databases.

The key feature is the connection between patents and papers. It tracks which scholarly works are cited in patents, which lets researchers and technology transfer offices see how academic research is used in inventions, and lets innovators find prior art. Collections let you save and share sets of results, and analytics and dashboards visualize trends, such as top applicants or fast-growing technology areas. Sequence search supports biotechnology patents.

Lens.org is run by Cambia, a nonprofit, and is free for individual use, with paid subscriptions for institutions and API access that supports larger volumes and commercial applications. Legal status data can be incomplete or lag, so formal freedom-to-operate decisions need professional search services. Alternatives include Google Patents, which is free but less analytical, Espacenet, PatentScope, and commercial tools such as Derwent Innovation, PatSnap, and Orbit. It's a great starting point for students, startups, and researchers.
