DNAnexus gives life science organizations a place to work with very large datasets. Sequencing a genome produces enormous files, and analyzing thousands of them takes substantial computing and careful data management. The platform provides cloud storage, scalable compute, workflow tools, and a catalog of bioinformatics pipelines, so teams can run analyses without managing their own servers. It is built to meet regulatory and privacy requirements for clinical and health data.

Collaboration is a defining feature. Multiple organizations can share datasets and tools in controlled environments, while access policies keep sensitive information protected. The platform has been used for large national research programs, where researchers analyze data inside a secure workspace and bring their code to the data instead of downloading it. Tools for cohort building and analysis help researchers find patterns across genomic and clinical information, and AI and machine learning workflows can be run on the same data.

DNAnexus is sold to enterprises, health systems, and research programs under custom contracts, with costs tied to storage and compute use. It requires bioinformatics expertise to get the most out of it. Competitors and alternatives include Seven Bridges (now part of Velsera), Terra from the Broad Institute and partners, Illumina's connected software, and the genomics services offered by AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft. It suits organizations with regulated, large-scale genomic work.
