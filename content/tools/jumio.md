Jumio helps businesses confirm that a customer is who they claim to be. A user photographs an ID document, such as a passport or driver's license, and takes a selfie. Jumio checks the document for authenticity, reads the data, compares the selfie to the photo on the ID, and uses liveness detection to make sure a real person is present, not a photo, video, or deepfake. The process takes seconds and replaces in-person checks.

The company covers the wider identity lifecycle. Services include ongoing monitoring, screening against sanctions and watchlists, checks on age and address, and signals that detect fraud rings and suspicious behavior across accounts. It supports identity documents from many countries, which helps global financial services, crypto platforms, online marketplaces, and gig economy companies. AI and machine learning are central to document analysis and fraud detection, and the company works to counter synthetic identities and deepfakes.

Jumio is sold to businesses with pricing based on verification volume, and it is aimed at regulated industries. Biometric verification raises privacy and bias issues, and organizations must follow laws on biometric data, which vary by jurisdiction. Competitors include Onfido, owned by Entrust, Persona, Veriff, Socure, and Sumsub. Choosing a provider usually involves testing accuracy across your user base, checking regional coverage, and reviewing how data is stored and retained.
