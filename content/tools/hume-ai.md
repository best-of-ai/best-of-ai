Hume AI started from research on human emotion. Its models analyze the way people speak and show emotion, including tone, pace, and inflection, and its Empathic Voice Interface (EVI) uses that understanding in a conversational voice assistant. EVI listens to the user's voice, picks up on cues like frustration or excitement, and adjusts its responses and speaking style accordingly, so a voice agent can feel more natural than one that only transcribes words.

The platform has several parts. EVI is the real-time voice interface for apps and agents, Octave is a text-to-speech model that can be directed with natural-language descriptions of how a voice should sound, and expression measurement APIs analyze speech, faces, and text for emotional signals. Developers connect through APIs and SDKs, and it can be used with various language models. Use cases include coaching, customer service, wellness apps, and games.

Hume has a free tier for developers and usage-based pricing. In a licensing deal, Google DeepMind hired Hume's CEO and several engineers, and Hume said it would keep operating and supply its technology to other labs, so the company's direction may change. Emotion detection from voice is imprecise and raises ethical questions about consent and manipulation. ElevenLabs, OpenAI's voice models, Cartesia, and Deepgram are alternatives for voice agents.
