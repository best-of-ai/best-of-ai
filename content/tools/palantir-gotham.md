Gotham helps organizations that work with sensitive and scattered data make decisions. It pulls information from many systems, such as databases, reports, sensor feeds, and documents, into a unified model of people, places, events, and relationships, called an ontology. Analysts can search, visualize connections, map activity, and collaborate on investigations, and operational users can plan and track missions.

The platform is used in defense, intelligence, and public safety. Capabilities include link analysis, geospatial views, timelines, and tools for managing access to classified or restricted data, and it can run in secure and air-gapped environments. Palantir's Artificial Intelligence Platform adds large language models that work with the same data under access controls, allowing users to ask questions and automate analysis.

Gotham is sold to governments and large agencies under contracts, and it is not available to the public. Palantir attracts controversy, including debates about surveillance, immigration enforcement, privacy, and military uses, which organizations and the public weigh differently. Competitors and alternatives include Anduril's software, Databricks and Snowflake for data platforms, Esri for geospatial, Babel Street, and Cognyte. Gotham suits agencies with complex, sensitive data and mission needs.
