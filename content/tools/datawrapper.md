Datawrapper was built by journalists for journalists. A user pastes or uploads data, chooses a chart type such as a bar, line, scatter plot, or map, and adjusts labels, colors, and annotations. The result is a responsive graphic that works on phones and desktops, and can be embedded in a story or exported as an image.

Its design defaults follow good data visualization practice, so charts look clear without fiddling. Features include color-blind friendly palettes, tables with inline bars, locator maps, and a way to update charts automatically from a linked data source. News organizations around the world use it, and teams can share themes to keep a consistent style.

Datawrapper has a free tier for basic use and paid plans for teams, custom branding, and more exports. It focuses on publication-ready charts rather than interactive dashboards or complex analysis. Alternatives include Flourish, Tableau Public, Infogram, Canva charts, and Observable. Datawrapper suits journalists and communicators who publish data stories.
