Spellbook was built to meet lawyers where they work: in Word. As you draft or review a contract, it suggests language, flags missing or risky terms, and proposes edits as tracked changes. You can ask it questions about a clause, have it compare a draft with your standard positions, or generate a first draft from a prompt, always inside the document you are working on.

Features extend to the review and negotiation steps. A library of benchmarks shows how a term compares to market standards, a playbook feature lets firms encode their preferred positions for checking contracts, and agent-style workflows can run multi-step tasks such as reviewing a set of agreements. It's used by law firms and in-house teams, particularly for transactional work, and builds on leading language models with legal-specific prompts and data.

Spellbook is paid, per user, with a trial, and aimed at legal professionals. Its suggestions can contain mistakes, so lawyers have to review them, and confidential client data requires attention to vendor security terms. Competitors include Harvey, Robin AI, Ironclad, Luminance, Thomson Reuters CoCounsel, and Microsoft Copilot in Word. Spellbook is regarded as one of the more practical tools for day-to-day drafting.
