ArcGIS is the leading commercial GIS platform. Governments, utilities, environmental agencies, city planners, and businesses use it to collect location data, display it on maps, and analyze spatial relationships, such as which neighborhoods lie within a flood zone or where to place a new store. The product family includes ArcGIS Pro for desktop analysis, ArcGIS Online as a cloud service for building and sharing maps, and ArcGIS Enterprise for organizations that run their own servers.

Additional tools extend it. StoryMaps combines maps with narrative for presentations, Survey123 and Field Maps collect data in the field, and the Living Atlas provides a large library of ready-made layers, from demographics to satellite imagery. GeoAI features use machine learning to detect objects such as buildings, vehicles, or land cover in imagery, and an AI assistant helps users find data, run analysis, and write code through plain language.

ArcGIS is licensed by user type and capability, and costs can be substantial, with discounts for education and nonprofits. There is a steep learning curve for advanced analysis. QGIS is the major free, open-source alternative, and Google Earth Engine, Mapbox, and CARTO serve different parts of the market. Organizations that rely on established geospatial workflows and support often stay with Esri, while newer web-first teams choose lighter tools.
