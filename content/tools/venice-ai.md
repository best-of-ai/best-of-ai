Venice AI builds its pitch around privacy and freedom of use. The company states it does not log or store conversations on its servers, and that chats are kept in the user's browser, which appeals to people uneasy about their prompts being retained or used for training. It runs open-source models, so users can pick from several language models and an image generator, and the platform applies fewer content restrictions than many mainstream services.

Features include text chat, image generation, document uploads, and a developer API. A Pro plan raises limits and unlocks more models and features. Venice is associated with a cryptocurrency token that offers access benefits, which some users like and others see as an unnecessary complication. The company positions itself as an alternative to centralized AI platforms.

Venice has a free tier with limits and paid plans. Privacy claims are hard to verify independently, and fewer restrictions mean more potential for misuse, so users should apply their own judgment. The open models it hosts are less capable than the latest proprietary systems. Alternatives include running models locally with Ollama or LM Studio, Brave Leo, DuckDuckGo's AI chat, and Proton's Lumo. Venice suits users prioritizing privacy and open models.
