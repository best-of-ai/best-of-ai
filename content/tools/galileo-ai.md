Galileo AI was one of the early tools for turning a prompt into interface designs. You described a screen, such as a fitness app dashboard or a checkout page, and it produced a detailed, editable design with components, imagery, and layout, which could be copied into Figma and refined. It was popular with product designers and founders as a way to explore ideas and get past the blank canvas.

Google acquired Galileo AI and brought its team and technology into its own design-generation product, Stitch, which creates interfaces from text or images and exports to Figma and code. The standalone Galileo service was wound down as part of that move.

People looking for similar functionality can use Google Stitch, Figma Make and First Draft, Uizard, Claude Design, v0 for web UI in code, and Lovable. These tools differ in whether they produce editable design files or working code, so choose based on what you want to do with the result. This entry stays for reference because older articles still recommend Galileo AI.
