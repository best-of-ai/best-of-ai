Aurora Solar is used by solar installers, designers, and sales teams. A designer enters an address and the software builds a 3D model of the property from aerial imagery and lidar data, including trees and nearby buildings that cast shade. It then lays out panels, models how much electricity the system will produce across the year, and calculates savings and payback so a homeowner sees the economics.

Aurora automates much of the work that used to take hours. AI generates roof models and code-compliant panel layouts in minutes, handles stringing and fire pathways, and prepares permit-ready plan sets. Sales tools produce branded proposals and handle financing options and contracts, and drone mapping can capture detail for larger or more complex sites. For commercial projects, the platform covers larger arrays and different incentives.

Aurora is sold on subscription to solar businesses, with plans scaled to team size and features, not to homeowners. The accuracy of its predictions depends on the quality of source imagery and local data on incentives and tariffs. Competitors include OpenSolar, which is free for installers, Helioscope for commercial design, and PVsyst for detailed engineering simulation. Solar installers use Aurora to shorten the path from lead to a signed contract.
