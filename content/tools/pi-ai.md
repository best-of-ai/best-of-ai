Pi was built to be a kind and curious conversational partner instead of a productivity tool. Its responses are short, warm, and conversational, and it often asks follow-up questions, which made it a favorite with people who wanted to talk through ideas, plans, or feelings in a low-pressure setting. It supports voice conversations with several voices, which suits casual use.

Inflection AI, founded by former DeepMind and LinkedIn leaders, launched Pi with the aim of making a personal AI. The company later saw its founders and much of its team join Microsoft, and it pivoted to licensing its models to enterprises. Pi remains online as a free consumer product at pi.ai and in apps, but active development has slowed, and the messaging integrations it once had are gone.

Pi is free. It is weaker than top assistants for tasks such as coding, research, and long documents, and it is not a substitute for professional mental health support. Because the future of the app is less certain, treat it as a casual companion and don't rely on it for important data. Alternatives include ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Replika for companionship, and Wysa for guided wellbeing. Pi suits people who want a gentle chat partner.
