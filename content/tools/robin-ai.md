Robin AI developed tools for lawyers who spend their days reading contracts. Its platform used language models trained on legal text to review agreements, flag risks, suggest edits to bring them in line with a company's playbook, and answer questions about clauses. It worked in Microsoft Word and was used by in-house legal teams and law firms for routine commercial agreements such as NDAs, supplier contracts, and sales terms.

The company also combined software with a managed legal services offering, in which lawyers reviewed work and handled volume for clients. That mix aimed to deliver quick, reliable turnaround, with AI doing the first pass and humans checking. It gained attention for fast growth and for partnerships and funding in the legal AI boom.

Robin AI later struggled to raise further funding, reduced staff, and sold its managed services division to another firm, while its technology platform was in separate negotiations. Because of this, customers and prospective buyers need to check who operates the platform today and what support exists. Alternatives include Spellbook, Harvey, Luminance, Ironclad, Kira, and Thomson Reuters CoCounsel. As with any legal AI, review by a qualified lawyer is essential.
