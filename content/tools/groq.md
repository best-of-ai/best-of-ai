Groq is known for speed. Its chips, called Language Processing Units, were designed from scratch for running AI models rather than adapted from graphics hardware, and they produce text far faster than typical GPU-based services. That makes a difference for chat that feels instant, voice assistants that cannot afford to wait, and agents that make many model calls in a row. The company is not a model lab; it hosts models made by others.

Most people reach Groq through GroqCloud, a developer platform with an OpenAI-compatible API. It serves open-weight models such as Llama, Qwen, and OpenAI's gpt-oss, along with speech-to-text models, and it offers a free tier for testing and pay-as-you-go pricing for production. Switching an existing application is often just a matter of changing the base URL and API key. Enterprises can also buy dedicated capacity.

Speed comes with trade-offs. The catalog is limited to the models Groq chooses to host, and the newest or largest frontier models from closed labs are not available there. Capacity and rate limits can be tight during busy periods. The company has also gone through a major change after Nvidia licensed its inference technology and hired some of its leadership, though GroqCloud was said to continue operating independently. Cerebras, SambaNova, Together AI, and Fireworks are the closest alternatives.
