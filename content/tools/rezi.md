Rezi focuses on a specific worry: that automated screening software will reject a resume before a person reads it. The builder uses a simple, single-column layout and standard section names that parsing software reads reliably. As you write, a score shows how well the resume matches ATS best practices, with checks on length, formatting, action verbs, and keywords from a target job posting.

AI helps with content. It can generate bullet points from a role and tasks, rewrite weak phrases into stronger ones, and tailor a resume to a job description. It also drafts cover letters and has features for tracking applications and tuning your LinkedIn profile. The interface is plain, with less design flair than many competitors, which is intentional.

Rezi has a free tier and paid plans including an option for lifetime access, which differs from subscription-only competitors. The claim that an ATS score predicts success is imprecise, since systems vary and many companies' software is less strict than marketing suggests. A human reader still needs to be persuaded. Alternatives include Kickresume, Resume.io, Jobscan for matching, Teal, and Enhancv. Rezi suits job seekers who prioritize screening compatibility.
