Khroma starts by asking you to pick 50 colors you like. A neural network learns your tastes from those choices, then generates an endless stream of color combinations that match your style, shown as palettes, gradients, typography samples, and images. The aim is to make exploring color feel personal instead of random.

Designers use it to find inspiration for brand identities, websites, and illustrations. You can save favorite palettes, view them in different layouts, check color contrast, and search for combinations by a specific color. The interface is minimal and focused on discovery, and everything runs in the browser.

Khroma is free, and its scope is narrow. It doesn't export directly to design tools as extensively as Coolors, and its recommendations depend on the initial training set. Color decisions still require judgment about brand, context, and accessibility. Alternatives include Coolors, Adobe Color, Colormind, Paletton, and Huemint, which generates palettes tailored to design layouts. Khroma suits designers who want a personalized stream of color ideas.
