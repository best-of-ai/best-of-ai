Leonardo.ai began as a tool for game developers who needed lots of consistent assets. Users generate images from text prompts with a choice of Leonardo's own models and others, then refine them with inpainting, upscaling, and background removal. Fine-tuned models for characters, environments, items, and concept art made it popular with game artists, illustrators, and designers who care about a particular visual style.

Customization is a major draw. You can train your own models on a set of images to produce consistent characters or brand looks, use reference images to control composition and style, and draw on a canvas to guide generation. The platform has added video generation and animation of stills, along with an API for developers. A community feed lets users see what others are making and borrow prompts.

Leonardo has a free tier with a daily allowance of tokens and paid plans with more tokens, private generations, and commercial usage rights. After its acquisition by Canva, its models and technology are being linked with Canva's design tools. As with other generators, results vary and credit use can add up with video and high-resolution work. Midjourney, FLUX, Adobe Firefly, and Ideogram are alternatives. Leonardo is a good fit for creators who want control and consistency.
