Yelp built its business on reviews. Users write about their experiences with restaurants, home services, medical offices, and other local businesses, rate them, and upload photos. Search results combine ratings, distance, price, and filters such as open now or takeout, which helps people choose. In the United States, it is a major source for restaurant and service recommendations.

Businesses can claim their listing, add information, respond to reviews, and run ads, and some features support booking, waitlists, and requesting quotes from local professionals. AI has been added to the product: assistants help users find places through conversation, summaries condense long review histories, and tools detect and filter spam and fake reviews through a recommendation software that sorts reviews by reliability.

Yelp is free for consumers and earns money from advertising and business products. Reviews can be biased, and the filter that hides some reviews is a frequent complaint from businesses. Google Maps has become a stronger competitor for local search, Tripadvisor leads for travel, and Nextdoor and Angi cover neighborhood and home services. Yelp remains useful for finding local businesses when you want detailed opinions.
