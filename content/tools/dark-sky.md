Dark Sky changed expectations for weather apps. Using radar data and machine learning, it predicted when rain or snow would start and stop at your exact location, with messages such as light rain starting in 10 minutes. That focus on the next hour, rather than a general daily forecast, made it popular with people who walk, run, or commute, and its design was clean and quick to read.

It also offered an API that thousands of apps and websites used for weather data. Developers valued its simple interface and detailed forecasts, which made it a common building block in other products. Apple acquired the company, integrated its technology into Apple Weather, and then retired the Dark Sky app and its API.

People who loved Dark Sky have moved on to Apple's Weather app and WeatherKit, which carries the hyperlocal forecasts, and to alternatives such as Carrot Weather, AccuWeather's MinuteCast, Weather Underground, Windy, and Pirate Weather, an API designed as a drop-in replacement for the old Dark Sky API. This entry stays for reference because many older apps and articles mention Dark Sky.
