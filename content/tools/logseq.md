Logseq is built on the idea that notes should be a network of connected blocks. You write in an outliner where each bullet is a block, link to other pages with double brackets, and see backlinks that show everywhere an idea is referenced. Daily journals are the default entry point, so you capture thoughts as they come and organize them through links instead of folders. A graph view shows how pages relate.

Your data lives in files on your computer, in Markdown or Org-mode, so you are not locked into a service and can use any editor to open it. Queries pull together blocks that match conditions, such as all open tasks tagged with a project. Flashcards support spaced repetition, a PDF annotator links highlights to notes, and a large plugin and theme ecosystem adds features. Logseq Sync is an optional paid service for syncing across devices, and a database-based version has been in development.

Logseq is free and open source, supported by donations and the sync service. The interface and mobile apps are less polished than commercial rivals, and some people find the outliner format limiting. Obsidian is the closest competitor with a more document-oriented approach, Roam Research is the original outliner, Notion offers team features, and Capacities and Tana take object-based approaches. Logseq suits people who care about open formats and local ownership.
