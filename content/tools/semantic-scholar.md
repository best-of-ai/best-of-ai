Semantic Scholar is a research search engine built by the Allen Institute for AI, a nonprofit research organization. It covers hundreds of millions of academic papers across science, medicine, engineering, and the social sciences, and it is free. Unlike a plain keyword search, it uses language models to understand what papers are about and how they connect, which helps surface relevant work that does not use the exact words you typed.

Several features help you decide what to read. A short TLDR summary appears on many papers, and citation pages show how other papers cited a work and whether the citation supports, contrasts with, or only mentions it. The tool highlights influential citations, shows related papers, and lets you build a library with recommendations based on what you save. The Semantic Reader overlays definitions and citation cards onto PDFs, so you can understand a paper without jumping between tabs. Alerts notify you about new papers on a topic or from an author.

An open API gives developers access to the data, and researchers use it for large analyses of the literature. Semantic Scholar does not hold every paper's full text, and coverage is better in computer science and biomedicine than in some other fields. Google Scholar is broader in coverage but less structured, while Consensus, Elicit, and SciSpace add AI answers on top of search. For finding and triaging papers at no cost, it is a strong starting point.
