Flatiron Health works in cancer care. Its electronic health record, OncoEMR, is used by many community oncology practices, and the data from those practices, once de-identified and curated, form a large real-world dataset. Researchers use it to study how treatments work outside clinical trials.

The company uses machine learning and human abstraction to turn unstructured notes into research-ready data. Biopharma companies use these datasets to understand patient populations, design trials, and support regulatory submissions. Products for clinics include tools for clinical workflows, billing, and trial matching.

Roche acquired Flatiron, which continues to operate with data standards that protect patient privacy. The data is licensed to customers, and the company's software is sold to practices. Alternatives include Tempus, COTA, ConcertAI, Syapse, and Epic's oncology tools. Flatiron Health suits oncology practices and researchers using real-world evidence.
