Vimeo started as a community for filmmakers and grew into a professional video platform. Businesses use it to host and share video without ads, embed customizable players on their websites, control who can watch, and see analytics on viewing. Features such as review tools, live streaming, and webinars support marketing, training, and internal communications.

AI features have been added to speed up work. Tools can generate scripts, create video from text or slides, produce captions and translations, and edit footage by removing filler words or creating highlights, and an AI assistant helps with chapters and summaries. Vimeo's editing tools and recording app allow teams to produce video without separate software.

Bending Spoons acquired Vimeo, a company known for acquiring software products and reorganizing them, so pricing, features, and staffing may change. Vimeo has paid plans by features and storage and a limited free tier. Alternatives include YouTube, which is free but ad-supported, Wistia for marketing video, Loom for quick messages, Brightcove for enterprise, and Mux for developers. Vimeo suits businesses wanting a polished, private video platform.
