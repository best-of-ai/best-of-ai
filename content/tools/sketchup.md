SketchUp is known for being easy to learn. Its core idea is pushing and pulling flat shapes into 3D forms, which makes it quick to sketch a building, a room, or a piece of furniture. Architects use it for early design, interior designers for layouts, and construction and woodworking professionals for planning and shop drawings. The 3D Warehouse offers a huge library of free models of furniture, fixtures, and building components.

The ecosystem extends its capabilities. Extensions add rendering, analysis, and automation, LayOut turns models into documents with dimensions and scales, and cloud tools help share and review models. Newer AI features such as SketchUp Diffusion generate realistic images and style variations from a model and a text prompt, and AI helps search and prepare content. It runs on desktop and the web.

SketchUp has a free web version with limited features and paid subscriptions for desktop and professional tools, with education discounts. It's less suited than Revit for detailed building information modeling, and lacks the organic modeling power of tools like Blender. Alternatives include Revit, Rhino, Blender, Fusion, and Planner 5D for consumers. SketchUp suits designers who value speed and simplicity in 3D.
