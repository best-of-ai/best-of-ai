Murf is known for its studio, but its voice features are a separate reason people use it. The voice changer converts a recording of your own speech into a different voice from the library, keeping your timing and emotion, which suits people who want a polished sound without hiring talent. Voice cloning can create a custom voice for a brand or presenter, typically with consent and verification, and is offered mainly for business customers.

For day-to-day work, the library has voices in many accents and languages, with controls for pitch, speed, pauses, and emphasis. Users can sync narration with slides or video, add music, and share projects with teammates. An API provides text-to-speech for apps, and the platform includes dubbing and conversational agent products.

Murf has a free plan for testing without commercial rights, and paid plans for downloads, commercial use, and more voices. Cloning and changing voices raise consent and misuse concerns, so use only voices you have permission to copy. ElevenLabs is the strongest competitor for cloning, with WellSaid, Speechify, and Resemble AI also in the field. See the main Murf entry for its voiceover studio and broader platform.
