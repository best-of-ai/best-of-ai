MyFitnessPal is the best-known food diary app. You log what you eat by searching a database of packaged foods, restaurant meals, and homemade recipes, and the app totals calories and macronutrients against daily goals. It syncs with fitness trackers and apps, so exercise adjusts your targets, and it records weight and measurements over time. Millions of people use it for weight loss, muscle gain, or general awareness.

Its size is its strength. The food database is huge, though many entries are user-submitted and can contain errors, so it pays to choose verified items. Features include barcode scanning, recipe import, meal scanning with AI to log from a photo, and voice logging. Premium adds more detailed nutrient tracking, custom goals, macro presets, and meal planning, while a free version covers basic logging.

Some features that were once free, like barcode scanning, moved behind the paid plan, which frustrated many users. Calorie counting works for some people and can be harmful for those with a history of disordered eating, so consult a professional if that applies. Cronometer offers more accurate nutrient data, Lose It is a simpler alternative, MacroFactor adapts targets using your weight trend, and Noom focuses on behavior change. MyFitnessPal is best for convenience and database size.
