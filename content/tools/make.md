Make visualises automations as flowcharts rather than simple trigger-and-action pairs, which makes it the right tool for complex workflows that need branching logic, loops, error handling, and data transformation at each step. A Zapier Zap is a straight line; a Make scenario can branch based on conditions, retry failed steps, run parallel paths, and iterate over arrays of data. This extra complexity is what makes it worth using for anything more sophisticated than simple app-to-app handoffs.

The scenario editor is visual in a way that makes debugging and iteration significantly easier than Zapier's linear format. You can see the data flowing through each node, pause and test individual steps, and understand exactly what's happening at each stage of a complex automation. Native modules for OpenAI and Google Gemini let you embed AI steps — classification, summarisation, generation — directly into your workflow without any custom code.

Make's free plan allows 1,000 operations per month (each step in a scenario counts as an operation), which is enough for light experimentation. Core at $9/month provides 10,000 operations and removes the concurrent scenario limit; Pro at $16/month gives 10,000 operations with more features; Teams and Enterprise plans scale to millions of operations for production use. Make is particularly popular with developers and technical operations people who want the expressiveness of code-based automation but prefer to work visually. It has a passionate power-user community that shares templates and techniques for common use cases.
