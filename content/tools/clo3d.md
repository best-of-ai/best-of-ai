CLO3D, also known as CLO, replaces some physical sampling with digital prototypes. A designer imports or draws 2D patterns, stitches them together virtually, and sees the garment fall on an avatar with realistic fabric behavior. Changes to pattern shapes, colors, or materials show right away, and fit can be checked across sizes.

The software supports the work of a fashion team. It simulates fabric drape and movement, applies prints and trims, and produces renderings and animations for presentations. Files can be shared with colleagues and factories, and a related platform supports collaboration and asset libraries. Some automation assists with pattern adjustments.

CLO3D is paid, sold by subscription, and has a significant learning curve. Digital results still need physical validation for some products. Alternatives include Browzwear, Marvelous Designer, Style3D, Optitex, and Lectra. CLO3D suits fashion designers and apparel companies adopting 3D design.
