Supermaven was a coding assistant that focused on one thing: fast, accurate autocomplete. Its main claim was an unusually large context window, which let it consider much more of your project than most completion tools when suggesting the next lines. Developers liked how quickly suggestions appeared, and extensions for VS Code, JetBrains IDEs, and Neovim made it easy to try. A free tier and a low-priced paid plan helped it spread.

Anysphere, the company behind the Cursor editor, acquired Supermaven, and the team joined Cursor to work on its next-generation Tab completion model. About a year later, Supermaven announced it was sunsetting the product, refunding unused balances, and recommending that users move to Cursor. Existing customers were promised free autocomplete inference for the foreseeable future, so the extensions may still function, but the product is no longer developed or sold.

For anyone who used Supermaven, the natural next step is Cursor, which now carries its technology in the Tab feature. Other autocomplete options include GitHub Copilot, Windsurf, and Tabnine. The entry stays for reference, since older reviews and comparison pages still mention it. The lesson for tool buyers is that small AI tools are often acquired and folded into larger ones, so it is worth checking whether a product is still actively maintained before committing a team to it.
