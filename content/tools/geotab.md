Geotab makes the small devices that plug into vehicles and report what they do. A device in the diagnostic port records location, speed, harsh braking, idling, engine fault codes, and more, and sends the data to the cloud, where fleet managers view it on maps and dashboards. Companies with delivery vans, service trucks, buses, and long-haul trucks use it to track vehicles, optimize routes, and reduce costs.

Analytics turn the data into insights. Driver scoring identifies risky behavior and supports coaching, maintenance alerts flag vehicles that need service based on engine data, and fuel and emissions reports support efficiency and electric vehicle planning. AI features include an assistant that answers questions about fleet data in natural language, and models that predict problems and rank safety risks. A marketplace of third-party apps extends the platform, and compliance tools support electronic logging rules.

Geotab is sold through resellers and partners as a subscription per vehicle, with pricing varying by plan. It targets business fleets, from small to very large. Competitors include Samsara, Motive, Verizon Connect, Fleet Complete, and Fleetio for maintenance. Collecting driver data raises privacy issues that employers must handle transparently. Geotab suits fleets that want broad data and a flexible ecosystem.
