Notion lets people build their own workspace from simple pieces. A page can hold text, headings, checklists, images, embedded files, and databases, and databases can be shown as tables, boards, calendars, or galleries. Teams use it for wikis, meeting notes, project tracking, product roadmaps, and personal organization, and templates help people get started. Real-time collaboration, comments, and permissions make it work for teams as well as individuals.

The product has grown beyond notes. Notion Calendar and Notion Mail connect scheduling and email to the workspace, and Sites turns pages into public web pages. Notion AI adds writing help, summaries, translation, and question answering across your workspace and connected tools like Slack and Google Drive, plus agents that perform tasks. AI meeting notes capture and summarize calls. See the Notion AI entry for details on those features.

Notion has a free plan for individuals and small use, with paid plans for teams that add unlimited blocks, permissions, and admin controls, and AI included in higher tiers. Flexibility has a cost: new users can find the blank canvas overwhelming, and very large workspaces can feel slow. Confluence is the enterprise wiki alternative, Coda is similar, Obsidian and Logseq store notes locally, and Airtable focuses on databases. Notion is widely popular for its balance of simplicity and power.
