Notion is a workspace for notes, documents, wikis, and databases, and Notion AI works inside it instead of in a separate chat window. Highlight a paragraph and ask it to tighten the writing, summarize a long page, translate a section, or turn rough notes into a clean doc. It can also fill in database properties, such as tagging each row with a category or pulling action items out of meeting notes, which saves a lot of manual cleanup in busy workspaces.

The larger piece is the Notion Agent, which can read and edit across your pages and databases, answer questions using your own content, and carry out multi-step jobs like setting up a project hub or reorganizing a messy wiki. AI Meeting Notes records and transcribes a meeting and writes up the summary and tasks in a page. Enterprise Search looks through Notion and connected apps such as Slack and Google Drive. Custom Agents can run on a schedule or a trigger, like compiling a weekly status report, using credits that are billed separately.

Notion AI is included in the Business and Enterprise plans, with limited trial use on the others, so it tends to be a reason to pay for a higher tier. Because it uses your workspace content as context, permissions matter: it should only see what the person using it can already see. Enterprise customers get stronger data-retention terms than other plans. It makes the most sense for teams that already keep their docs and projects in Notion. If your knowledge lives in other tools, a general assistant like ChatGPT or Claude with connectors may fit better.
