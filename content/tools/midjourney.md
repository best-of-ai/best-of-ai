Midjourney runs inside Discord, which sounds odd at first — until you experience the community feed where thousands of other users' generations scroll by in real time. It's an endless source of prompting ideas and instant quality benchmarks. You type `/imagine` followed by your description, wait about a minute, and get four image variations. From there, you upscale, vary, or subtly adjust without starting from scratch.

The aesthetic Midjourney produces is genuinely its own. Images tend toward cinematic, richly textured, and deliberately composed — qualities that rival professional concept art and illustration. Learning to prompt it well is a real skill: layering in film stocks, lighting references, aspect ratios, and artist styles shifts the output dramatically. Version 6 added more literal prompt interpretation for when you need the AI to match a specific idea rather than freely interpret it, which made it substantially more useful for commercial creative work.

Plans start at $10/month for 200 fast generations and go up to $60/month for heavy use. There is no free tier. That pricing puts Midjourney squarely in the professional creative category — concept artists, brand designers, art directors, and campaign marketers who want the best image quality AI can produce and are prepared to invest time in learning the craft of prompting.
