Connected Papers helps you get oriented in a research area. You enter a paper title, DOI, or link, and it produces a graph where each node is a paper and closer nodes are more similar. Similarity comes from co-citation and bibliographic coupling, meaning papers that cite similar sources or are cited together, rather than from keywords. Node size and color show citation count and year, which lets you see influential and recent work at a glance.

Two companion views add context. Prior works lists the papers that many in the graph build upon, which tend to be the foundations of the field, and derivative works lists later papers that build on the group. Together these help you find reviews, landmark papers, and recent developments. You can save graphs, export references, and use it as a way to check that a literature search has not missed a cluster of related work.

The free plan allows a small number of graphs per month, and paid plans increase that and add features. The tool depends on the citation data behind it, so very new papers and some fields have thinner coverage. It does not summarize or explain papers. Litmaps, ResearchRabbit, and Inciteful offer similar visual exploration, Semantic Scholar and Google Scholar provide search, and Elicit and Consensus add AI answers.
