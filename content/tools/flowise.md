Flowise lets people assemble AI applications visually. In its canvas, you connect nodes representing models, prompts, memory, document loaders, vector stores, and tools into a flow, test it in a chat window, and expose it through an API or an embedded widget. It started as a visual layer over LangChain and LlamaIndex and has grown to include agent flows with branching, loops, and human approval steps.

Typical uses include chatbots that answer from company documents, customer support assistants, and agents that call APIs and tools. It supports many model providers, including local models through Ollama, and many vector databases, which gives teams a choice about where data and compute live. Because it is open source, it can be self-hosted, and the project has a strong community and many templates.

Flowise is free to self-host, with a hosted cloud service for people who prefer not to manage servers. Workday acquired Flowise to give its customers a way to build agents on its platform, and said it would continue to support the open-source project. As with other visual builders, complex logic can become hard to read, and production use needs attention to security, access control, and monitoring. Dify, Langflow, n8n, and Coze are close alternatives, and LangGraph or CrewAI suit developers who prefer to write code.
