Aleph Alpha was Europe's attempt to build a homegrown large language model company. Its Luminous models offered multilingual text generation and could handle images alongside text, with an emphasis on explainability, so users could see which parts of a source supported an answer. The company pitched itself to public sector agencies, banks, and industrial companies in Europe that wanted AI under their own control and in line with European regulation.

Its focus later shifted from competing on model size to offering a platform. PhariaAI combines tools for building and deploying AI applications, managing models, and running them on private infrastructure or in cloud environments such as Schwarz Group's STACKIT. The Luminous models are now treated as legacy, and the company works with models from various sources while stressing governance and transparency.

Cohere agreed to acquire Aleph Alpha, with backing from its long-time investor Schwarz Group, a move that combines two enterprise-focused AI companies and positions them as a sovereign-AI provider across Europe and North America. As the deal progresses, product plans may change. Competitors and alternatives include Mistral AI, Cohere, SAP's AI offerings, and the major cloud providers' sovereign cloud services. Aleph Alpha is relevant to organizations prioritizing European data control.
