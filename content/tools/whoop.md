WHOOP is a screenless wearable worn on the wrist or in apparel. It records heart rate, heart rate variability, skin temperature, blood oxygen, and movement around the clock, and its app turns that into daily scores for recovery, strain, and sleep. The idea is to show how prepared your body is to train hard or rest, and to give athletes and health-conscious people a sense of how habits like alcohol or late nights affect them.

Beyond scores, WHOOP adds features for health tracking. These include sleep coaching, a healthspan view of long-term trends, women's health insights, and optional tools like blood pressure insights and ECG. A conversational AI coach lets members ask questions about their data and get personalized advice. A community and team features add accountability.

WHOOP is sold as a membership, with the hardware included, so you pay monthly or annually. The cost can exceed the price of a one-time purchase tracker, and data should be seen as wellness guidance, not medical diagnosis. Some features have drawn regulatory attention, so watch for changes. Competitors include Oura, Garmin, Apple Watch, Fitbit, and Polar. WHOOP suits athletes and people who want recovery guidance without a screen.
