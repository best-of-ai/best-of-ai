MonkeyLearn let people analyze text without machine learning expertise. Teams could connect support tickets, survey responses, reviews, and social posts, pick a ready-made model for sentiment or topics, or train a custom classifier by tagging examples. Results appeared in dashboards and could be sent to spreadsheets, helpdesks, and BI tools, which made it popular with customer experience, product, and marketing teams.

Its approach was to make natural language processing approachable. A visual interface handled model creation and testing, and the API let developers add analysis to their own apps. Typical uses included tagging support requests by issue, tracking sentiment in reviews, and pulling themes from open-ended survey answers.

The product was discontinued after Medallia acquired the company, and it is no longer available. Anyone who relied on it needed to move their workflows. Today, similar analysis can be done with large language models in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, which handle sentiment and topic extraction with a prompt. Dedicated tools include Thematic, Medallia, Qualtrics Text iQ, and Chattermill, and cloud services such as Google Natural Language and AWS Comprehend offer APIs. This entry stays for reference, since older guides still mention MonkeyLearn.
