Humata lets people converse with documents. After you upload a file, it reads the content and answers questions about it: asking for a summary, a specific figure, a comparison between sections, or an explanation of a clause. Each answer includes a citation that links to the passage in the source, which makes it easy to verify. It works with long files that would take a long time to scan manually, such as contracts, research papers, and financial reports.

Beyond a single document, you can work with sets of files and ask questions that span them. Folders organize collections, and features support extracting information into a table or drafting new text based on the content. An API lets developers build document question answering into their own tools. Teams use it for research, legal review, finance, and operations where large volumes of documents are common.

Humata has a free tier with limits on pages and questions and paid plans for heavier use and teams. Accuracy depends on the clarity of the document: scans, tables, and unusual layouts can cause errors, and the AI may miss details. Uploading sensitive files requires trust in the vendor's security terms. ChatPDF, Adobe Acrobat's AI Assistant, NotebookLM, and the file features in ChatGPT and Claude overlap with Humata. It suits users who want a dedicated, citation-first document tool.
