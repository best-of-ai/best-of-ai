Chegg began by renting textbooks and grew into a subscription service for homework help. Students could search a library of solved textbook problems, ask experts questions, and get tutoring. The model worked well until general-purpose chatbots arrived, at which point many students began asking ChatGPT instead. Chegg's traffic and subscriber numbers fell sharply, and the company has cut staff more than once and rethought its strategy.

CheggMate is the company's response, an AI study assistant that combines large language models with Chegg's own content. It aims to explain problems step by step, help with concepts, and generate practice questions, drawing on the study material the company has built over the years. The goal is to be more reliable for coursework than a generic chatbot because it grounds answers in verified content.

Chegg is sold by subscription and has been moving toward skills-based learning, including areas like language and professional development, in addition to study help. Using any homework-help service for graded work may violate school rules, and a student should check their institution's policy. Free alternatives such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Khan Academy's Khanmigo cover much of the same ground. Quizlet and Course Hero are other study platforms, and the broader category continues to change quickly.
