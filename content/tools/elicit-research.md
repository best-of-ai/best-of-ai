Elicit helps researchers move through academic literature faster. A user asks a research question, and Elicit searches a large database of papers, presents relevant results, and extracts details such as sample size, methods, and outcomes into a table. Seeing structured comparisons across studies is much quicker than reading each paper in full.

Additional tools support systematic review work. Users can screen papers against criteria, summarize findings, and build reports, with citations tied to the passages in each paper so claims can be checked. It is used by students, academics, and professionals in evidence-based fields.

Elicit has a free plan with limits and paid tiers for more extraction and papers. AI summaries can misread studies, so important claims should be verified against the source. Alternatives include Consensus, Scite, SciSpace, Undermind, and Semantic Scholar. Elicit suits researchers conducting literature reviews.
