Pika grew popular by making AI video fun and easy for people with no editing experience. You type a prompt or upload an image, and it generates a short clip in a few moments. Its signature features are effects that change objects in playful ways, such as melting, squishing, inflating, or turning something into cake, which spread quickly across social media. The interface is simple and mobile-friendly, which suited creators who make clips for TikTok and Instagram.

The toolset has widened beyond effects. Keyframe features let you set the start and end of a shot, swap tools replace a person or object in existing footage, and facial animation tools make a still image talk, sing, or react. Editing tools let you extend clips, modify parts of a scene, and refine motion, and newer models improve realism and physics. It is aimed more at social and creative content than at long cinematic work.

Pika has a free tier with a small credit allowance and paid plans with more credits, higher resolution, and commercial use. Clips are short, so storytelling means stitching several together, and results vary between attempts. Runway offers a more professional toolset, Luma and Kling focus on realism, and Google's Veo is a major newer competitor. Pika is a good fit for quick, shareable effects and experiments, and less so for polished production.
