Doxy.me gives clinicians a simple way to see patients by video. A provider gets a personal link; patients click it, enter a virtual waiting room, and join the visit in their browser with no account or app installation. That simplicity matters for patients who are not comfortable with technology, and it suits therapists, physicians, dietitians, speech and physical therapists, and other practitioners.

Beyond video, the paid tiers add features that clinics need. These include a customizable waiting room, patient check-in, screen sharing, group sessions, text message reminders, payments, and integrations with electronic health record systems. Administrators can manage a team of providers under one account, and the platform includes business associate agreements that healthcare organizations need under HIPAA. Newer AI tools offer note-taking from visits.

A free plan covers a single provider with basic video visits, and paid plans add team features and branding. Telehealth rules vary by state and country, including licensing, consent, and prescribing, so the platform alone does not make a practice compliant. Connection quality depends on the patient's internet and device. Competitors include Zoom for Healthcare, Doximity, SimplePractice, and Amwell for larger systems. Doxy.me remains popular with small practices that want something simple and affordable.
