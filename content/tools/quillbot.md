QuillBot began as a paraphrasing tool and still draws most of its users for that. You paste in a passage, choose a mode such as standard, fluent, formal, or shorter, and it rewrites the text while keeping the meaning. A slider controls how much the wording changes. Students, non-native English speakers, and professional writers use it to rephrase awkward sentences, shorten drafts, and vary their style.

Around the paraphraser is a set of writing utilities. The grammar checker catches spelling and punctuation mistakes, the summarizer condenses long articles into key points or paragraphs, and the citation generator formats references in styles such as APA and MLA. A translator, an AI chat assistant, and detectors that estimate whether text is AI-written or plagiarized are included. Browser extensions and a Word add-in bring these tools into the places where people write.

The free version has limits on text length and modes, and the premium plan removes them and adds more modes, faster processing, and extra checks. Rewriting tools can produce text that sounds stilted or changes meaning, so output should be read carefully. Schools often have rules against using paraphrasers on assignments, and AI detectors give unreliable results, so students should check their institution's policy. Grammarly is the closest competitor, and Wordtune offers similar rewriting.
