Typeform changed the feel of online forms. Instead of showing a long page of fields, it presents one question at a time with large type and smooth transitions, which tends to make forms feel friendlier and often improves completion rates. People use it for surveys, quizzes, lead capture, registrations, feedback, and onboarding. Design options and templates make it easy to match a brand.

Behind the interface are capable features. Logic jumps change the next question based on earlier answers, calculators and scoring support quizzes, payments can be collected, and files can be uploaded. Integrations send responses to tools like Google Sheets, HubSpot, Slack, and Zapier. AI features help generate a form from a short prompt, suggest questions, and summarize responses so teams can understand results faster.

Typeform has a limited free plan and paid plans with more responses and features. Prices are higher than competitors, and the one-question-at-a-time format can be slower for long forms where users prefer to see everything at once. Google Forms is free and simple, Tally and Fillout offer generous free options, Jotform has extensive features, and SurveyMonkey is stronger for research surveys. Typeform suits teams that care about the respondent's experience.
