Dots move ChatGPT from a tool you open to an assistant that keeps working. Each dot has its own cloud computer with a browser, so it can carry out tasks on websites and in apps without tying up your device. You give it goals, such as keeping track of your inbox, preparing for meetings, or monitoring a project, and it works in the background, checking back when it needs input. Over time it learns from your feedback.

Dots connect to a large catalog of apps, which lets them read and act across the tools you use. They can also be reached through Slack and Microsoft Teams, so you can talk to them where you already work. OpenAI has previewed specialist dots that carry their own identity and credentials and can work with a company's systems of record. Because these agents can act, they are designed to ask for approval before sensitive steps.

Access is tiered. At launch, dots are available to higher-priced ChatGPT plans in eligible regions, and a user can create one at first, with more planned. Setup happens in the desktop app or on the web. As with other autonomous agents, mistakes are possible, and consequential work should be reviewed. Meta's Muse, Claude Duet, and ChatGPT Work are comparable products. Check OpenAI's current plan pages for availability and limits.
