Runway is one of the original AI video companies, and it built its tools with filmmakers and artists in mind. The core of the product is its Gen video models, which create short clips from a text prompt, a still image, or both. Reference images help keep a character or setting consistent from one shot to the next, which is the hard part of making anything longer than a single clip. The company started as a set of browser-based editing tools, and some of that remains, such as background removal, motion tracking, and inpainting.

Two newer tools show where it is aimed. Aleph edits existing video using plain instructions, so you can change the weather, remove an object, restyle a shot, or generate a new camera angle from footage you already have. Act-Two takes a video of an actor's performance, including face and hand movement, and applies it to a generated character. Workflows chain several steps together so a repeatable production process can run without clicking through each stage. Runway also researches world models, which simulate environments for games and robotics.

Runway runs in the browser and has a free tier with a small number of credits, plus paid plans that include more credits, higher resolution, and commercial use. Video generation burns credits fast, so budget matters on longer projects. Quality has improved a lot, but fine details such as hands, text, and long continuous action can still go wrong, and most finished pieces are assembled from several generations and a conventional editor. Alternatives include Luma, Google's Veo, Kling, and Pika.
