Dubverse helps creators reach audiences who speak other languages. You upload a video or paste a link, and the platform transcribes the speech, translates the script, and generates a dubbed voice track in the target language, timed to match the original. It supports a range of languages, including many Indian ones, and lets you edit the transcript and translation before the voice is produced, which matters for accuracy.

Features support a full localization workflow. You can choose among voices, adjust speed, and edit individual lines, and the platform can add subtitles. An API supports automation for companies that process a lot of content, and a text-to-speech studio produces voiceovers for videos without translation. Typical customers are YouTubers, e-learning teams, news and entertainment publishers, and brands that run content in several markets.

Dubverse has a free tier with limited minutes and paid plans for more dubbing time, premium voices, and team features. As with any machine dubbing, quality depends on the language pair and the clarity of the source audio, and cultural nuance, humor, and names often need human review. ElevenLabs Dubbing, HeyGen, Rask, and Papercup are competitors, and YouTube offers automatic multi-language audio for some channels. Dubverse is most useful for volume and speed at moderate cost.
