MindMeister helps you think in branches. You start with a central topic and add connected ideas around it, using keyboard shortcuts to build a map quickly. Maps can be formatted with colors, icons, images, links, and notes, rearranged freely, and turned into outlines. People use it for brainstorming, studying, project planning, meeting notes, and mapping out content such as articles or courses.

Collaboration is a core strength. Several people can edit a map at once, with comments and voting to gather input, and maps can be shared by link. A presentation mode turns a map into slides, and tasks can be assigned on map nodes and synced with the project tools in the same suite. Integrations connect with Google Workspace, Microsoft Teams, and other apps, and exports include PDF, image, and Word formats. AI features suggest ideas and generate map structures from a topic.

MindMeister has a free plan limited to a few maps and paid plans for unlimited maps, more sharing, and extras. It is part of a company that also makes other productivity tools. Competitors include Miro, XMind, MindManager, Whimsical, and Coggle, and Obsidian and Logseq have graph views for note-based mapping. It suits teams that want simple, browser-based mind mapping with good collaboration.
