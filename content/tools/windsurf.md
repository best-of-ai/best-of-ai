Windsurf is built around Cascade, an AI agent that runs in the background while you work rather than responding to individual prompts. Cascade maintains a map of what it has done, what changes it made and why, and what still needs completing — which means it can pick up a task that spans multiple files and multiple steps without losing track mid-way. You can describe an entire feature, step away, and come back to a complete implementation across the relevant parts of your codebase.

The editor is a fork of VS Code, so migrating from VS Code or Cursor is straightforward — your extensions and settings carry over. Where Windsurf differentiates itself is in the depth of autonomous operation. Rather than generating code and presenting it for your review, Cascade runs tests, reads error messages, fixes them, and iterates until the task is complete. It also has terminal access, so it can install packages, run scripts, and interact with your development environment as part of completing a task.

Windsurf's free tier includes 25 Cascade Flow actions and 5 premium model uses per day — enough for light use. Pro at $15/month unlocks significantly more capacity. The privacy settings let you choose which data leaves your machine, and an enterprise version supports team management and policy controls. Developers who want a less interactive, more autonomous AI coding experience — where you describe the goal and the AI handles implementation — tend to prefer Windsurf to Cursor's more conversational model.
