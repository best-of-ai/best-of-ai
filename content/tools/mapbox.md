Mapbox is the mapping toolkit behind many apps. Developers use its SDKs to embed interactive maps in websites and in iOS and Android apps, with control over styles, layers, and data. Mapbox Studio lets designers customize map appearance, while vector tiles keep maps fast and sharp. Companies in logistics, travel, real estate, and mobility use it to show routes, locations, and live data.

The platform goes beyond display. APIs handle geocoding and search, turn-by-turn navigation, route optimization, isochrones that show how far you can get in a given time, and traffic-aware directions. A navigation SDK supports in-app guidance, and tools for working with large geospatial datasets allow analysis and visualization. Machine learning is used for features such as map matching and road data updates, and recent products add AI assistants for developers.

Mapbox has a generous free tier and pay-as-you-go pricing based on map loads and API requests, with enterprise plans. Costs can grow quickly for high-traffic apps, and the licensing of data and terms of service matter. Google Maps Platform is the main competitor and has broader place data, HERE and TomTom serve automotive and enterprise needs, and MapLibre is an open-source fork of earlier Mapbox code that lets developers avoid vendor lock-in. Mapbox is popular for custom-designed maps.
