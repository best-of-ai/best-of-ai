CVAT was created at Intel and released as open source, and is now maintained by a company called CVAT.ai along with a community. It is a web-based tool for labeling data for computer vision: drawing bounding boxes, polygons, polylines, points, and masks, and tagging images or video frames with classes. It handles tasks such as object detection, segmentation, tracking across video, and classification, and exports to common formats such as COCO, YOLO, and Pascal VOC.

Automation speeds up the work. Interpolation fills in boxes between keyframes in video, and models can pre-label data, including integrations with Segment Anything and other detectors, so annotators only correct errors. Projects have roles, review workflows, and quality checks for teams, and the tool can be self-hosted with Docker, which keeps data private. A cloud version at app.cvat.ai provides hosted access for those who do not want to run it.

The self-hosted community edition is free. The company also sells hosted plans, enterprise features, and labeling services. Setup and maintenance of a self-hosted server require some technical skill. Label Studio is the other popular open-source labeler, Roboflow and Labelbox offer commercial platforms, and Supervisely and V7 compete on features. CVAT is a strong choice for teams that want control over their data and a free, proven tool.
