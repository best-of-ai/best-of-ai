Amplenote treats notes and tasks as part of the same system. You write notes with a Markdown-like editor, link them together, and embed tasks that appear both in the note and in a central task list and calendar. Daily jots capture quick thoughts, and the calendar shows tasks and events alongside notes, so planning the day and recording information happen in one place.

Its task score is an unusual feature: each task gets a number based on its importance, urgency, and effort, and the app highlights the highest-scoring tasks so you know where to begin. A plugin system lets users extend the app with scripts, including features that connect to outside tools. The built-in AI assistant can summarize notes, brainstorm, rewrite text, and use the contents of your notes as context, and it works on web, mobile, and desktop.

Amplenote has a free plan with limits and paid plans for full features, including more AI use and syncing integrations. It is a smaller product than Notion, Obsidian, or Evernote, and its community and ecosystem are modest. Notion offers broader team functionality, Obsidian emphasizes local files, Todoist is stronger as a pure task manager, and Sunsama focuses on daily planning. Amplenote suits individuals who want notes, tasks, and calendar in one app.
