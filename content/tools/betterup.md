BetterUp sells coaching to organizations as a benefit for employees. Each person is matched with a coach based on goals such as becoming a better manager, handling stress, improving communication, or preparing for a promotion. Sessions take place by video, with coaches who are credentialed and trained in a consistent method. The company also includes assessments, programs for managers, and group sessions, and it publishes research on its results.

Technology plays a growing role. AI tools supplement human coaches by offering reflection prompts, summarizing sessions, suggesting next steps, and giving employees an always-available assistant for quick questions. Dashboards give employers aggregate data on participation and outcomes, without exposing individual conversations. Programs can be scaled for small teams or an entire company.

BetterUp is sold to businesses on custom contracts and is not generally available to individual consumers, though some people get it through an employer. It is expensive per employee compared with training courses, so organizations usually use it for managers and high-potential staff. Evidence of results is mixed across the coaching industry, and the human relationship matters a great deal. Competitors include CoachHub, Ezra, and Torch, and some companies build internal coaching programs with external consultants.
