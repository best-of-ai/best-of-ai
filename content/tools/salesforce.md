Salesforce is the CRM that most enterprise revenue teams build their operations around, not just because of its features but because of the ecosystem surrounding it. The AppExchange marketplace has 4,000+ apps and integrations built specifically for Salesforce, meaning almost any workflow requirement — a specific industry's compliance needs, an unusual data model, a niche integration — has likely been solved by a third-party partner. The network effect of this ecosystem is what keeps Salesforce entrenched even when competitors offer comparable core functionality at lower cost.

Einstein AI is embedded throughout the platform. For sales reps, it scores leads by likelihood to close, forecasts revenue from deal data with greater accuracy than manual estimates, and suggests the next best action for each open opportunity. For service teams, Einstein routes cases to the right agent, suggests resolution steps from historical ticket data, and generates draft replies. For marketing, it predicts which email subject lines and send times optimise open rates for each segment. These aren't add-on features — they're woven into standard views and workflows.

Salesforce Sales Cloud starts at $25/user/month for basic CRM; Enterprise at $165/user/month adds forecasting, advanced automation, and Einstein AI. Marketing Cloud, Service Cloud, and Commerce Cloud are separate products that add additional revenue through the same customer relationship. The total cost of ownership at enterprise scale — licensing plus administration plus customisation — is high, which is why Salesforce implementations typically include specialist administrators and often external consultants. The investment is justified by the scale and complexity it enables for organisations managing thousands of customer relationships across multiple teams.
