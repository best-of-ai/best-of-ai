Salesforce is the CRM that large companies tend to run their sales and customer operations on. It tracks leads, accounts, opportunities, and support cases, and it is sold as separate clouds for Sales, Service, Marketing, and Commerce, with Slack, Tableau, and MuleSoft added for collaboration, analytics, and integration. Underneath is a platform that lets companies build custom objects, workflows, and apps, which is why Salesforce setups can look very different from one company to the next.

The AI strategy centers on Agentforce, a platform for building agents that answer customer questions, qualify leads, and take actions in Salesforce and other systems. Data Cloud, now branded Data 360, brings customer data from many sources into one place so agents and predictions have context. Einstein features cover lead scoring, forecasting, email drafting, and call summaries. Companies can build agents with low-code tools and set the rules and guardrails that govern what the agents are allowed to do.

Salesforce is priced per user per month by edition, and many of the AI features cost extra or use consumption credits. The total cost often includes consultants or in-house administrators, because complex setups take real work to build and maintain. That is the usual trade-off: it can model almost any sales process, but it is heavier and more expensive than lighter CRMs like HubSpot, Pipedrive, or Zoho. It makes the most sense for mid-sized and large organizations with complicated sales processes, many teams, and dedicated admin staff.
