Lexalytics builds natural language processing for businesses that need to make sense of large amounts of text. Its Salience engine reads reviews, survey comments, support tickets, social posts, and call transcripts and returns structured information: whether the sentiment is positive or negative, which products or people are mentioned, what themes recur, and what the author intends. Analysts can then count, trend, and compare these signals instead of reading comments one by one.

The technology can be deployed in different ways, including as a cloud API and as software installed inside a customer's own environment, which matters for industries with strict data rules. It supports multiple languages and can be tuned for specific industries by adding custom dictionaries and rules. Lexalytics is owned by InMoment, a customer experience company, and its capabilities feed that company's analytics products.

Lexalytics is sold to enterprises and software vendors on license, with custom pricing. Rule-based and statistical text analytics are now often replaced or supplemented by large language models, which can classify and summarize text with less setup but cost more per document. Competitors include Medallia, Qualtrics Text iQ, Thematic, Chattermill, and cloud services like Google Natural Language and AWS Comprehend. Lexalytics suits organizations that need high-volume, controllable text analysis.
