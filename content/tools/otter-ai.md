Otter was one of the first widely used meeting transcription tools. You connect your calendar, and its assistant joins your Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams calls to record, transcribe, and label speakers. The transcript is available live, so participants can follow along, and afterward you get a summary, key points, and action items. Everything is searchable, so you can find what was said in a past meeting by keyword. It also works on phones for in-person conversations and lectures.

Further features have been built around the transcripts. You can ask Otter questions about a meeting or across many meetings, generate follow-up emails, and share notes with your team. Integrations send summaries to tools like Slack, Salesforce, and HubSpot, and sales teams use agents that take notes and update the CRM. Some plans let attendees add comments or highlights, and the meeting assistant can attend on your behalf.

Otter has a free plan with limited monthly minutes and shorter meeting length, and paid plans add more transcription time and features, with business plans offering admin controls. As with any recording tool, you should tell participants and follow your region's consent rules, and accuracy drops with heavy accents, crosstalk, and poor audio. Fireflies, Fathom, and Read AI are the closest competitors, and built-in notes from Zoom, Teams, and Google Meet have reduced the need for a separate tool.
