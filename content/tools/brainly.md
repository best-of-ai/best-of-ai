Brainly grew from a Q&A site for students. A learner posts a question, other users and moderators answer it, and the best responses are verified. Over time, it has built a large library of questions and answers across math, science, languages, and history, with versions for many countries that follow local curricula.

The product now blends community with AI. An AI tutor gives step-by-step explanations, can solve problems from a photo, and helps with writing and study guides. A paid plan removes ads and unlocks more AI help and expert-verified answers. Some features are free with limits.

As with all homework helpers, the risk is that students copy answers without learning. Teachers often prefer tools that explain the steps, and Brainly's step-by-step mode is intended for that. Answers can contain mistakes, especially from community members, so check important results. Alternatives include Khan Academy's Khanmigo, Photomath, Quizlet, Chegg, and Course Hero. Brainly suits students who want help understanding school problems.
