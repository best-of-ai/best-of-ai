Miro provides an infinite shared canvas where teams place sticky notes, diagrams, documents, and images, and work together live or asynchronously. It's used for brainstorming, retrospectives, user journey maps, roadmaps, and workshops, particularly for distributed teams. A large library of templates, integrations with tools such as Jira, Slack, and Google Drive, and facilitation features like timers and voting make it versatile.

AI features speed up creation and synthesis. Users can generate diagrams, flowcharts, and mind maps from a prompt, summarize and cluster hundreds of sticky notes into themes, and turn a board into a document or a prototype. Sidekicks are AI helpers with specific roles, such as a product manager or a retrospective facilitator, that give feedback and suggestions on the board. Flows connect several AI steps for repeatable processes.

Miro has a free plan with limited boards and paid plans for teams and enterprise. Boards can become cluttered, and AI summaries need review for nuance. Alternatives include FigJam from Figma, Lucidspark, Mural, Microsoft Whiteboard, and Excalidraw for simple sketching. Miro suits teams that run frequent collaborative sessions.
