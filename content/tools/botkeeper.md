Botkeeper offered accounting firms a way to scale bookkeeping with automation. Its software connected to clients' accounting systems, categorized transactions with machine learning, reconciled accounts, and prepared financial statements, with human reviewers checking the work. The aim was to let firms serve more clients with the same staff and to reduce routine data entry.

The company raised substantial funding, but it shut down after losing a large share of its revenue when major customers were acquired and changed their arrangements. It then sold its Infinite AI platform to Xendoo, a bookkeeping firm, which said it would keep the platform active and supported as its own division. The long-term roadmap is less clear, and customers had to decide whether to move.

Accounting firms looking for similar tools have several options, including Dext, Hubdoc, Bill, Ramp's accounting tools, Pilot, Fyle, and features built into QuickBooks and Xero, as well as newer AI bookkeeping startups. Anyone who used Botkeeper should confirm what happened to their data and service arrangements. The case shows the risk of depending on a vendor with concentrated revenue. This entry stays for reference, since older articles still recommend Botkeeper.
