Google Assistant is the voice assistant that ships on Android phones, Nest speakers and displays, Android Auto, Wear OS watches, and many third-party smart home devices. You wake it with a phrase or a button, then ask it to set a timer, add a calendar event, send a message, play music, or turn off the lights. It works well for short, concrete commands, and a large ecosystem of smart home brands supports it, which made it one of the more capable options for controlling a connected home.

Its limits come from how it was built. Assistant relies on fixed intents and a hand-built set of actions, so it handles a command it knows and struggles with open-ended requests or follow-up questions that need real reasoning. That is the main reason Google is moving to Gemini, which can hold a conversation and handle looser phrasing. Google has been replacing Assistant with Gemini on phones and on home devices, and some Assistant features have been removed or changed along the way. The speed and layout of that change differ by device and region.

Google Assistant is free and does not need a separate subscription, although some newer Gemini features on home devices sit behind a paid plan. If you own older speakers or displays, they may keep working with Assistant, get an update to Gemini, or lose features, depending on the model. For new projects, Google steers developers toward Gemini rather than Assistant actions, and people choosing a voice assistant today are more likely to be comparing Gemini, Alexa, and Siri than treating Assistant as a long-term pick.
