HubSpot began as an inbound marketing tool, and Marketing Hub is still the center of its original idea: attract visitors with useful content, capture them as contacts, and nurture them toward a purchase. It includes email marketing, landing pages, forms, blogging, social publishing, ad tracking, and SEO recommendations, all tied to a CRM that records every interaction with a contact. That shared data is what lets a marketer see which campaigns produce actual customers, not only clicks.

Automation and AI take much of the repetitive work. Workflows send emails or update records based on behavior, such as a page visit or a form submission. Breeze AI drafts emails, blog posts, social copy, and landing page text, suggests subject lines, and builds audience segments from a description. Reporting dashboards attribute revenue to campaigns, and lead scoring helps sales teams know who to call first.

Marketing Hub is sold in Free, Starter, Professional, and Enterprise editions. The free tools are generous for small teams, but the jump to Professional, where advanced automation and reporting live, costs substantially more and often includes an onboarding fee. Contacts are billed in tiers, so a growing list raises the bill. It suits small and mid-sized businesses that want marketing and sales in one place. Mailchimp is simpler and cheaper, while Adobe Marketo and Salesforce Marketing Cloud serve larger enterprises.
