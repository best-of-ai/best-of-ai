Asana organizes work as tasks inside projects. Each task has an owner, a due date, and a place in a larger plan, and the same project can be viewed as a list, a kanban board, a timeline, or a calendar. Teams use it for campaign planning, product launches, client work, and operations requests, anywhere several people need to see who is doing what and by when. Portfolios and Goals roll individual projects up into a view managers can read without opening every one.

Automation is a big part of the product. Rules can assign tasks, move them between stages, or ping a teammate when something changes, and forms turn incoming requests into tracked tasks. On the AI side, AI Studio lets teams build their own workflows that use AI to read, classify, and respond to work. AI Teammates are assistants that can be given tasks like any other member of a project. They can draft status updates, summarize long threads, and flag work at risk of slipping.

Asana has a free plan for small groups, and paid plans add things like timelines, portfolios, reporting, and admin controls. Larger plans include security features such as single sign-on. The AI features differ by plan, and heavier use may be metered, so check current limits before relying on them. Compared with Jira, Asana is aimed at general business teams rather than software engineering. Compared with Trello or Monday, it sits in the middle: more structure than a simple board and less setup than an enterprise work-management suite.
