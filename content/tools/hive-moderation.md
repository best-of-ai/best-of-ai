Hive offers content moderation as an API. A platform sends user uploads, such as photos, videos, livestreams, comments, or audio, and Hive returns classifications with confidence scores across categories like nudity, violence, hate symbols, drugs, and self-harm. Companies use these results to automatically remove content, send borderline cases to human reviewers, or restrict access, which would be impossible to do manually at scale.

The company's models have broadened beyond traditional moderation. Hive offers AI-generated content detection to identify synthetic images, video, and audio, deepfake detection used by government and media customers, and tools for brand safety and contextual advertising. It trains on large human-labeled datasets, and customers can customize settings for their own policies.

Hive is sold to platforms and enterprises with usage-based pricing, with a free trial for evaluation. Moderation always involves trade-offs between catching harmful content and over-blocking acceptable content, and models make mistakes, so human review remains important for sensitive decisions. Competitors include Amazon Rekognition, Google Cloud's content safety tools, Azure AI Content Safety, Sightengine, and OpenAI's moderation endpoint. Hive suits platforms with large amounts of user-generated content.
