Sora was OpenAI's attempt to build a general-purpose video model. You described a scene in text, or supplied an image or a clip, and it produced a short video with consistent characters, plausible physics, and controlled camera movement. When it first appeared it was well ahead of most competing video generators, and it later arrived as a standalone social app where people could generate, remix, and share clips.

It could also extend and edit existing footage, blend two clips, and generate several variations of a prompt. The app added features for placing yourself or friends in generated scenes through a consent-based likeness system. That feature drew attention and some controversy over deepfakes, copyright, and who owns the output, and OpenAI had to tighten the rules several times. Generating video also takes a lot of computing power, which made it expensive to run at scale.

OpenAI has shut down the Sora app and its API and moved its attention toward other products, so it can no longer be used for new work. Sora remains a research effort around models that simulate the physical world, and OpenAI said image generation in ChatGPT is unaffected. People who need a video generator should look at alternatives such as Google's Veo, Runway, Kling, Luma, or Pika. If you still have videos made with Sora, keep local copies, since the hosted library is not a permanent archive.
