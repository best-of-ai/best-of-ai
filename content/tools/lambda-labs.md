Lambda started by selling deep learning workstations and servers to researchers and grew into a GPU cloud. Developers rent instances with NVIDIA GPUs, from single machines to large clusters, pre-configured with the Lambda Stack of machine learning software, so they can start training or running models quickly. Its focus on AI workloads and straightforward pricing has made it a favorite among startups, universities, and AI labs.

The company offers several ways to buy compute. On-demand instances are billed by the hour, reserved capacity provides discounted access for longer commitments, and 1-Click clusters let teams spin up multi-node setups for large training runs. Lambda also sells hardware for on-premises use, including workstations and servers, and offers inference services for deploying models.

Lambda is paid, with pricing by GPU type and time, and availability of popular GPUs can be limited when demand is high. Support for advanced networking and storage is not as broad as hyperscale clouds like AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure. Alternatives include CoreWeave, Crusoe, RunPod, Vast.ai, Together AI, and Paperspace. Lambda suits teams that want simple, AI-focused GPU access.
