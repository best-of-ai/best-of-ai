Muse is Meta's step from a chat assistant to an agent that acts on your behalf. You ask it to do something, such as arrange a trip, draft and send an email, or handle a multi-step errand, and it works across apps and websites to complete the job. Each user's agent runs in a secure virtual machine, so it can continue working after you leave the app and come back when it needs a decision.

Control and safeguards are central. Muse asks for approval before it sends an email, makes a purchase, or takes similar sensitive steps, and users connect only the accounts they choose. The agent is powered by Muse Spark, Meta's own multimodal reasoning model, which supports tool use and coordinating multiple agents. It is available through a website, mobile apps, and messaging surfaces such as WhatsApp, starting in the United States.

Meta has said most everyday functions will be free, with paid plans for advanced use to follow, and that it plans to add features over time, including links to its smart glasses. Giving an agent access to email and calendars raises privacy and security questions, and the details of data retention should be reviewed. Competitors include ChatGPT Work, Claude Cowork and Duet, Manus, and Google's agents. Availability and features will likely change as the product rolls out.
