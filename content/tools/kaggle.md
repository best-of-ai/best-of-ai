Kaggle is where many data scientists learn and prove their skills. Companies and researchers post competitions with real problems, such as predicting outcomes from data or classifying images, and participants submit models to a leaderboard. Winning solutions are often published, and the discussion forums are a rich source of techniques. Rankings and medals help build a public track record.

The platform offers more than contests. Public datasets cover topics from health to sports, free notebooks run Python and R in the browser with access to GPUs and TPUs within limits, and a models hub shares pretrained models. Kaggle Learn provides short, practical courses on Python, pandas, machine learning, and more. Benchmarks and community challenges around large language models have been added, and the site integrates with Google Cloud.

Kaggle is free. Free compute has weekly limits, and datasets vary in quality and licensing, so check terms before using them for commercial work. Competitions reward leaderboard performance, which differs from building maintainable production systems. Alternatives include Hugging Face for models and datasets, Google Colab for notebooks, DrivenData and Zindi for competitions, and Papers with Code for research. Kaggle is an excellent place to practice and to find datasets to experiment with.
