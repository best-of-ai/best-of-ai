Kling is the AI video product from Kuaishou, the Chinese short-video company behind the Kwai app. Its models generate clips from a text description or a starting image, and they are well regarded for natural motion, realistic physics, and faces and bodies that stay coherent through a shot. When it first appeared, it was one of the strongest challengers to Western video tools, and it has stayed in the top group as the field has moved quickly.

The platform offers more than text-to-video. You can supply start and end frames, control camera movement, extend a clip into a longer sequence, apply motion from one video to another, and add synchronized sound or lip sync. Reference images help keep a character or product consistent across shots, which matters for ads and storytelling. Image generation and editing tools sit alongside the video features, and an API makes the models available to developers.

Kling has a free tier with a modest number of daily or monthly credits and paid plans that increase credits, resolution, and commercial rights. Generation takes time and uses credits fast, and results vary, so creators often make several versions. As with any tool from a company based in China, some users weigh data handling and content restrictions in their decision. Competitors include Runway, Luma, Google's Veo, Pika, and MiniMax's Hailuo.
