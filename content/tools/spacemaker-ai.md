Spacemaker was a Norwegian startup that applied AI to the first phase of building design. Given a site and a set of goals, it generates many layouts for buildings, then analyzes each for sunlight, daylight, wind comfort, noise, views, and the amount of floor area it provides. Designers can compare options and adjust parameters, rather than testing a few ideas by hand.

Autodesk acquired the company and integrated its technology into Autodesk Forma, a cloud platform for early design. Forma combines site context, environmental analysis, and design tools, and connects to Revit and other Autodesk products so early decisions can be carried into detailed design. The platform serves architects, developers, and city planners evaluating projects.

Forma is sold by subscription as part of Autodesk's offerings. Generated options are starting points and don't capture every local rule or design consideration, so professionals review and refine them. Alternatives include TestFit, Finch3D, Archistar, and Hypar for generative design, and Rhino with Grasshopper for custom workflows. This entry covers the product's original name, which many articles still use. Spacemaker suits teams doing early-stage site studies.
