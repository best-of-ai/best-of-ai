Cura converts 3D models into printer instructions. Users load a model, position it, choose a material and quality profile, and the software slices it into layers, with a preview that shows how the print will be built. Settings control supports, infill, speed, temperature, and more, and a simple mode hides complexity for beginners while a custom mode exposes hundreds of options.

It has a very large user base because it works with many printers, not only those made by UltiMaker. Community-maintained profiles cover popular brands, and a plugin marketplace adds features such as post-processing scripts and cloud integrations. Cura is developed in the open and has had long-running support from the maker community.

Cura is free under an open-source license. Newer slicers such as OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio have gained users with additional features and faster development in some areas, and Cura offers no meaningful AI features. Alternatives include PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer, Bambu Studio, Simplify3D as a paid option, and SuperSlicer. Cura suits makers who want a widely supported, free slicer.
