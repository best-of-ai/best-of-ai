Workday began as cloud HR software and expanded into financials. Large employers use it to keep employee records, run payroll and benefits, manage recruiting and performance, plan headcount and budgets, and close the books. Having HR and finance on one data model helps companies tie workforce decisions to costs.

AI shows up across the suite. It suggests candidates and skills matches, flags attrition risks, helps managers with planning, and drafts job descriptions and summaries. Workday has also introduced AI agents that take on tasks inside its apps, along with an agent management layer so that companies can control what those agents may do. It has bought AI companies to extend this, including some that build agent platforms.

Workday is sold to mid-sized and large organizations on subscription with custom pricing, and implementations take time and partner help. It is built for complex global employers more than small teams. Alternatives include SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle HCM, UKG, ADP, and Rippling for smaller firms. Workday suits large organizations that want one system for people and money.
