accessiBe promises to make websites more accessible with minimal effort. A line of code adds an accessibility interface for visitors, with options such as larger text, contrast adjustments, and keyboard navigation, while AI runs in the background to detect issues like missing image descriptions and improper labels and attempt to fix them. Businesses use it to reduce the risk of accessibility lawsuits and to serve users with disabilities.

The company has built other products, including audits, monitoring, and expert-assisted remediation. It reports compliance status and offers statements that businesses can publish. Its marketing emphasized automation and quick compliance.

The approach is controversial. Many accessibility professionals and disability advocates argue that overlay products cannot fully fix accessibility problems, can interfere with assistive technology, and do not by themselves guarantee legal compliance, and some companies using overlays have still been sued. The Federal Trade Commission has also taken action regarding marketing claims by an accessibility overlay company. Real accessibility generally requires fixing the underlying code and testing with users, using tools such as axe, WAVE, Deque, and manual audits. accessiBe may be useful as a supplement but should not be relied on alone.
