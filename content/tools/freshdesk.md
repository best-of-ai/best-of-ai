Freshdesk gives support teams a single place to handle customer requests. Messages from email, a web form, live chat, phone, and channels like WhatsApp, Messenger, and Instagram land in a shared inbox as tickets that can be assigned, tagged, prioritized, and tracked against service-level targets. A knowledge base lets customers find answers on their own, and a customer portal shows the status of their requests. It is used by companies from small startups to large support organizations.

Freddy AI adds automation. AI agents can answer common questions from your help articles and resolve simple requests without a human. A copilot suggests replies, summarizes long conversations, and helps agents decide what to do next, while insights flag trends and recurring problems. Automations route tickets by topic or urgency, escalate delayed ones, and handle repetitive actions, which keeps response times down as volume grows.

Freshdesk has a free plan for small teams and paid tiers that add automation, reporting, skills-based routing, and enterprise controls. AI agents are usually priced by usage, such as per resolved conversation, so costs scale with volume. It is generally easier to set up and cheaper than Zendesk, though large organizations with complex requirements may find Zendesk or Salesforce Service Cloud deeper. Help Scout, Intercom, and Zoho Desk are other common choices.
