Adobe Podcast started with one very useful trick: you upload a voice recording made in a noisy room, and it comes back sounding like it was recorded in a studio. Enhance Speech removes hiss, echo, and background noise and evens out the voice. People use it to rescue interview audio, remote recordings, and phone-quality clips, and it works well on single speakers talking clearly.

The wider product is a browser-based recording and editing studio. You can record yourself or a remote guest, then edit by deleting words in the transcript, much like editing a text document. Features that clean up speech, adjust volume, and add music or sound effects sit alongside it. A mic check tool tests how your setup sounds before you start. Because everything runs in the browser, there is nothing to install, and files can be exported for use in other editors.

Enhance Speech has been free to use with daily limits, and some of the heavier features sit behind an Adobe subscription. AI voice cleaning can sometimes leave voices sounding slightly processed, and it cannot fix badly clipped or heavily overlapping speech. Audition and Descript are the closer rivals for editing work, while tools like Auphonic and Krisp handle cleanup and noise removal. For quick fixes to spoken audio, it is one of the simplest tools to try.
