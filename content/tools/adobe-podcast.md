Adobe Podcast Enhance Speech does one thing extremely well: it takes audio recorded on a laptop microphone, a phone, or in a noisy room, and processes it to sound like it was recorded in a professional studio. Background hum, room echo, keyboard noise, HVAC hiss, and ambient chatter are removed automatically without manual noise reduction settings. You upload a file, wait about 30 seconds per minute of audio, and download the enhanced version. The improvement on poor-quality recordings is often startling.

The tool is free through Adobe's website (podcast.adobe.com) without needing an Adobe account for the basic enhancement feature — which has made it widely adopted among podcasters, educators, and remote workers who want better audio quality without investing in acoustic treatment or professional microphones. For people recording interviews over Zoom or Teams calls, running the audio through Enhance Speech afterward produces noticeably cleaner dialogue.

The broader Adobe Podcast platform also includes a browser-based recording tool that processes audio in real time during recording, a multi-track editor for arranging and editing podcast episodes, and transcript-based editing similar to Descript (delete words from the transcript to cut audio). The recording and editing environment requires a free Adobe account. It integrates with Premiere Pro and the broader Creative Cloud for post-production workflows. Audio journalists, corporate communicators, online educators, and independent podcasters use it most — particularly those who need a simple, effective enhancement tool rather than a full-featured audio workstation.
