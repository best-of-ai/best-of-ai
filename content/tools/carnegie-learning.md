Carnegie Learning grew out of research at Carnegie Mellon University on how people learn math. Its flagship product, MATHia, is an intelligent tutoring system that watches how a student solves each step of a problem, estimates which skills the student has mastered, and adjusts the next problem and hints accordingly. The aim is to give every student something like a personal tutor, with feedback at the moment a mistake occurs.

The company sells complete programs rather than isolated apps. Middle and high school math curricula combine textbooks, MATHia software, and teacher resources, with professional development to help teachers use the data. Dashboards show teachers which students are struggling and on which skills. The company has also added literacy and tutoring offerings, and more recent AI features support hints and explanations.

Carnegie Learning is sold to schools and districts under custom contracts, not to individual families, and results depend on teachers using the program as designed. Studies of its cognitive tutor approach have shown gains in some settings and smaller effects in others. Khan Academy's Khanmigo, IXL, DreamBox, and ALEKS are alternatives. For districts that want an evidence-based math program with adaptive software and teacher support, it is a well-known option.
