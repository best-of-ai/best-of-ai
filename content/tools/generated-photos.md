Generated Photos solves a licensing problem: real people's faces carry privacy and consent obligations, and stock photos can be expensive or restrictive. Its library holds images of faces created by generative models, which depict nobody in particular. Designers use them for website and app mockups, marketers for illustrations, and developers for placeholder avatars in user interfaces.

Several tools extend the library. A face generator lets you pick attributes such as age, gender, ethnicity, hair, and emotion and create new faces, and a photo anonymizer replaces a real face in a picture with a synthetic one. Datasets of synthetic faces serve machine learning teams who need varied images without privacy concerns, and an API provides access for automated use. Full-body and other synthetic image options have been added.

Generated Photos has a free tier with limited downloads and paid plans for broader use and commercial licensing. Synthetic faces carry ethical risks: they have been used to create fake profiles for scams and disinformation, so responsible use means not presenting them as real people. Some images show telltale flaws in teeth, ears, or backgrounds. Alternatives include general image generators like Midjourney and FLUX, stock sites that sell AI-generated people, and Icons8's Face Generator.
