Intercom began as a live chat widget for websites and now covers the full range of customer communication. It combines a messenger on your site or app, a shared inbox for support teams, a searchable help center, email, and product tours. Companies use it to answer questions, onboard new users, and keep conversations in one thread across channels. It is common among software and subscription businesses.

Fin is the AI agent at the center of the current product. It reads your help articles and past conversations, then answers customer questions directly in chat and email, including follow-ups. When it cannot solve a problem or the customer asks for a person, it hands the conversation to a human with context attached. Fin can also take actions in other systems, like checking an order or updating an account, if you connect it. A copilot for human agents drafts replies and summarizes long threads.

Intercom's pricing combines per-seat fees for human agents with usage charges for Fin, typically per resolved conversation, so costs track how many issues the AI actually handles. That model is transparent but can add up at high volume. The platform has a learning curve and is more expensive than basic tools like Freshdesk or Help Scout. Zendesk is the biggest alternative, and HubSpot and Gorgias offer overlapping features. Intercom is a good fit if you want AI-first support with a polished customer experience.
