Augury helps factories and facilities avoid unexpected equipment failures. Small wireless sensors attach to machines such as motors, pumps, fans, and compressors and measure vibration, temperature, and other signals. The data goes to the cloud, where machine learning models trained on large numbers of machines identify patterns that signal a developing problem, such as bearing wear or misalignment, often weeks before a breakdown.

The platform sends alerts with a diagnosis and recommended action, so maintenance teams can plan repairs during scheduled downtime instead of reacting to a failure. Dashboards show the health of assets across plants, and the service has expanded to include process monitoring and insights into production performance. Industries include food and beverage, chemicals, pulp and paper, and building management.

Augury is sold to industrial customers on subscription, often per machine or site, together with installation and support. Value depends on instrumenting critical equipment, and predictions are not perfect. Competitors include Siemens, GE Vernova, AVEVA, Samsara in some areas, SKF, and Emerson, along with a range of startups. Augury suits manufacturers who want to move from reactive to predictive maintenance.
