BigID starts with a question many companies cannot answer: where is our sensitive data? It scans databases, file shares, cloud storage, SaaS apps, and data lakes to discover personal information, financial records, and other regulated data, then classifies it using machine learning. The results build a catalog showing what data exists, where it sits, and who can access it. That map is the basis for privacy and security work.

Several functions build on the catalog. Privacy teams use it to respond to access and deletion requests, track consent, and meet rules like GDPR and CCPA. Security teams use it to find overexposed data, fix permissions, and reduce risk through remediation workflows. A newer focus is AI governance: the platform identifies which AI agents, copilots, and models can reach sensitive data, helps limit that access, and checks against frameworks such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and the EU AI Act.

BigID is sold to enterprises on custom pricing, with deployment options in the cloud or in a customer's own environment. Large scans can be complex to configure, and false positives in classification require tuning. Competitors include OneTrust, Varonis, Securiti, Cyera, and Microsoft Purview. It is aimed at security, privacy, and compliance teams at large organizations that handle substantial amounts of personal data.
