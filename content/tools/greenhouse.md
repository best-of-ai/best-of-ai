Greenhouse is an applicant tracking system for companies that want hiring to be systematic instead of ad hoc. Each job has a defined process: a posting, a set of stages, interview kits with questions, and scorecards that interviewers fill out against the same criteria. The aim is to reduce bias and gut-feel decisions by making every candidate go through the same structured steps. Recruiters, hiring managers, and interviewers each get a view suited to their part of the work.

Around the core are tools for sourcing, scheduling, and onboarding. Job posts can be sent to many boards at once, candidates can be sourced into a pipeline, and interview scheduling can be automated so recruiters are not exchanging dozens of emails. Reporting shows time to hire, funnel conversion, and where candidates drop out. A large marketplace connects it to HR systems, background check providers, and assessment tools.

AI features help with drafting job descriptions, summarizing interviewer notes, suggesting matches from an existing candidate pool, and handling routine communication. Greenhouse also publishes guidance on using AI fairly in hiring, which matters because automated screening can introduce bias and is increasingly regulated. It is sold on annual plans sized by company and features, and pricing is not public. It is common among mid-sized and growing tech companies. Lever, Ashby, and Workable are the main alternatives, and large enterprises often use Workday or SmartRecruiters.
