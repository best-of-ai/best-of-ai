After Effects works on a timeline of layers that you animate, composite, and apply effects to. It is used for broadcast graphics, title sequences, explainer videos, social animations, and visual effects shots. Keyframes, expressions, and a huge plugin ecosystem give it flexibility, and it connects closely with Premiere Pro, Photoshop, Illustrator, and Cinema 4D, so assets move between apps easily. Many motion designers spend most of their working day in it.

AI features target tedious tasks. Roto Brush uses machine learning to separate a moving subject from its background, which used to take frame-by-frame manual work. Content-aware fill removes objects such as wires or logos from video, and other tools track motion, detect scene edits, and help with text and audio. Generative options connect to Adobe's Firefly models for creating or extending elements. These speed up the routine parts while leaving the creative decisions to the artist.

After Effects is part of Adobe Creative Cloud and available by subscription, alone or in a bundle, on Windows and macOS. It has a steep learning curve and can be demanding on memory and graphics hardware. DaVinci Resolve's Fusion page is the strongest free alternative, Blender covers some 3D motion work, and Cavalry is popular for procedural animation. For compositing and motion design, After Effects still has the biggest tutorial base and talent pool.
