Indeed started as a search engine for job listings, pulling postings from company career pages and other boards into one place. Job seekers search by title and location, set alerts, upload a resume, and apply directly, often in a few clicks. The site also provides salary estimates, company information, and reviews, and Indeed is now closely tied to Glassdoor, which is part of the same company.

For employers, it is a major source of applicants. Companies post jobs for free or pay to sponsor them for more visibility, and tools help with screening questions, scheduling interviews, and messaging candidates. AI features match job seekers to relevant roles and recommend candidates to employers, summarize job requirements, and increasingly assist with resume improvement and hiring workflows. Recruiters search a resume database, with paid access to some functions.

Indeed is free for job seekers. Employers pay per click or per application for sponsored jobs, which can add up in competitive hiring markets. Listings can be outdated or duplicated, and some are scams, so verify employers before sharing personal information. LinkedIn is stronger for professional networking and white-collar roles, ZipRecruiter and Glassdoor overlap, and niche boards serve specific industries. Indeed is particularly strong for hourly, trades, and entry-level jobs.
