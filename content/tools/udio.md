Udio produces music with an emphasis on audio quality and handling of lyrics. You describe a song or supply lyrics, and Udio generates clips that you can extend into full tracks, remix, and edit section by section. Users liked it for realistic vocals and for the control it gives over structure, and it has been used for songwriting ideas, demos, and entertainment.

Its path changed after legal disputes with major record labels. Udio reached licensing agreements with some labels and moved toward a platform where creation and sharing happen inside the service, often described as a walled garden, with limits on downloading tracks. Plans to build tools around licensed music mean terms can continue to change, and the experience differs from earlier versions, which let users download freely.

Udio offers a free tier with limited credits and paid plans for more generations. Because the rules about ownership, downloads, and commercial use have shifted, check the current terms before depending on it. Suno is the closest competitor and has also made label agreements with different terms, Stability's Stable Audio and Google's tools are other options, and Soundraw and AIVA fit background music needs. Udio suits people who want to experiment and share within the platform.
