Duck.ai is a chat interface from DuckDuckGo that lets you use several third-party models in one place. You open duck.ai, pick a model and start typing. No sign-in is required. The lineup has included small GPT models from OpenAI, Claude Haiku from Anthropic, Llama from Meta and Mistral models, and it changes over time.

The privacy setup is the reason to use it. DuckDuckGo sends your prompt to the model provider with your IP address removed, so the provider sees DuckDuckGo instead of you. It says its agreements with those providers bar them from training on your chats and require them to delete the data within a set window, which DuckDuckGo gives as 30 days. Your conversation history is saved in your own browser, not on DuckDuckGo's servers, and you can wipe it with the Fire button.

The free tier has usage limits, and the strongest models sit behind a DuckDuckGo subscription. The models are mostly smaller or older ones, so expect weaker answers than the flagship versions of ChatGPT or Claude. Duck.ai is also a chat box, not a full assistant, so there are few extras such as file workflows or agents. It works well for quick questions you would rather not tie to an account.
