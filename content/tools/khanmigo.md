Khanmigo is designed around a tutoring philosophy. Instead of handing students the answer, it asks guiding questions, offers hints, and checks understanding, much like a good human tutor. It works with Khan Academy's exercises and videos, so it can step in when a student gets stuck on a particular problem. It also offers activities such as practicing a debate, writing with feedback, and exploring historical figures through role-play.

Teachers have a version with tools that save planning time. It can help create lesson plans, quizzes, rubrics, and examples, suggest ways to explain a concept, and differentiate material for different levels. Teachers can see how students interact with it, which provides insight into where they struggle. Safeguards are built in, such as moderation and notifying adults if conversations raise concerns, because the users are children.

Khanmigo is free for teachers, in many cases, and available to families and schools for a small subscription or through district partnerships, because running AI is costly. Like any language model, it can make mistakes, especially in math, so students should keep checking work. Evidence on AI tutors is still emerging, and results depend on how they are used. Alternatives and comparisons include Duolingo Max for languages, Socratic, Brainly, and ChatGPT's study mode, and human tutoring remains important for many students.
