Attention Insight offers an inexpensive shortcut to a classic design question: will anyone notice this? Upload a screenshot, ad, packaging mockup, or video, and its AI produces a heatmap of the areas likely to draw the eye in the first few seconds. The model is trained on eye-tracking data, so the predictions approximate what real viewers would do. It returns results in seconds, which makes it practical to compare several versions of a design.

Reports include scores such as Clarity and Focus, and you can mark areas of interest to see how much attention they receive, for example whether a call-to-action button competes with a logo. Reports can be shared by link, competitor designs can be compared, and an API lets teams build the check into their workflow. UX designers, marketers, and conversion specialists use it early in the design process, before they have traffic to test.

There is a limited free option and paid plans with seats, credits, and watermark-free reports. The tool predicts first-impression attention, not whether someone will understand the message or buy, so it complements usability testing and A/B tests instead of replacing them. Predictions can be less reliable for unusual designs. Competing tools include EyeQuant, Neurons, and Feng-GUI, and real eye-tracking or Hotjar-style heatmaps provide measured behavior.
