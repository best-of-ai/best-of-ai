Ancestry is a genealogy service. Users build a family tree and the site suggests matching records, such as birth certificates, census pages, immigration lists, and military files, from its collection. Hints appear as you add relatives, which helps trace lines back through generations.

Technology helps both records and relatives. Handwriting recognition and machine learning transcribe old documents so they become searchable, and the DNA test connects users with genetic relatives and estimates ethnic origins. Members can share trees and message distant cousins, which often breaks through research dead ends.

Ancestry requires a subscription for most records, and DNA kits are sold separately. Family tree hints can be wrong, and trees contributed by others contain errors, so verifying with documents matters. DNA data raises privacy considerations, so read the terms about data use. Alternatives include MyHeritage, FamilySearch, which is free, 23andMe, and Findmypast. Ancestry suits people researching their family history.
