Lottie is an animation format created at Airbnb that exports motion from After Effects as small JSON files. The files are vector-based, so they scale to any size without blur, and they are far lighter than GIFs or videos. App and web developers use them for loading spinners, onboarding screens, icons, and illustrations. A Lottie animation can be changed in code, colors can be swapped, and playback controlled, which makes it interactive.

LottieFiles is the company and community that grew up around the format. It offers a huge library of free and paid animations, a web-based Lottie Creator for making and editing animations without After Effects, plugins for Figma and After Effects, and players and SDKs for web, iOS, Android, and other platforms. A newer compressed format, dotLottie, reduces file sizes further. AI features help generate animations from text or convert existing assets into motion.

LottieFiles has a free tier and paid plans for teams, with extra storage, collaboration, and advanced features. Complex animations can still be heavy or hard to export, and not every After Effects feature is supported by the format. Rive is the main alternative, with an interactive runtime and its own editor, while SVGator and Jitter offer other web-based motion tools. For simple, lightweight motion in apps and websites, Lottie is the common standard.
