Buoy Health helps people decide what to do when they feel unwell. It asks a series of questions in a conversational flow, adapts based on the answers, and then provides possible explanations and a recommendation on the right level of care, such as self-care, a primary care visit, urgent care, or the emergency room. The aim is to reduce confusion and avoid unnecessary visits as well as delays in seeking urgent care.

The tool was developed with input from clinicians and drawn from large sets of medical literature and data. It's offered directly to consumers and through health systems, insurers, and employers, who embed it in their websites or apps to guide members to appropriate services. Some versions connect users with local providers or telehealth options.

Buoy is free for consumers. Like all symptom checkers, it provides information and not a diagnosis, and its accuracy has limits, so it should not replace professional advice, especially in an emergency. Health data privacy is important, so review how data is handled. Alternatives include Ada Health, K Health, Symptomate, and WebMD's symptom checker. Buoy suits people who want guidance before seeking care.
