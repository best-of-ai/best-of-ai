FlowGPT started as a place to share ChatGPT prompts. People post prompts for writing, coding, productivity, roleplay, and other uses, and others can browse by category, upvote the best, and copy them. Over time it added the ability to run prompts directly on the site and to build small chatbot-style apps, so a prompt can become a reusable tool.

The community aspect drives it. Creators gain followers and recognition for popular prompts, and the site hosts character chats and templates for tasks such as resume writing or SEO. It offers a way to learn prompting techniques by examining what others have written. Many of the contributions are playful or experimental, with quality that varies.

FlowGPT is free to use, with optional paid features. Prompts shared publicly may not be suitable for sensitive data, and a few categories of content on the site are adult-oriented, so check settings. The value of prompt libraries has declined as AI assistants have become better at understanding plain requests and offer built-in prompt features. Alternatives include AIPRM, PromptBase, which sells prompts, Hugging Face Spaces, and Reddit communities. FlowGPT suits people who like exploring community-made prompts.
