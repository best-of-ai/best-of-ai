Udemy opened online teaching to anyone. Instructors create video courses on a huge range of topics, from Python programming and UX design to photography and personal finance, and learners buy them individually, often at steep discounts. Courses can be watched at your own pace for life, and ratings and reviews help buyers choose. Quality varies widely, as there is little gatekeeping.

For companies, Udemy Business provides a curated library of courses with analytics and learning paths, which many organizations use for employee training. AI features include assistants that answer questions about course content, recommend lessons, and help instructors build courses, and role-play practice tools for skills training. Udemy has emphasized AI and technology skills, which are among its most popular categories.

Udemy has merged with Coursera, so it now operates as part of that company, and the combined offerings may evolve. Courses are paid individually, though they are frequently discounted, and a subscription option exists in some markets. Certificates of completion carry less weight than university credentials. Coursera and edX focus on university-backed courses, LinkedIn Learning offers professional training, Skillshare covers creative skills, and YouTube is free. Udemy suits learners looking for practical, affordable skills courses.
