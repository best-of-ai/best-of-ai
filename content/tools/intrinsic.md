Intrinsic works on a long-standing challenge in manufacturing: robots are powerful, but programming them is slow, specialized, and expensive. The company develops a software platform that lets developers build robotic applications, using skills and tools that can be reused across different robots and tasks. The idea is similar to how an operating system and app tools made computers easier to program for a wide range of hardware.

AI and simulation play a central role. Perception tools help robots see and understand objects, motion planning handles collisions, and simulation lets teams test and refine an application virtually before running it on the factory floor. Intrinsic works with robot makers and system integrators, and it has explored combining its platform with advanced AI models so that robots can handle more varied tasks without extensive reprogramming.

Intrinsic sells to manufacturers and integrators through custom engagements, not as a consumer product. It sits within Alphabet's longer-term efforts in robotics and is not a standalone consumer brand. Adoption depends on how easily it integrates with existing equipment, and industrial deployments take careful testing and safety validation. Competitors and alternatives include Siemens, ABB and Fanuc programming environments, NVIDIA's Isaac platform, Covariant-style AI picking systems, and open-source ROS.
