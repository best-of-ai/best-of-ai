Heptabase treats notes as cards that you place on whiteboards, group, and connect. Instead of a long document or a folder tree, you build up an understanding of a topic visually: a card for each idea, laid out next to related ones, with arrows and sections that show structure. It suits people who learn and plan by seeing the relationships among ideas, such as researchers, students, writers, and product thinkers.

Cards can live on multiple whiteboards at the same time, and edits show everywhere. Journals capture daily thoughts, PDF and web highlights bring source material into the workspace, and tags and backlinks help link ideas. The AI chat can answer questions based on the contents of your notes, summarize selected cards, and help find connections, so the notes become a knowledge base you can talk to.

Heptabase is a paid app with a free trial, available on desktop and web, with mobile support improving over time. It has a learning curve, and the approach appeals most to visual thinkers. It has fewer team collaboration and database features than Notion, and less plugin flexibility than Obsidian. Miro and FigJam are general whiteboards, Capacities and Tana take object-based approaches, and Logseq is open-source. Heptabase is a good choice for deep thinking and learning projects.
