Glassdoor made workplace information public. Employees write anonymous reviews of their employers, covering culture, management, pay, and benefits, and share salary data and the questions they were asked in interviews. Job seekers use it to research a company before applying or accepting an offer, and to check whether an offer is in line with what others earn in similar roles.

The site also runs a job board and tools for employers. Companies can respond to reviews, post jobs, and promote their employer brand, while job seekers can search listings, set alerts, and apply. AI features appear in job recommendations and in summaries of reviews and salary information. Many of the platform's operations have been merged with Indeed, its sister site, and Glassdoor is now legally part of Indeed, though the brand and reviews continue.

Glassdoor is free for job seekers, with a requirement to contribute a review or salary in some cases to see all content, and employers pay for advertising and branding products. Reviews can be skewed by those with strong opinions, can be gamed or removed, and are a limited signal on their own, so they are best read in aggregate with other research. Indeed, LinkedIn, Blind, Comparably, and Levels.fyi offer related information, with Levels.fyi strong for tech compensation.
