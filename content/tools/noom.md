Noom approaches weight loss as a behavior change problem. After a quiz about your habits and goals, the app builds a plan and delivers short daily lessons based on cognitive behavioral therapy, covering topics like emotional eating, habits, and triggers. A food log uses a color system for food density instead of strict calorie bans, and the app tracks weight, steps, and water.

Support comes through coaching and community. Depending on the plan, users can message a coach, join groups, and use an AI assistant to ask questions and get guidance. Noom has expanded into medical weight management, offering clinician-supervised programs that may include prescription medication in some regions. Programs are meant to help people form sustainable habits.

Noom is sold by subscription, usually after a trial, and has faced criticism over auto-renewal practices and marketing, resulting in a legal settlement. Weight-loss results vary and depend on adherence, and anyone with a medical condition or eating disorder should consult a professional. MyFitnessPal and Lose It are cheaper trackers, WeightWatchers combines coaching and community, and telehealth providers like Ro and Found focus on medication. Noom suits people who want structured, psychology-based support.
