Nintex helps organizations digitize their processes. Using a visual designer, business analysts build workflows that route requests, collect approvals, update systems, and send notifications, and they create forms to start those workflows. It integrates with Microsoft 365, SharePoint, Salesforce, and many other systems, which is where its roots lie, with many customers automating processes on SharePoint.

The platform has several parts. Process mapping tools document how work actually happens, automation tools execute workflows and robotic process automation, and document generation tools produce contracts and reports from data. AI features assist with building workflows from descriptions and extracting data from documents, and analytics show where processes slow down.

Nintex is sold to enterprises on subscription with custom pricing. Competitors include Microsoft Power Automate, ServiceNow, UiPath, Appian, Pega, and Zapier or Make for lighter needs. The value depends on picking processes worth automating and keeping workflows maintained. Many organizations choose between Nintex and Power Automate based on licensing and existing platform investments. Nintex suits large organizations with many approval-heavy processes.
