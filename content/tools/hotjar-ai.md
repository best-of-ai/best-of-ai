Hotjar helps teams see what visitors actually do on a site. Heatmaps show where people click, move, and scroll, session recordings replay individual visits, and on-page surveys and feedback widgets ask visitors what they think. Together they reveal confusing layouts, broken elements, and places where people give up, which complements numbers from analytics tools that show what happened but not why.

AI cuts down the time it takes to find insights. Instead of watching hundreds of recordings, teams can get automatic summaries of sessions, highlights of common frustrations, and analysis of open-ended survey responses grouped into themes. This helps designers, product managers, and marketers decide what to fix first. Funnels and trends let you see where users drop out, and integrations send findings to tools like Slack, Jira, and HubSpot.

Hotjar has a free plan with limited sessions and paid plans scaled by traffic. It operates under Contentsquare, which offers an enterprise-level digital experience platform. Recordings of real users raise privacy duties: sensitive fields must be masked and consent should follow regional laws. Competitors include FullStory, Microsoft Clarity, which is free, Mouseflow, and PostHog, which combines analytics and replays. Hotjar is a common first step into behavior analytics for small and mid-sized teams.
