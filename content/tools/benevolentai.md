BenevolentAI built a platform that gathers large amounts of biomedical information, including scientific literature, clinical data, and experimental results, into a knowledge graph that connects diseases, genes, proteins, and drugs. Machine learning explores the graph to suggest which biological targets might be worth pursuing for a given disease and which molecules might act on them, aiming to speed up the early stages of drug discovery.

The company gained attention when its system flagged baricitinib, an existing drug, as a possible treatment for COVID-19, which was later supported by clinical trials. It has formed partnerships with large pharmaceutical firms and developed its own programs. The platform combines AI with scientists who validate predictions in the lab.

BenevolentAI was listed on a stock exchange and later went private, saying it would focus on making its technology available to partners for drug research and development. As with other AI drug discovery companies, the main question is whether the approach produces better medicines, which takes years of trials to answer. Peers include Recursion, Insilico Medicine, Isomorphic Labs, Exscientia, now part of Recursion, and Schrödinger. It's relevant to pharma and biotech organizations.
