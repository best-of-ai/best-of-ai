Exscientia designed small-molecule drug candidates with AI models and ran automated labs to synthesize and test the top predictions. Results fed back into the models to improve the next round, a closed loop that aimed to shorten discovery time and reduce the number of compounds that must be made.

The company worked on cancer and other diseases, and formed partnerships with large pharmaceutical firms. It was among the first to move AI-designed molecules into clinical trials. After a period of strategic changes, it combined with Recursion, which brought together Recursion's biology data and Exscientia's chemistry design and automated synthesis.

Exscientia no longer operates as a separate company, and its capabilities are part of Recursion's platform. AI drug discovery remains early, and clinical success rates are still unproven. Alternatives and peers include Recursion, Insilico Medicine, Schrödinger, Isomorphic Labs, and Relay Therapeutics. This entry is kept for reference.
