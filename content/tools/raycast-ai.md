Raycast starts as a Spotlight replacement — press the hotkey, type what you want, and launch apps, search files, run scripts, or manage calendar events through a keyboard-driven interface that's faster and more capable than macOS's built-in launcher. The AI layer activates from the same interface with a keyboard shortcut: it opens an inline AI chat that's aware of what's on your screen, what's in your clipboard, and what you were just doing, without requiring you to switch to a browser tab or a separate app.

The AI commands let you accomplish common tasks from anywhere on your Mac. Highlight an email draft and trigger "Improve Writing." Select a paragraph and hit "Translate to Spanish." Copy a Stack Overflow error message and run "Explain this error." The AI is aware of the context — it knows you're working on email when it helps you rewrite a subject line, which produces more relevant suggestions than a general-purpose chat interface. Custom AI commands let you build your own recurring workflows: a command that drafts a specific type of document, summarises highlighted text in a specific format, or processes clipboard content in a standard way.

Raycast is free with the core launcher features and basic AI access. Raycast Pro at $8/month unlocks unlimited AI usage with GPT-4o, Claude, and other models. The extension marketplace gives access to community-built integrations for tools like GitHub, Jira, Notion, and dozens of other products. Raycast is available only on macOS. The user base is heavily skewed toward developers and designers — people who work keyboard-first and want every task, including AI queries, available through a single fast interface rather than scattered across multiple apps.
