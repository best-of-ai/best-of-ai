Shorthand makes it possible to create the kind of immersive web story that once required a design and development team. You assemble a story from sections with full-width images, video backgrounds, parallax effects, and text, and the platform handles responsive layout and animation. The finished piece is published as a webpage that scrolls like a magazine feature, and works on phones and desktops.

Newsrooms, universities, nonprofits, and corporate communications teams use it for long-form journalism, annual reports, campaign stories, and case studies. Collaboration features let writers and editors work together with review stages, brand themes keep designs consistent, and analytics show readership. Stories can be embedded in a site or hosted by Shorthand, and a CMS integration helps publishing.

Shorthand is paid, with plans for individuals, teams, and organizations, and offers a trial. It's focused on narrative publishing and isn't a general website builder. Alternatives include Adobe Express pages, Pageflow, Atavist, Readymag, Flourish for data stories, and custom development. AI features are limited compared with design tools like Canva. Shorthand suits communications teams that want polished scrollytelling without engineers.
