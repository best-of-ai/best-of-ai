Vision Pro puts a computer in front of your eyes. High-resolution displays and cameras show the real world with digital windows and objects placed in it, and you interact by looking at things and pinching your fingers, with no controllers needed. It runs visionOS and supports iPad and Apple apps, along with apps made for spatial computing, such as immersive video, 3D design, and games.

Typical uses include watching movies on a giant virtual screen, working with multiple floating windows, viewing and recording spatial photos and videos, and previewing 3D models. Mac Virtual Display mirrors a Mac into the headset, and enterprise users explore training, design review, and remote collaboration. Apple Intelligence features are being added to visionOS, including writing tools and a smarter Siri, and the device's sensors power features like hand tracking and scene understanding.

Vision Pro is expensive, which limits its audience, and it is heavy enough that long sessions can be uncomfortable. The app ecosystem is smaller than for phones, and battery life is limited by an external pack. Meta Quest headsets are far cheaper and focus on gaming and social VR, Samsung's Galaxy XR runs Android XR, and Xreal glasses offer lighter display options. Vision Pro suits developers, early adopters, and professionals exploring spatial computing.
