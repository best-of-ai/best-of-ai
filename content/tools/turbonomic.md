Turbonomic tries to answer a question that infrastructure teams face constantly: is each application getting the resources it needs without wasting money? The platform monitors applications and the infrastructure they run on, including virtual machines, containers, and cloud services, and uses analytics to decide where workloads should run, how large they should be, and when to scale. It can take actions automatically, such as resizing instances, moving workloads, or adjusting Kubernetes resources.

The approach ties application performance to resource decisions, so reducing cost does not harm user experience. Features include cloud cost optimization, reservation and savings plan guidance, Kubernetes optimization, and tracking of sustainability. IBM acquired Turbonomic and integrates it with its automation and observability products, such as Instana. It offers dashboards that show the effect of changes and potential savings.

Turbonomic is sold to enterprises on subscription, with pricing based on the size of the environment. Automation needs trust and careful policy settings, so many teams start in recommendation mode. Competitors and complements include CloudHealth, Apptio Cloudability, Spot by NetApp, Kubecost, and the native tools of AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud. Turbonomic suits large organizations with complex hybrid infrastructure.
