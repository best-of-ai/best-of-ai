Oura packs sensors into a ring that is comfortable to wear day and night. It tracks sleep stages, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, body temperature, and movement, then gives scores for sleep and readiness that suggest how hard to push each day. Many people choose it for sleep tracking, since a ring is less obtrusive than a watch in bed, and battery life lasts several days.

The app presents trends and offers guidance. Features cover cycle and fertility insights, stress and recovery information, activity goals, and workouts. An AI advisor lets users ask questions about their data in plain language and receive personalized suggestions. Oura works with Apple Health, Google Health Connect, and other apps, and it has partnered with companies in areas like glucose monitoring.

The ring is a hardware purchase, and the app features require a monthly or annual membership, which is a recurring cost many reviewers note. Oura is a wellness device, not a medical device for diagnosis, and its estimates of sleep and readiness are approximations. Accuracy varies by person, and sizing matters. Competitors include Whoop, Ultrahuman, Samsung's Galaxy Ring, Apple Watch, and Garmin. Oura suits people who want sleep and recovery insights without wearing a watch.
