HireVue lets candidates complete interviews on their own schedule. Employers set up structured questions, candidates record video answers, and recruiters review them later, which saves scheduling time when hiring many people. The platform has expanded to include game-based and skills assessments, live interviews, text-based chat for scheduling and screening, and tools for coding interviews.

Its use of AI has been controversial. HireVue once analyzed facial expressions and voice in interviews, which drew criticism from privacy and civil rights groups, and it dropped facial analysis. Today it describes using structured scoring based on what candidates say and on job-relevant skills, and publishes audits and explanations of its models. Regulations in places such as New York City and Illinois now require notice, consent, or bias audits for automated hiring tools.

HireVue is sold to enterprises through custom contracts and is widely used in retail, finance, and healthcare. Candidates sometimes find one-way video interviews impersonal. Employers should validate any assessment for fairness and legal compliance. Competitors include Eightfold, Paradox for conversational recruiting, Spark Hire, Willo, and the interview features of applicant tracking systems. HireVue suits organizations that screen large numbers of applicants.
