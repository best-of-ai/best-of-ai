Ramp issues corporate cards and wraps them in software for controlling spending. Companies give employees physical and virtual cards with limits and rules, and receipts are captured and matched automatically through the app. Finance teams see spending in real time and avoid chasing employees for expense reports. Ramp is known for fast growth among startups and mid-sized businesses.

The product has widened into a finance operating system. Bill pay handles vendor invoices, procurement tools manage purchase requests and approvals, and accounting automation categorizes transactions and syncs with systems such as NetSuite, QuickBooks, and Xero. AI agents review expenses against policy, suggest ways to cut costs from vendor spending data, and handle routine tasks such as coding transactions and preparing month-end close.

Ramp's core features are free, and the company earns revenue from card interchange, with paid plans for advanced capabilities. Eligibility depends on a company's finances. Competitors include Brex, which is now part of Capital One, Mercury, Amex and Chase business cards, Divvy, and Expensify. Companies should weigh data privacy when connecting AI to financial information. Ramp suits startups and mid-sized businesses looking for modern spend control.
