DeepSeek is an AI research company based in China whose models are released with their weights open, so anyone can download, run, and fine-tune them. Its general-purpose models handle chat, writing, and coding, and its reasoning models work through a problem step by step before answering. What got the industry's attention was efficiency: DeepSeek's models perform near the best closed systems on many math and coding benchmarks while being far cheaper to train and serve. Its technical papers, which describe how it did this, are widely read by other labs.

There are three ways to use it. The chat app and website are free and include a mode that shows the model's reasoning. The API is priced well below most competitors and follows the same format as OpenAI's, so many tools can switch to it by changing a URL and key. The open weights can be downloaded and run on your own hardware, which is the option that matters most for companies that cannot send data to a third party. The full models are very large, so most people run smaller distilled versions on a single machine.

There are trade-offs to weigh. The hosted app and API run on servers in China, and its privacy policy and censorship behavior have drawn scrutiny; the models decline or reshape answers on some politically sensitive topics. Several governments and companies have restricted its use on work devices. Running the open weights yourself avoids the data-handling concern but not the content behavior baked into the model. For cost-sensitive developers and teams that want to self-host, it is one of the strongest options available.
