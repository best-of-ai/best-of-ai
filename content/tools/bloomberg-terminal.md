The Bloomberg Terminal is the main workstation for traders, portfolio managers, analysts, and bankers. It delivers real-time and historical prices across stocks, bonds, currencies, and commodities, together with news, research, company data, and analytics. A command-based interface lets users call up functions with short codes, which is why people who use it daily rarely touch menus. Its messaging system, Bloomberg Chat, is also where much of the market does its dealing.

AI now helps with the reading workload. Tools summarize earnings calls and long documents, surface key points from news, and let users ask questions of filings and transcripts in plain language. Bloomberg has also built its own financial language model, and its data is often used by outside AI tools. The point is to cut the time analysts spend reading, while the numbers themselves come from Bloomberg's own data feeds.

The Terminal is sold by subscription per user, with a price that runs to many thousands of dollars a year, and it includes proprietary hardware and a dedicated support desk. That cost keeps it mostly in institutions. People outside finance can find free options such as Yahoo Finance, TradingView, and OpenBB for a smaller set of features. Competitors include LSEG Workspace, FactSet, and S&P Capital IQ, though few match the breadth of Bloomberg's data and network.
