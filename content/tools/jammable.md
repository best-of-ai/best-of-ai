Jammable, formerly Voicify, lets people hear songs sung in a different voice. A user chooses a voice model from a library, uploads a vocal or song, and the system converts it so it sounds like the chosen voice. Fans and creators use it for parodies, mashups, and experiments.

The technology raises serious legal and ethical issues. Voices of real artists are protected in many places, and music labels and artists have objected to unauthorized voice clones. Sharing such covers can violate copyright and rights of publicity, and platforms may remove them. Use voices you have permission to use, and avoid presenting synthetic songs as the real artist.

Jammable has a free tier and paid plans. Alternatives include ElevenLabs voice tools, RVC-based apps, Kits.ai with licensed voices, and Suno and Udio for original music. Jammable suits hobbyists exploring voice conversion, with attention to rights.
