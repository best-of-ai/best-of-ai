Liner started as a tool for highlighting text on web pages and saving it. Users mark passages in articles and PDFs, which are collected and organized, so they can find them later. A browser extension and app make highlighting part of normal browsing.

AI features add search and summaries. An AI search engine returns answers with sources and surfaces relevant parts of highlighted content, and tools summarize pages and documents. A chat assistant answers questions about what a user is reading. It is popular with students and researchers, particularly in Korea where the company began.

Liner has a free tier and paid plans. AI summaries can miss nuance, so check sources. Alternatives include Readwise, Perplexity, Hypothesis, Notion Web Clipper, and NotebookLM. Liner suits students and researchers who read a lot online.
