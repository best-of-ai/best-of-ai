Cleanup.pictures does one thing with minimal effort. Upload a photo, brush over a stranger in the background, a power line, a logo, or a blemish, and the tool removes it and fills the gap with a plausible background. Results come back within seconds and can be downloaded, which makes it a quick fix for product photos, real estate listings, travel pictures, and social media posts.

It works well on simple backgrounds such as sky, grass, walls, and water, and less well when the removed object sits in front of detailed patterns, faces, or text that the model would have to invent. For larger images, a higher-resolution output is available on paid plans. The same kind of inpainting appears in other tools, including Clipdrop, which offers a related cleanup feature, along with Photoshop's Generative Fill and Remove tool and the Magic Eraser on Google Pixel phones.

The free tier limits the resolution and number of images, and a Pro plan offers higher resolution and fewer restrictions. As with all AI editing tools, check the output at full size for smudges and repeated textures. Removing watermarks from images you do not own raises copyright concerns, so use it on your own photos. Photoroom, Canva, and Adobe Express provide similar cleanup tools inside broader editors.
