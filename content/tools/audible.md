Audible made audiobooks mainstream. Members get monthly credits to buy audiobooks they keep, plus access to a catalog of included titles, podcasts, and originals, and they can listen through the app on phones, tablets, and smart speakers. Features such as variable speed, bookmarks, sleep timers, and syncing between listening and reading with Kindle books make it practical for commuting and chores.

Technology is changing how titles are made. Audible has been introducing AI narration for publishers and authors who cannot afford a human narrator, and AI-powered translation and dubbing for audiobooks, alongside AI-based recommendations and summaries. Human narrators remain central to the premium experience, and the shift has prompted debate among narrators and listeners about quality and the future of the profession.

Audible requires a membership or purchase of individual titles, with a free trial, and its credit model and catalog access differ by plan. Audiobooks bought there can be tied to Amazon's apps, which limits portability. Alternatives include Libro.fm, which supports independent bookstores, Spotify's audiobook offerings, Apple Books, Storytel, and library apps such as Libby, which are free with a library card. Audible suits people who listen to a lot of books and like Amazon's ecosystem.
