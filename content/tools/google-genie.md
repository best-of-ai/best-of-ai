Genie is a different kind of generative model. Instead of producing a fixed image or video, it creates a world you can explore: describe a scene, and the model generates an environment that responds as you move through it, frame by frame, with the scene staying consistent as you turn around. DeepMind frames world models as a step toward AI that understands how environments behave, with possible uses in games, robotics training, and simulation.

Successive versions have improved quality, duration, and control. Later releases add features such as the ability to change things in the world while it runs, like weather or characters, and to keep scenes stable for longer. Project Genie is a prototype web app that lets users sketch, explore, and remix worlds, with limits on the length of each generation, and Google marks outputs with a watermark to show they are AI-generated.

Access has been limited to a research preview and to subscribers of Google's highest AI plan in the United States, with expansion planned over time. Generated worlds don't always follow real physics, and sessions are short. Competitors and related work include Odyssey, Decart, World Labs' Marble, Runway's world models, and game engines like Unreal for traditional development. Genie is of most interest to researchers and curious early adopters.
