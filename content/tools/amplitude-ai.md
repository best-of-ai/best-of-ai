Amplitude helps product teams see what users actually do. You instrument your app or website to send events, such as signing up, clicking a button, or finishing a purchase, and Amplitude turns them into funnels, retention curves, user paths, and cohorts. These views answer questions like where new users drop off and which features bring people back. It is used by product managers, designers, and data analysts at companies from startups to large enterprises.

Amplitude has grown into a broader platform. Experiment runs A/B tests and feature flags, Session Replay shows recordings of real sessions, and Guides and Surveys let teams show in-product messages. Its customer data tools collect and route event data to other systems. The AI features include a natural-language question box so people who do not write queries can ask about their data, and agents that watch metrics, spot changes, and suggest follow-ups.

Amplitude has a free plan suited to smaller products and paid tiers that scale with event volume and add features. Costs rise with traffic, so planning the events you track is important. Getting good insight also depends on clean data collection, which takes engineering work up front. Mixpanel, Heap, and PostHog are the usual alternatives, and Google Analytics covers simpler website measurement.
