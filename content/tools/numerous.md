Numerous brings language models into the spreadsheet. After installing the add-in, you use a formula such as =AI("prompt", cell) in a cell to ask the model to do something with the data: categorize customer feedback, write product descriptions, extract names from messy text, translate, or summarize. Filling the formula down a column performs the task on hundreds or thousands of rows, which would take hours by hand.

It aims to be approachable for people who know spreadsheets but not code. Prompts are written in plain language, results appear in cells where you can filter and calculate with them, and features help with common tasks like cleaning, tagging, and brainstorming. Marketers, e-commerce teams, researchers, and operations staff use it to process lists of items quickly.

Numerous is paid, with plans based on the number of AI calls, and a free trial. AI outputs can be inconsistent across rows, so spot-checking is important, and sending data to an outside model has privacy implications. Alternatives include Excel's Copilot and the COPILOT function, Google Sheets' Gemini, Formula Bot, Ajelix, and GPT for Sheets. Numerous suits spreadsheet users who want AI over lists of text.
