Dify gives teams a visual way to build AI applications without writing everything from scratch. In its workflow editor, you connect blocks for models, prompts, knowledge retrieval, code, conditions, and tool calls to create chatbots, agents, and automations. Knowledge pipelines ingest documents, split and index them, and let the application answer questions from your own data. You can test and debug each run, then publish the app as a web interface or API.

Support for many model providers lets you mix commercial and open-source models, and a plugin marketplace adds tools and integrations. Observability features show logs and costs, and prompt tools help refine instructions. Because Dify is open source, companies can run it on their own servers with Docker, and an enterprise edition adds single sign-on, role-based access, and compliance controls for self-hosted or private cloud deployments.

Dify Cloud offers hosted plans that scale with usage, and a free tier for experimenting. The community edition is available under a license based on Apache 2.0 with some additional conditions, so check the terms if you plan to offer it as a commercial service. Visual builders trade flexibility for speed, and complex logic can become hard to maintain. n8n, Flowise, Langflow, and Coze are alternatives, and LangGraph or CrewAI suit developers who prefer code.
