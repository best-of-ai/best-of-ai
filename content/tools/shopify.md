Shopify lets someone start an online store without building the software behind it. You choose a theme, add products, set up payments and shipping, and the platform handles hosting, the checkout, and tax and currency settings. The same system also covers in-person sales through point-of-sale hardware, so a shop can manage online and physical inventory together. Everything from a one-person side business to large brands runs on it, and Shop Pay, its accelerated checkout, is used well beyond Shopify's own stores.

A big app store extends the platform with tools for email, reviews, subscriptions, loyalty programs, and more. Shopify's AI features sit across the admin. Shopify Magic writes product descriptions and generates other store content, and Sidekick is an assistant that answers questions about your store, builds reports, and helps with setup tasks like creating discounts or editing a theme. Shopify has also been working on agentic commerce, which lets products be discovered and bought through AI assistants and chat platforms.

Shopify charges a monthly subscription that depends on the plan, plus payment processing fees, and it typically takes a cut if you use an outside payment provider. Apps add to the monthly cost, so a store with many paid add-ons can end up spending more than expected. It offers a trial for new merchants. Themes are easy to customize, but deep changes require knowing Liquid, Shopify's template language, or hiring a developer. Compared with WooCommerce, which you host yourself, Shopify trades some flexibility for a simpler setup.
