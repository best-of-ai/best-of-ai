Clarifai started as an image recognition API and has grown into a broader AI platform. Teams use it to find and call models for vision, language, and audio tasks, to train their own models on labeled data, and to run them in production. It includes tools for organizing and labeling data, searching images and video, and building workflows that chain several models together. Industries such as retail, media, and public sector have used it to classify content and find objects in large collections.

A major focus now is compute orchestration and inference. Clarifai lets organizations deploy models on a mix of infrastructure, whether that is a public cloud, their own data centers, or edge devices, and manage them from one place. It also supports hosting open-source and third-party models behind an API, and it can run agent-style applications. For customers with strict data rules, the ability to keep workloads inside their own environment is a main selling point.

There is a free tier for experimenting and paid plans billed on usage, with enterprise options for private deployments. It is more of a platform for AI and data teams than a point-and-click tool, so it takes some technical setup. Competitors include AWS Rekognition and Google Cloud Vision for image analysis, and Hugging Face, Replicate, and Together AI for hosting models. Its advantage over the cloud providers is flexibility in where models run.
