Bumble set itself apart by changing who starts the conversation. When a man and a woman match, the woman has to send the first message within a time limit, which the company says reduces unwanted messages and gives women more control. In same-sex matches, either person can begin. The app uses the swipe format, and offers filters for interests, intentions, and distance.

Beyond dating, Bumble has modes to meet friends and to network professionally, though these have been less central to its success. Safety features include photo verification, a feature that detects and blurs unwanted nude images, and tools for reporting and blocking. AI is used to detect fake profiles, spam, and scams, and to flag harassing messages, which are persistent problems for dating apps.

Bumble is free to use, with paid tiers that add features such as seeing who has liked you, unlimited swipes, and more control over matches. As with other dating apps, users report fatigue, scams, and uneven match quality, and results vary by city. Hinge is often seen as geared toward longer-term relationships, Tinder has the largest casual user base, and Feeld and Coffee Meets Bagel appeal to other preferences. Be careful about sharing personal information early in any dating app.
