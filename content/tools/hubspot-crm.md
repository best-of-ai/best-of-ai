HubSpot CRM is the shared database underneath every HubSpot product, and it can also be used on its own at no cost. It stores contacts, companies, deals, and tickets, logs emails and calls against each record, and shows a pipeline of deals by stage. Connecting Gmail or Outlook means sent and received messages are attached to the right contact automatically, so people spend less time on manual data entry. For a small business that has been running on spreadsheets, it is an easy first step into a real CRM.

The free tier covers basics like contact management, deal pipelines, email tracking, meeting scheduling, a live chat widget, and simple reporting. AI features include an assistant that drafts emails and summarizes contact records, along with scoring that helps sort leads and deals. More advanced automation, forecasting, and the AI agents belong to the paid tiers, which are sold per hub as Starter, Professional, and Enterprise. Moving up a tier changes the price a lot, so check what you need before committing.

HubSpot CRM is a good fit for small sales and service teams that want something usable on day one and are not ready for a complex implementation. Its marketplace has hundreds of integrations, and the interface is easier to learn than Salesforce's. The trade-offs are that some limits on the free plan, such as reporting and automation, push growing teams toward paid plans, and heavily customized processes can be harder to model. Zoho CRM, Pipedrive, and Salesforce are the usual alternatives.
