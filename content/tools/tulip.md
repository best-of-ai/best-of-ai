Tulip brings software to the factory floor. Engineers and operators build apps using a drag-and-drop editor, with steps that guide workers through assembly, inspection, or maintenance, collect data, and trigger actions. Connecting sensors, machines, and tools lets the apps read real-time values, and the platform replaces clipboards and spreadsheets with digital work instructions and quality checks.

Analytics and automation extend the value. Dashboards show production performance, quality, and downtime, and machine learning models can detect anomalies or predict issues. Generative AI helps create apps from a description and analyze data in plain language. As a lightweight manufacturing execution system, it can be adopted gradually, line by line.

Tulip has a free plan for small use and paid subscriptions for production, with pricing for interfaces, edge devices, and users. It targets manufacturers in industries such as electronics, medical devices, and automotive. Competitors include Siemens Opcenter, Plex, Parsable, L2L, and Sight Machine for analytics. Rolling out across plants requires change management. Tulip suits manufacturers digitizing frontline work.
