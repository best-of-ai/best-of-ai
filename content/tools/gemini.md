Gemini is Google's family of AI models and the assistant built on top of them. You can use it in the standalone Gemini app and website, but it also shows up inside the products many people already use: it can help draft emails in Gmail, rewrite text in Docs, summarize files in Drive, and answer questions on Android phones. If most of your work and personal life already runs on a Google account, that integration is the main reason to choose Gemini over another assistant.

The app does a lot beyond chat. It takes images, documents, audio, and video as input and works with a long context window, so you can load large files at once. Deep Research builds a multi-step plan, searches many sources, and returns a cited report, and it can draw on your own Gmail and Drive content when you allow it. Gemini Live supports spoken conversation and can work with what your camera or screen shows. Canvas is a side workspace for drafting documents and building small apps. Gems let you save custom versions of the assistant for a recurring job, and Google's image and video models are accessible from the same app.

The basic app is free, and paid Google AI plans raise usage limits and unlock the strongest models and newer features. These plans often come bundled with Google One storage, and Workspace customers get Gemini through their company accounts. Developers can reach the same models through Google AI Studio and the Gemini API. Plan names and limits change often, so check Google's current subscription page. One thing to keep in mind is that, like other consumer assistants, conversations may be reviewed and used to improve Google's products unless you adjust your activity settings.
