Duolingo made language learning feel like a game. Lessons take a few minutes and mix translation, matching, listening, and speaking exercises. Streaks, experience points, and leagues reward consistency, and the friendly design and persistent reminders turned it into one of the most used education apps in the world. Courses cover languages from Spanish and French to Japanese, Korean, and smaller options, and it also offers math, music, and chess.

AI is a growing part of the product. A paid tier called Max includes features such as explanations of why an answer was right or wrong and role-play conversations with an AI character, and video call practice lets learners speak with an animated character. The company also uses AI to generate and personalize lesson content, which has been controversial among some users and staff who worry about quality and jobs.

Duolingo is free with ads and limited hearts for mistakes, and paid plans remove ads, allow unlimited practice, and add AI features. The app is excellent at building habit and vocabulary but is not enough on its own to reach fluency, since real conversation and listening practice with humans matter. Babbel is more structured, Busuu adds feedback from native speakers, Pimsleur focuses on audio, and iTalki connects you with tutors. Many learners combine Duolingo with another source.
