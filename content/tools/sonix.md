Sonix focuses on fast, accurate machine transcription with a polished editor. You upload a file or connect a source, and get a transcript with timestamps and speaker labels, which you can edit while listening and the text highlights as the audio plays. It supports over forty languages and can translate transcripts into many more. Journalists, researchers, podcasters, and video producers use it to turn recordings into searchable text.

Additional tools support publishing. Sonix exports subtitles and captions in standard formats, offers a media player that embeds the transcript, and provides search across all your files. Integrations and an API connect it with video editors like Premiere Pro and Final Cut, and Zoom. Teams can share folders and custom vocabulary to improve accuracy on specialized terms.

Sonix uses pay-as-you-go pricing by the hour or subscription plans, with a free trial. As with any automatic transcription, accuracy drops with noise and overlapping speech. Otter, Descript, Trint, Rev, and Happy Scribe are competitors, and Whisper is a free open-source engine for those who can run it themselves. Sonix is a good fit for people who want a clean editor and straightforward pricing.
