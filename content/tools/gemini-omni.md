In the Gemini app, Omni replaces Veo as the video model, and Google describes it as Nano Banana for video. You give it text, photos, or a short clip, and it returns video with generated audio. Version 1.2 Flash, which Google published on November 5, 2026, makes clips of 3 to 15 seconds at up to 4K and 24 frames per second.

Editing is the main difference from earlier video generators. You can tell Gemini to swap a character, change the lighting or background, stabilize a shot, extend a scene, or keep refining the result over several turns. Up to five photos can become one video, and you can add an avatar of yourself to make clips that look and sound like you. Google embeds SynthID watermarks in the output and restricts changing people's speech for now.

Omni is in the Gemini app for Google AI subscribers, and Google also lists Flow, YouTube, Workspace, Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, and Vertex AI. API video output costs $12 per million tokens, about 7 cents per second at 720p, and the free API tier does not include it. On Arena's text-to-video leaderboard on November 8, Omni models held the top three places. DeepMind still lists Veo as a separate model. Alternatives include Veo, Kling, Runway, Wan, and Hailuo. Omni suits people who want to generate and revise video in one place.
