AutoCrit is an editing tool for novelists. A writer pastes in a chapter or uploads a manuscript, and the software reports on things editors look for: repeated words and phrases, adverbs, filter words, clichés, sentence variety, and dialogue. Reports highlight the text so you can see where to revise.

Its distinctive feature is comparison with published fiction. It measures a manuscript against patterns from successful books in the same genre, such as pacing and the balance of dialogue and description, so a writer can see how their draft differs. Generative features offer suggestions on revision and brainstorming, and a library of guides helps with craft.

AutoCrit has a free tier and paid plans. Automated feedback applies rules and averages, and good writing often breaks them, so use the reports as prompts for thought rather than orders. Alternatives include ProWritingAid, Grammarly, Hemingway Editor, Sudowrite, and a human editor. AutoCrit suits fiction writers revising drafts.
