HeyGen lets you make a talking-head video without filming. You choose an avatar or create your own from a short recording, paste or write a script, and HeyGen generates a video with natural speech and lip movement. It supports dozens of languages, and one of its popular uses is video translation: upload a video of yourself, and it produces a version in another language with your voice and matching lip movements. Marketers, trainers, and sales teams use it to produce content quickly.

Features cover the rest of the workflow. A studio editor handles scenes, backgrounds, text, and media, and templates give quick starts. Personalized video at scale lets you generate versions addressed to individual customers, and an API automates production. Interactive avatars can hold real-time conversations on a website or in an app. Custom avatars require consent from the person being replicated.

HeyGen offers a free tier with limited minutes and paid plans scaled by video length and features, with enterprise options. Synthetic avatars can appear slightly stiff, and audiences may expect disclosure when a person is not real. Misuse for impersonation is a serious concern, which is why identity checks for custom avatars matter. Synthesia is the best-known competitor, Colossyan serves training teams, D-ID offers talking photos, and Captions and Hedra provide other styles of avatar video.
