Claap targets teams that want fewer live meetings. You can record your screen and camera as a short video, share a link, and let colleagues watch and respond on their own time. The same tool records live calls on Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams, then stores them with a transcript. Comments and reactions can be attached to specific moments, so feedback stays tied to the part of the video it refers to.

AI turns the recordings into usable text. Claap generates a transcript, a summary, chapters, and action items, and it can write a follow-up message or extract customer feedback. Because every recording is indexed, a team can search across videos to find what was said about a topic, which is useful for sales calls, product feedback, and onboarding. Integrations with CRMs and tools such as Slack, HubSpot, and Notion push summaries where people work.

Claap has a free plan with limits on recordings and storage, and paid plans for more history, AI features, and team controls. It is a smaller product from a French company, and it is most useful for teams that adopt async video as a habit. Loom is the best-known async video tool, Fireflies and Otter focus on meeting notes, and Tella and Descript offer more editing. Recordings of customers need consent and clear data handling.
