Tome became a popular early example of generative AI for presentations. A user described a topic, and Tome produced a complete, designed narrative with text, images, and layout, which could be edited in a flexible, canvas-like format. It was positioned as a more modern, storytelling-focused alternative to PowerPoint, and gained millions of users, with attention from students, founders, and professionals.

The company later decided to retire the presentation product and pivot to something different, a sales-focused application. Users were given a period to export their work before the service closed. The pivot reflected a challenge facing many AI tools: general features are quickly matched by larger products, and a standalone app needs a defensible niche.

Anyone who used Tome can move to other tools. Gamma is the closest AI-first presentation tool, Beautiful.ai and Pitch provide design-focused editors, Canva and Google Slides include AI features, and Plus AI works inside PowerPoint and Slides. Downloaded exports of old Tome presentations may be worth converting to a different format. This entry stays for reference because older articles and guides still recommend Tome.
