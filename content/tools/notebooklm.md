NotebookLM's key design principle is that it only answers from the sources you've provided. Upload a collection of PDFs, Google Docs, or pasted text, and every answer it gives comes with a citation pointing to the exact passage. There are no hallucinations about facts not in your sources because the model is explicitly grounded in what you've given it. For research tasks where accuracy is non-negotiable — academic work, legal review, technical analysis — that constraint is a feature, not a limitation.

The Audio Overview feature is what made NotebookLM go viral in late 2024. It takes your uploaded documents and generates a two-person podcast-style conversation where two AI hosts discuss and explain the material as if doing a deep dive for a general audience. The voices are natural, the conversation is genuinely engaging, and it's become a popular way to absorb dense documents during a commute or workout. You can also guide the discussion by providing focus instructions before it generates.

NotebookLM is free with a Google account and allows up to 50 sources per notebook, with each source up to 500,000 words. This is enough to load an entire textbook, a research literature review, or a company's internal documentation set. The Plus version (part of Google One AI Premium) removes limits and adds features like audio customisation. Researchers, students, journalists, consultants, and legal teams are the primary users — anywhere that reading and synthesising large document volumes is a regular part of the job.
