Hopper started as a flight price prediction app. It analyzes historical and live fare data to forecast whether a ticket will get cheaper or more expensive, and it tells you to buy now or wait. You can watch a route and get notifications when prices drop, and it highlights flexible dates and nearby airports to save money. The same approach extends to hotels and rental cars.

What sets it apart from simple search tools is a set of financial products around travel. Price Freeze lets you lock a fare for a fee while you decide, and cancellation, change, and delay protections give travelers extra flexibility, usually for an additional cost. Hopper also runs a business that provides these travel and fintech features to other companies through partnerships. Predictions rely on machine learning, but they are estimates, and airline pricing can be unpredictable.

The app is free to use, and Hopper earns money from bookings and from fees for add-on products. The extras are optional, and they are worth comparing against simply buying a refundable fare. Booking through a third party can make changes and customer support harder than booking directly with an airline, so keep confirmation details handy. Google Flights has price tracking and insights, KAYAK offers forecasts, Skyscanner is a leading meta-search, and Going focuses on flight deal alerts.
