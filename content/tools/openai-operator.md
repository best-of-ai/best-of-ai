Operator was OpenAI's first widely available computer-using agent. It ran a browser in the cloud and looked at screenshots of web pages, then clicked, typed, and scrolled like a person to carry out a task such as ordering groceries, filling out a form, or booking a restaurant. You could watch it work, take control, and it paused to ask for confirmation before steps such as payments or entering sensitive details.

It showed both the promise and the limits of web agents. It could finish many simple errands, but it was slow, struggled with sites that used captchas or complex layouts, and sometimes needed help. The experience informed OpenAI's later products. Its browsing abilities were folded into ChatGPT's agent mode, and the standalone Operator site was then retired.

Anyone who wants similar abilities today should look at OpenAI's newer agent products inside ChatGPT, such as ChatGPT Work and Dots, or at competitors like Claude's computer-use agents, Manus, and Perplexity's Comet browser. Browser agents continue to raise security issues, including prompt injection from malicious web pages. This entry stays for reference, since many articles and comparisons still mention Operator.
