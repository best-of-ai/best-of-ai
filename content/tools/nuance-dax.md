DAX, short for Dragon Ambient eXperience, was developed by Nuance and later acquired by Microsoft. With patient consent, the system captures the conversation between clinician and patient, uses speech recognition and AI to turn it into a structured medical note in the format the clinician needs, and sends it to the electronic health record for review and signature. The goal is to cut the hours doctors spend on documentation and let them focus on patients.

Microsoft has combined DAX with Nuance's Dragon Medical dictation and added generative AI features into a product called Dragon Copilot. It supports tasks such as drafting notes, referral letters, and after-visit summaries, searching for information in the chart, and automating routine orders. Deep integration with Epic and other records systems is a main selling point for hospitals.

Dragon Copilot is sold to health systems and practices on enterprise contracts with per-user pricing. Ambient notes can include errors or missing details, so clinicians must review them, and privacy and consent requirements apply. Competitors include Abridge, Suki, Ambience Healthcare, Nabla, Heidi Health, and Epic's own built-in AI scribes. Nuance DAX suits healthcare organizations that want a large vendor with strong record integration.
