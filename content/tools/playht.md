PlayHT offered realistic text-to-speech with a large catalog of voices across many languages, plus voice cloning from short audio samples. Creators used its web studio to narrate videos, podcasts, and audiobooks, and developers used its API to add speech to apps and phone systems. Its later conversational models were built to sound natural in dialogue, with emotion and pacing, and the company also developed voice agents under the PlayAI name.

Meta acquired the PlayAI team, and the standalone PlayHT product was shut down. The API was reported to go offline ahead of the final closing date, which disrupted customers who relied on it. Accounts, saved voices, and projects were not carried over, and the website no longer works, so anyone who used it needed to move their work elsewhere.

If you are looking for a replacement, ElevenLabs is the closest in voice quality and features, including cloning and an API. Murf offers a studio aimed at business voiceovers and offered migration help to PlayHT customers. Other options include Cartesia and Resemble AI for developers, Speechify for reading and creator voiceovers, and cloud services such as Amazon Polly, Google Cloud Text-to-Speech, and Azure for large-scale use. Because voice clones are tied to the service that created them, anyone who lost PlayHT voices would need to re-record to recreate them. This entry stays for reference, since older articles and links still mention PlayHT.
