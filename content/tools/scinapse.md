Scinapse is a search tool for scientific literature. It indexes a large body of scholarly papers across disciplines and ranks results with machine learning that considers relevance, citations, and recency. Users search by topic, title, or author, filter by year, journal, and field, and read abstracts with links to the publishers or open-access copies.

Features go beyond a basic list. Author pages show a researcher's publications and citation profile, paper pages show who cited a work and related papers, and collections let you save papers to read later. Alerts notify you about new papers on a topic or from an author. The site was created by a Korean research technology company and is free to use, supported by its broader services for researchers.

Scinapse is a discovery engine, not a reader, so it doesn't summarize or answer questions about papers. Coverage and data quality vary by field, and access to full text depends on the publisher. Google Scholar is the default for breadth, Semantic Scholar adds AI summaries and a strong API, Lens.org links patents and papers, and Scopus and Web of Science are curated subscription databases. Scinapse suits researchers who want an additional free search option.
