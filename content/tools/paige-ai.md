Paige works on a part of medicine where AI could make a real difference: pathology, in which specialists examine tissue slides to diagnose cancer. Slides are scanned into very large digital images, and Paige's algorithms analyze them, flagging areas that look suspicious and helping pathologists find cancer more efficiently and consistently. Its prostate cancer detection software was the first AI pathology tool to receive FDA clearance.

The company grew out of a collaboration with Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center and built a large collection of digitized slides paired with clinical and molecular data. It developed foundation models trained on millions of slides to support a broad range of cancer analysis tasks, and offers a platform for laboratories to deploy AI tools alongside digital pathology systems.

Tempus acquired Paige to strengthen its oncology data and AI capabilities, and Paige's products are expected to continue under Tempus. Pathology AI is used as decision support, and regulatory clearance varies by product and region. Competitors include PathAI, Ibex Medical Analytics, Proscia, Aiforia, and Owkin. Paige is relevant to pathology labs and cancer centers adopting digital workflows.
