Maxar was best known for high-resolution satellite imagery. Its WorldView satellites photographed the Earth in great detail, and governments, news organizations, mapmakers, and businesses used the images for defense and intelligence, disaster response, agriculture, infrastructure monitoring, and navigation. Maxar's imagery supported mapping products and was often seen in news coverage of conflicts and natural disasters.

Its other major business was space systems: building satellites and spacecraft components for communications, observation, and exploration. Machine learning was applied to extract features such as buildings, roads, and vehicles from imagery at scale, and to create 3D models of the Earth. The company was taken private by a private equity firm and later separated its two main businesses under new names.

The imagery and intelligence business now operates as Vantor, which promotes a platform that fuses satellite, aerial, and ground data into a 3D picture of the world. The satellite manufacturing business operates as Lanteris. Anyone searching for Maxar today should look for those successors. Products are sold to governments and enterprises through contracts, not to consumers. Competitors in satellite imagery include Planet Labs, Airbus, BlackSky, and Satellogic, and ArcGIS and Google Earth Engine are common tools for analyzing the data.
