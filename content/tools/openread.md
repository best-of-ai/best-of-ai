OpenRead is built for people who read a lot of research. After you import a paper, an AI assistant can answer questions about it, explain terms, summarize sections, and pull out items like methods, results, and limitations. A structured summary view lays out the main points in a consistent format, which helps when scanning many papers for a review.

The platform extends beyond a single paper. It helps search the literature, organize papers into collections, take notes alongside the text, and generate outlines or review drafts from selected sources. Support for multiple languages makes it useful for researchers who read in a second language, and translation features help with papers published in other languages.

OpenRead has a free tier with limits and paid plans for heavier use. As with every AI reading tool, the summaries can miss nuance or make errors, particularly in technical sections, so quotes and numbers should be verified in the original. Alternatives include Elicit and Consensus for evidence synthesis, SciSpace for paper explanations, Explainpaper for quick clarifications, ChatPDF for general documents, and NotebookLM for source-grounded Q&A. OpenRead suits students and academics who want an all-in-one reading assistant.
