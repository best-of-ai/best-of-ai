Google Translate is the translation tool most people try first. You type or paste text and get a translation in a second, with the language detected automatically. The mobile app adds the features travelers rely on: a camera mode that replaces text on signs and menus, a conversation mode that translates both sides of a spoken exchange, handwriting input, and offline language packs that work without a connection. Chrome can translate whole web pages, and you can upload documents and get a translated copy.

Under the hood, it uses neural machine translation, and Google has been adding its Gemini models to improve fluency and context for common language pairs. Quality is very good for widely spoken languages such as Spanish, French, and German, and weaker for languages with less training data, where it can miss idioms, formality, and nuance. Live translation features also work with headphones for real-time conversation.

The consumer service is free. Developers and businesses can use the Cloud Translation API, which is billed per character, and companies can train custom glossaries or models for specialized vocabulary. For anything that carries legal, medical, or reputational weight, a human translator is still the safer choice, and DeepL is often preferred for European languages because its output reads more naturally. For quick understanding on the go, Google Translate is hard to beat for coverage and price.
