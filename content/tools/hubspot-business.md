HubSpot started as a marketing software company and grew into a full platform for managing the customer relationship from first visit to renewal. It is organized into hubs: Marketing Hub for email, forms, ads, and landing pages, Sales Hub for pipelines and sequences, Service Hub for tickets and help desk, Content Hub for websites and blogs, and Operations Hub for data syncing and automation. All of them read from the same CRM, so a marketer, a sales rep, and a support agent are looking at one record of the customer.

Breeze is the name for HubSpot's AI. The assistant drafts emails, summarizes records and calls, and builds reports from plain-language requests. Breeze agents go further: a customer agent answers support questions from your knowledge base and hands off to a person when it cannot, and a prospecting agent researches accounts and drafts outreach. Breeze Intelligence enriches contact records with company data. Several of the agent features are available only on higher tiers and consume credits, so the cost of heavy AI use is worth checking in advance.

HubSpot offers free tools to start, then Starter, Professional, and Enterprise tiers for each hub. Buying several hubs gets expensive, and the Professional and Enterprise tiers often include setup fees, which makes it a larger commitment than it first looks. It suits small and mid-sized companies that want marketing, sales, and service in one system with a gentler learning curve than Salesforce. Companies with unusual data models or heavy customization needs sometimes outgrow it.
