Yuka makes reading labels simpler. You scan the barcode of a food or personal care product with your phone, and the app returns a score with color coding and an explanation of what affects it. For food, the score considers nutritional quality, additives, and whether the product is organic. For cosmetics, it flags ingredients seen as risky.

The app is built to help shoppers choose. When a product scores poorly, Yuka suggests better alternatives in the same category, and you can keep a history of what you scanned. It does not accept advertising or payments from brands for ratings, and it funds itself through its premium subscription, which adds features like searching without a barcode and offline use.

The basic app is free. Scores simplify complex questions, and some nutrition experts argue that a single number can mislead, especially where the science on an additive is uncertain. Treat it as a guide, not medical advice. Alternatives include Open Food Facts, which is open data, Think Dirty for cosmetics, EWG's Healthy Living app, and Fooducate. Yuka suits shoppers who want quick label checks.
