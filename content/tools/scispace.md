SciSpace, formerly known as Typeset, is built around the problem of reading dense research. Upload a PDF or open a paper from its database, and an AI copilot lets you highlight a passage, a table, or an equation and ask for an explanation in simpler terms. You can also ask questions about the whole paper, request a summary, or get the key methods and results. It is helpful for students and researchers entering an unfamiliar field.

It also covers discovery and writing. A literature review tool searches for papers on a topic and builds a table of findings you can compare side by side. Other tools find related papers, generate citations in different styles, paraphrase text, check for AI-written content, and convert LaTeX or format a manuscript for a journal. Some users connect it to reference managers and use it to organize papers for a project.

SciSpace has a free tier with limited use and paid plans that raise the limits on chat, literature reviews, and downloads. As with any AI summary, answers can be wrong or simplified, so conclusions should be checked against the paper before they are cited. The breadth of tools means some are stronger than others. Elicit and Consensus overlap on evidence synthesis, Semantic Scholar offers free paper search, and ChatGPT or Claude can answer questions about a PDF without a specialist tool.
