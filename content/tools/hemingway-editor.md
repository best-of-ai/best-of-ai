Hemingway Editor takes its name from the writer known for short sentences. You paste or write text, and the app colors problems: long, dense sentences in yellow and red, adverbs in blue, passive voice in green, and simpler alternatives for complicated words in purple. A grade level score estimates how hard the writing is to read, and the aim is to bring it to a level most readers can follow easily.

The editor works as a mirror, not a rewriter, which helps writers learn to spot issues themselves. The free web version has no signup, and the desktop app adds formatting, publishing to WordPress or Medium, and offline use, sold as a one-time purchase. A newer paid tier uses AI to suggest rewrites, fix grammar, and change tone, bringing it closer to tools like Grammarly.

The advice is based on general rules that do not suit every kind of writing: academic, legal, or literary prose may reasonably use longer sentences and passive voice. The readability score is crude. Alternatives include Grammarly, ProWritingAid, LanguageTool, Wordtune, and plain use of an AI assistant for editing. Hemingway suits bloggers, marketers, and students who want to write more clearly.
