Benchling brings the scattered tasks of a research lab into one cloud system. Scientists use the notebook to record experiments, design DNA and protein sequences with molecular biology tools, and register the samples and materials they create, so everything is traceable. Teams track workflows, share results, and connect lab instruments, which replaces a mix of spreadsheets, paper notebooks, and separate databases. It is common at startups and large biopharma companies.

The platform has grown from a notebook into a broader R&D system. Products cover automation, a developer platform for custom integrations, an ecosystem of partner tools, and validated cloud environments for regulated work. On the AI side, Benchling offers agent teams that capture and analyze data and hand work between steps, plus access to scientific models for tasks such as protein structure prediction and sequence generation.

Academics can use Benchling for free, and companies pay for team and enterprise plans on custom contracts. Setup for a large organization takes real effort, because lab processes must be modeled in the system. Results from AI models are predictions that still need lab testing. Competitors and alternatives include Dotmatics, Genedata, Scispot, and LabArchives, while many labs still rely on paper notebooks. Benchling's strength is a modern interface and strong molecular biology features.
