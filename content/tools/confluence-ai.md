Confluence is where many teams keep their documentation. It organizes pages into spaces, with templates for meeting notes, product requirements, runbooks, project plans, and knowledge bases. Pages support rich formatting, comments, and embedded content from Jira and other tools. Version history and permissions help control who can edit and read what. Teams in engineering, product, IT, and operations use it as a shared source of truth.

AI features come through Atlassian's Rovo. Inside Confluence, you can summarize a long page, draft content from a short prompt, improve or translate writing, and ask questions in natural language and get answers drawn from the knowledge in your spaces. Rovo search reaches across Confluence, Jira, and connected tools like Google Drive and Slack, and agents can take on tasks such as turning a meeting note into action items.

Confluence has a free plan for small teams and paid plans that add storage, permissions, and administration, with enterprise options. AI features are included with paid tiers, and some usage limits apply. Search results depend on the quality of existing documentation: an outdated wiki yields outdated answers. Notion is a popular, more flexible alternative, Slab and Document360 focus on knowledge bases, and Google Docs with Drive suits lighter needs. Confluence's strength is its tie to Jira and the Atlassian ecosystem.
