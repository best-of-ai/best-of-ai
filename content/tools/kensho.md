Kensho began as a startup applying machine learning to financial markets and was acquired by S&P Global, where it operates as the company's AI innovation hub. Its products address practical data problems in finance. Kensho Link matches records between data sets, such as linking company names across different sources, Kensho Classify tags documents by category, and Kensho Extract pulls data out of PDFs and filings.

More recent work connects financial data to generative AI. Kensho develops tools that allow AI assistants and agents to access S&P Global's data reliably, with grounding that provides sources for answers, plus language models and benchmarks for finance. Its technology supports S&P products and is available to clients who want to build their own applications.

Kensho's products are sold to institutional customers, often through S&P Global, with custom pricing. It's a specialist toolkit rather than a consumer product. Competitors and alternatives include FactSet, Bloomberg's AI tools, AlphaSense, Moody's, LSEG, and the data platforms of Snowflake and Databricks. Kensho is relevant for banks, asset managers, and corporations that need to clean, link, and extract financial data at scale.
