Ready Player Me offered a way to carry one avatar across many apps. A user took a selfie or built a character in the creator, customized clothing and hair, and received a 3D avatar that worked in a growing network of games, social platforms, and VR worlds. For developers, an SDK and web tools made it easy to add avatars without building a character creator from scratch, and it supported engines such as Unity and Unreal.

The service served a market of virtual worlds, metaverse experiments, and games that wanted personalization without large art budgets. Over time, it added support for outfits, custom assets, and full-body avatars, and it was integrated by hundreds of apps. Its technology also appealed to companies thinking about identity across digital spaces.

Netflix acquired Ready Player Me, apparently for use in its gaming efforts, and the public avatar service was closed. Users could not export their avatars after the shutdown, and games that depended on the SDK lost avatar creation. Developers needing alternatives can look at Genies, Wolf3D, Avaturn, VRoid Studio, and Character Creator, or MetaHuman for realistic characters in Unreal. This entry stays for reference, since older documentation and tutorials still mention Ready Player Me.
