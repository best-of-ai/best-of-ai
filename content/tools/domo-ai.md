Domo aims to put an organization's data in one place. It connects to hundreds of sources, from databases and spreadsheets to marketing and finance platforms, and lets users clean and combine data and then build dashboards and reports. Business users can view metrics on the web or mobile and set alerts, and developers can build custom apps on top of the data.

AI is built through the product. Users can ask questions in natural language and receive charts and answers, models can forecast and detect anomalies, and AI agents and workflows can automate tasks, such as summarizing reports or triggering actions when numbers change. Domo also lets companies connect their own models, and offers governance features for data access.

Domo is paid, with consumption-based pricing and custom contracts, and a trial for evaluation. It competes in a crowded market where Power BI is often cheaper for Microsoft users and Tableau is known for visualization. Other alternatives include Looker, ThoughtSpot, Qlik, and Sigma. Domo suits mid-sized and large companies that want an all-in-one data and dashboard platform.
