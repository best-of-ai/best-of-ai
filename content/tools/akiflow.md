Akiflow is built for people whose tasks are scattered across many apps. A task might arrive as an email, a Slack message, a Jira ticket, or an Asana item, and Akiflow collects them in a universal inbox so you can decide what to do and when. You drag tasks onto a calendar to block out time for them, which turns a long to-do list into a realistic day. Keyboard shortcuts and a command bar make it fast to use.

The planning tools include a synced calendar, recurring tasks, a daily planning ritual, and auto-scheduling that finds time for tasks. Aki, the AI assistant, takes voice commands, gives a daily briefing, and sends reminders, helping you keep to the plan. Integrations cover calendars, email, project management tools, and note apps, so tasks can be created and updated in both directions.

There is a free trial but no permanent free plan, and the subscription costs more than basic to-do apps. It makes the most sense for founders, managers, and busy professionals who juggle many sources of work and want a single planning surface. If your tasks live in one tool, it adds little. Sunsama, Motion, and Reclaim.ai overlap with Akiflow on planning and scheduling, while Todoist and TickTick are simpler task lists.
