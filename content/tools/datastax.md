DataStax grew up around Apache Cassandra, a database designed for large, always-on applications. Its Astra DB service runs Cassandra in the cloud without servers to manage, and adds vector search so developers can store embeddings and find similar items by meaning. That makes it a base for retrieval-augmented generation, recommendations, and chat features.

The company also supports the tools around AI development. Langflow is a visual builder for agent and retrieval workflows, and its data APIs let developers use document or table models. Pairing a scalable database with AI tools gives teams a path from prototype to large production workloads.

IBM announced plans to acquire DataStax to strengthen its watsonx products, so packaging may shift over time. Astra DB has a free tier and usage-based pricing, and Cassandra itself remains open source. Alternatives include Pinecone, Weaviate, Qdrant, MongoDB Atlas Vector Search, and pgvector in Postgres. DataStax suits teams that need vector search at large scale.
