AIVA, short for Artificial Intelligence Virtual Artist, was built to compose music, with an early emphasis on classical and cinematic styles. You pick a style from a library of hundreds, set the length and key, and it generates a track. You can also upload an audio or MIDI file as an influence, or define your own chord progression. Video creators, game developers, podcasters, and advertisers use it to get background music without hiring a composer.

An editor lets you change the result instead of accepting what it produces. You can alter instruments, adjust the arrangement, and export in audio or MIDI so the music can be refined in a digital audio workstation. Custom style models can be trained from your own references, which helps produce a consistent sound for a brand or game.

The licensing tiers matter. The free plan produces music that AIVA owns the copyright to, with non-commercial use only. The Standard tier allows limited monetization, mostly on social platforms, and the Pro tier gives you ownership of the copyright with full commercial rights. Compared with text-prompt generators like Suno and Udio, AIVA is more instrument-focused and gives more control over the composition, but it is less suited to music with vocals. Soundraw and Mubert are other background-music tools.
