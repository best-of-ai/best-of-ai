Asana's interface is designed for visibility — every project has a timeline, a board, a list view, and a calendar view, all showing the same underlying task data from different angles. Project managers use the timeline to see dependency chains and spot bottlenecks. Individual contributors use the list view to see what's due and when. Executives use portfolio views to see the status of multiple projects without digging into individual task boards.

Asana AI (part of the platform since 2024) adds an intelligent layer to this visibility. It can summarise a project's status in a single paragraph from the underlying task and comment data. It flags tasks that are marked as on-time but have warning signs — no recent activity, missing dependencies, overdue subtasks — and surfaces them proactively rather than waiting for the deadline to arrive. It can draft project kickoff plans from a goal statement, and auto-generate milestones from a project description.

Asana's free plan covers unlimited tasks and projects for teams of up to 15 with basic features. Premium at $10.99/user/month adds timeline, workflow automations, and reporting. Business at $24.99/user/month unlocks portfolios, goals, and workload management for teams tracking progress across multiple streams. The AI features are included in Premium and above. Asana is most commonly used by marketing, operations, and cross-functional teams that coordinate large projects with many stakeholders — its structure and clarity features are what separate it from lighter task managers.
