DoNotPay started by helping people contest parking tickets and grew into an app for dealing with the small annoyances of consumer life. Its tools help users write letters and fill out forms to dispute charges, cancel unwanted subscriptions, request refunds, and complain to companies and agencies. The idea is that many of these tasks are tedious and intimidating, and a template-driven assistant can save people time and money.

The company's marketing as the world's first robot lawyer caused trouble. The Federal Trade Commission alleged that the service did not perform as well as a human lawyer and did not have the testing to back its claims. A final order barred DoNotPay from making that kind of claim without evidence and required it to pay a fine and notify affected customers. The case is often cited as a warning about overstating what AI can do in legal matters.

DoNotPay is a paid subscription. It is best used for simple consumer tasks where a form letter or a cancellation request is enough, and not for situations where legal rights are at stake, such as a lawsuit, an eviction, or an immigration case. Results are not guaranteed, and the user is responsible for the documents they send. Free options include writing your own letter with ChatGPT or Claude, and nonprofit legal aid groups help with serious problems. Subscription management apps like Rocket Money overlap on cancellations.
