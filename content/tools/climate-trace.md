Climate TRACE exists because official emissions reporting is slow and incomplete. The coalition combines satellite images, sensor data, and public records and applies machine learning to estimate emissions from individual sources: power plants, steel mills, oil and gas fields, ships, aircraft, cement plants, farms, and more. The result is a global inventory that can be searched by country, sector, or even a single facility, updated more frequently than government reports.

The data serves several users. Governments and companies can compare their reported emissions against independent estimates. Researchers, journalists, and campaigners use the map to find large sources and track changes over time, and investors use it to assess climate risk. Because the methods and data are open, others can check how an estimate was made, although estimates carry uncertainty, particularly for sources with little direct observation.

The platform and downloads are free. Climate TRACE is a coalition of nonprofits, universities, and technology companies, supported by philanthropic funding, with Al Gore among its founders. Its figures are modeled estimates, not direct measurements, so they should be treated as approximations with error ranges. Alternatives and complements include national greenhouse gas inventories, the EDGAR database, and commercial emissions-monitoring services such as GHGSat, which measures methane from space.
