Scale AI built its business on a simple observation: AI models need huge amounts of well-labeled data, and quality control at scale is hard. It combines software with a large network of human contributors and experts to annotate images, video, text, and sensor data. Early customers included self-driving car developers, and the company grew by supplying training data to the biggest AI labs.

Today much of its work involves large language models. Scale provides human feedback data used in reinforcement learning, expert-written examples for fine-tuning, and evaluations that test models for accuracy and safety. Its tools help companies manage data pipelines, and a generative AI platform lets enterprises customize models on their own data. Public-sector work includes AI systems for defense and government agencies, and the company runs public leaderboards that rank models on hard tests.

Scale sells to organizations through custom contracts, not to individuals, and prices are negotiated. Meta took a large minority stake and hired Scale's founder, after which some customers reduced their work with the company. Scale says it stays independent. The business also faces criticism over working conditions and pay for the contractors who do labeling. Surge AI, Labelbox, Appen, and Mercor are among the competitors, and some AI labs build data operations in-house.
