Luma AI's 3D capture process is remarkable for how simple it is. Walk around an object or a room with an iPhone, recording video as you go — the more angles you cover, the more complete the reconstruction. Upload the video to Luma's cloud processor, and within minutes you receive an interactive 3D scene you can embed on a website, view in AR, export as a 3D model, or use in production pipelines. The NeRF (neural radiance field) and Gaussian splatting techniques it uses produce photorealistic reconstructions that look nothing like traditional 3D scans.

The output quality is particularly strong for reflective, translucent, or complex-textured objects that defeat traditional structured-light or stereo-vision scanning approaches. Product photographers use it to create 360-degree interactive product views that outperform photographic turntables in perceived quality. Architects and interior designers use it to capture spaces for virtual walkthroughs. VFX studios use it to digitise physical assets for use in productions — capturing a specific car, location, or prop rather than modelling it from scratch.

Luma AI is available as a free iOS app for capture, with free hosting of resulting 3D scenes. Pro features — higher resolution processing, API access, private scenes, and commercial usage rights — require a paid plan starting at $29.99/month. API access lets developers integrate Luma captures into real estate platforms, e-commerce product pages, and AR applications. This is a niche tool in the best sense: if your work involves 3D capture of real-world objects and spaces, Luma produces better results faster and more cheaply than the alternatives that existed before NeRF-based capture became practical.
