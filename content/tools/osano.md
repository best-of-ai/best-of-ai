Osano began with cookie consent, the banner that asks visitors what tracking they accept. Its consent manager scans a site, categorizes cookies and scripts, shows region-appropriate banners, and records choices, which helps companies comply with laws such as GDPR and CCPA. Websites install it with a small piece of code, and it can block scripts until a visitor agrees.

The platform grew to cover the wider privacy program. Tools handle data subject access requests, data mapping, and assessments, and a vendor monitoring service tracks the privacy practices of third parties and flags risks. Osano also offers privacy rating information and a catalog of vendor reports. Features for managing AI use and policies are being added as regulation develops.

Osano is paid, with plans scaled by the size of the site portfolio and the modules selected, and a free tier for basic consent on small sites. It is simpler and cheaper than enterprise platforms like OneTrust but covers fewer advanced governance functions. Legal compliance depends on correct configuration and on understanding each law, so a legal review is still wise. Competitors include OneTrust, TrustArc, Cookiebot from Usercentrics, Termly, and Iubenda. It suits growing organizations that want privacy basics handled.
