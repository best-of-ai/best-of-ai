Fitbod creates workout plans without a human coach. At setup, you give your fitness level, goals, available equipment, and schedule, and it generates a session for each day. The routine adapts as you log sets, reps, and weights: it suggests weights and volume based on past performance, and it tracks which muscle groups have recovered, so you do not train the same area too hard on consecutive days. Videos demonstrate each exercise.

The app works for different settings, whether a full gym, a home setup with dumbbells, or a hotel room with no equipment. You can swap exercises you dislike or cannot do, and progress charts show strength gains and training volume. It connects to Apple Watch and Apple Health, which allows logging from your wrist. The machine learning behind it is based on large amounts of workout data and principles of progressive overload.

Fitbod uses a subscription after a short trial. It is good for people who want structure and variety, but it is not a replacement for an in-person coach who can correct form, adjust for injuries, or build a program for competition. Plans sometimes feel generic, and users with medical concerns should consult a professional. Strong, Hevy, and JEFIT are popular logging apps, Future and Caliber offer human coaching, and Whoop and Peloton cover different parts of the fitness market.
