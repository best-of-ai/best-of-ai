Amwell provides the technology behind a lot of virtual care. Hospitals and health systems use it to run telehealth programs for their own patients, health plans offer it as a benefit, and employers use it to give workers access to remote visits. Patients can see a clinician by video for urgent care, primary care, behavioral health, and specialty care, with scheduling, intake, and visit tools in one platform.

It also offers services around the visit. These include a hospital-grade platform for remote consultations within hospitals, digital programs for chronic conditions, and a network of clinicians for companies that don't have their own. AI features help with intake, triage, and documentation, and the platform integrates with electronic health records. Its ability to be customized for each client is a selling point.

Amwell is a public company that sells to organizations rather than individuals, with pricing tied to contracts and visit volumes, though patients can use its direct service through some channels. Telehealth rules vary by state, and virtual visits are not suitable for emergencies. Competitors include Teladoc Health, MDLIVE from Evernorth, Doctor on Demand, which is part of Included Health, and the telehealth tools built into electronic health record systems. Amwell suits health organizations building virtual care programs.
