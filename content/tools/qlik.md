Qlik is known for its associative engine, which treats all the data in an app as connected. When you click a value in a chart, everything else updates to show related data, and values that are unrelated are shown as excluded, which helps users discover connections they did not think to query. This differs from tools that require predefined queries and has been a selling point for business users exploring data.

The platform has grown beyond dashboards. Qlik Cloud offers data integration, data quality, and pipelines, including capabilities from its acquisition of Talend, along with automation, alerting, and embedded analytics. AI features include AutoML for building predictive models without code, natural language questions through an assistant, and agentic tools that answer questions on both structured and unstructured data.

Qlik is sold by subscription, with pricing by users and capacity, and runs in the cloud and in on-premises versions. Competitors include Microsoft Power BI, which is cheaper for many organizations, Tableau, Looker, ThoughtSpot, and Domo. Success depends on the quality of the data model and governance. Qlik suits enterprises that want exploratory analytics with data integration in one vendor.
