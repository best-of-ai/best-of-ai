TestFit speeds up the feasibility stage of real estate development. A user draws a site boundary and sets rules such as unit mix, parking ratios, setbacks, and building types, and TestFit generates layouts that fit, updating in real time as constraints change. It reports metrics like unit counts, parking, rentable area, and efficiency, which let developers judge whether a site works before investing heavily.

It handles multiple product types, including apartment buildings, townhomes, self-storage, hotels, and parking structures, and exports to common design tools such as Revit and CAD for the next stage. Cost and revenue features help assess returns, and collaboration tools let teams share scenarios. Generative algorithms explore many arrangements to find efficient solutions.

TestFit is sold by subscription to developers, architects, and brokers, and costs are considerable compared with simple tools. It produces conceptual studies, not construction drawings, and local code details still need professional review. Alternatives include Autodesk Forma, Finch3D, Archistar, Hypar, and manual work in SketchUp or Revit. TestFit suits real estate developers who need quick feasibility checks.
