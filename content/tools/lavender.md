Lavender sits in your email composer and gives feedback in real time. As you write a cold email, it gives a score and highlights problems: sentences that are too long, language that sounds salesy, a missing clear ask, or a message that is too focused on you instead of the recipient. It suggests edits and shows how changes affect the score, acting like a coach looking over your shoulder.

Personalization is a second pillar. Lavender can pull in information about the recipient, such as their role, company news, and social posts, and suggest personalized opening lines and talking points. A Chrome extension works in Gmail and Outlook and integrates with sales engagement tools, so it fits existing workflows. Managers can see team-wide analytics and coach reps on writing.

Lavender has a free tier with limited features and paid plans for individuals and teams. Its scoring is based on patterns from large numbers of emails and is a guide, not a guarantee of replies. Overusing templated personalization can sound artificial. Competitors and alternatives include Apollo's AI tools, Lemlist, Regie.ai, and general assistants like ChatGPT and Claude. Lavender suits sales reps who send many emails and want help improving them.
