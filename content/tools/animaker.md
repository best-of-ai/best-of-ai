Animaker lets people without animation skills make animated videos. You choose a template or start from scratch, add characters, props, backgrounds, and text, and arrange them on a timeline. A character builder customizes avatars with facial expressions and actions, and ready-made assets cover business, education, and storytelling scenes. It also supports whiteboard-style videos, infographic videos, and live-action editing, with exports up to 4K.

AI tools shorten the production steps. Text-to-animation turns a script into a rough animated scene, voiceover tools generate narration in many voices and languages, and translation and auto-subtitle features help localize videos. Teams use it for training videos, product explainers, school lessons, social media clips, and internal communications, where hiring an animation studio would be too costly.

Animaker has a free plan with watermarks and limited exports, and paid tiers that remove limits and add resolution, assets, and team features. The animation style can feel template-driven and recognizable, so it suits explainers more than polished brand films. Compared with Vyond, which targets corporate training, Animaker is cheaper and broader. Canva and Adobe Express offer simpler video options, and tools like Powtoon overlap closely for presentations and explainers.
