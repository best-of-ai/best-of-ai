Petcube sells connected cameras for people who want to check on pets while away. A camera with a wide-angle view streams video to a phone app, and two-way audio lets owners talk to the pet. Models differ in features: some include a built-in laser toy for play, some toss treats on command, and some are designed for indoor or outdoor use. Setup is through Wi-Fi and a smartphone.

Software adds intelligence. AI can detect when a pet is barking, moving, or when a person is in view, and send alerts, and a subscription provides cloud recording, event history, and access to veterinarians by video chat. Pet owners use it to keep an eye on anxious dogs, monitor cats, and share live views with family members.

Petcube is hardware plus an optional subscription. Cameras raise privacy considerations since they record in your home, so secure your account with strong passwords and two-factor authentication. Alternatives include Furbo, Wyze Cam, Eufy, Arlo, and general home cameras, which can monitor pets but lack treat-tossing and pet-specific features. Petcube suits pet owners who want to check in and interact remotely.
