Notion's block-based editor is what makes it so adaptable. Each piece of content — a paragraph, a table, a to-do list, an embedded video, a database view — is a block you can move, embed inside other pages, or link to from anywhere in your workspace. This means Notion can serve as a project management tool, a company wiki, a personal note system, a CRM, a content calendar, or all of these simultaneously. Teams typically take a few weeks to set it up the way they want, after which it becomes hard to imagine working without it.

Notion AI ($8/member/month add-on) embeds generative AI throughout the workspace. You can ask it to draft a document, rewrite a section in a different tone, translate a page, or summarise a long meeting note — without switching tools. The Q&A feature searches across your entire workspace to answer questions like "what did we decide about the pricing strategy last quarter?" with citations to the specific pages. For teams with large Notion workspaces, this turns a knowledge base into a conversational resource.

The free plan covers personal use with limited blocks and collaborators. Plus at $10/member/month removes block limits and adds version history. Business at $15/member/month adds SAML SSO and advanced permission controls. Enterprise adds audit logs, dedicated customer success, and compliance features. Notion is free for individuals, and the free plan is genuinely useful — the upgrade trigger is typically team size and the need for more sophisticated collaboration features rather than hitting hard usage limits.
