Airbyte solves the data movement problem. Data teams use it to pull information from applications, databases, and APIs, such as Salesforce, Postgres, Stripe, and Google Ads, and load it into destinations such as Snowflake, BigQuery, and Databricks, on a schedule. Hundreds of pre-built connectors cover common sources, and a low-code builder, with AI assistance, lets teams create connectors for others. Because it is open source, the connector catalog grows with community contributions.

Deployment is flexible. Airbyte Cloud is a managed service, and open-source and enterprise versions can run on a company's own infrastructure, which suits organizations with strict data rules. It supports incremental syncs, change data capture for databases, and integrations with transformation tools such as dbt and orchestration tools such as Airflow. Newer features aim to give AI agents access to live data from business systems.

Airbyte has a free open-source version and paid cloud and enterprise plans, with pricing by data volume or capacity. Running it at scale takes operational effort, and connector quality varies by source. Fivetran is the leading managed competitor and is more polished and costlier, Stitch and Hevo are other options, and Meltano and dlt are open-source alternatives. Airbyte suits teams that want flexibility and control over data pipelines.
