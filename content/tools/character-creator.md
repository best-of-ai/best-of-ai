Character Creator lets artists build 3D characters without modeling every detail. You start with a base body, adjust shape sliders for face and body, and apply skin, hair, clothing, and accessories from a library. A rigged skeleton and facial controls come ready to animate, so a character can be used right away in games, films, and animations. Pipelines to Unreal Engine, Unity, Blender, Maya, and other tools make it practical in production.

AI tools speed up the realistic parts. Headshot and similar plugins generate a 3D head from a photo, SkinGen helps create detailed skin textures, and other modules handle hair, facial expressions, and body shape. Motion capture and animation tools in Reallusion's iClone can drive the characters, and a content marketplace offers outfits and props. VTubers, indie game developers, and animation studios use it to create avatars quickly.

The software is paid and sold as a perpetual license with add-ons, and there is a trial. Results are good for stylized and realistic humans, but advanced hero characters in high-end productions often need further work in sculpting and texturing tools. MetaHuman Creator in Unreal Engine is a major free alternative for realistic humans in that engine, Daz Studio offers a large library, and Ready Player Me provides web avatars. Blender can do the same work with more manual effort.
