Ideogram became known for solving a problem that frustrated early image generators: spelling. Ask most models for a poster that says "Grand Opening" and you get distorted letters, while Ideogram's models usually produce clean, legible type in a chosen style. That makes it a practical choice for posters, book covers, t-shirt designs, logos, memes, and social media graphics, where words are part of the image.

It also performs well on general image generation, with strong prompt following across photorealistic, illustrated, and graphic design styles. Beyond generating from scratch, the apps include tools to remove backgrounds, erase objects, resize images for ads, and edit text layers. Teams can use private generation, shared libraries, and custom models, and a developer API provides the same models in other products.

Ideogram has a free tier with a limited number of generations and paid plans that raise the quota and add private generations and commercial rights. Free images may be public by default, so check the privacy setting if the work is sensitive. Like all generators, it can still get hands, complex scenes, and long passages of text wrong, so expect to regenerate. Midjourney tends to lead on artistic polish, and FLUX, OpenAI's image models, and Adobe Firefly are the other main choices.
