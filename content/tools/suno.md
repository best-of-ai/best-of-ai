Suno made AI music accessible. You describe the song you want, such as an upbeat pop track about a road trip, or paste your own lyrics, and Suno produces complete songs with singing and instrumentation in under a minute. Users can choose styles and genres, extend and edit songs, create covers, and make instrumentals. It has been used for demos, background music, parody, and just for fun by people with no musical training.

The company has had a complicated relationship with the music industry. Major record labels sued Suno and Udio over their training data, and some of those disputes have since led to licensing agreements. As part of one such settlement, Suno committed to move to models built on licensed material and to introduce limits on downloads, with free users restricted from downloading songs. Other label cases remain unresolved, so terms may continue to change.

Suno offers a free tier with limited daily credits for non-commercial use and paid plans with more credits and commercial rights for songs made during the subscription. Check the current terms before using songs commercially, since rights depend on your plan. Quality is impressive but can be inconsistent, with occasional artifacts and lyrics that drift. Udio is the closest competitor, Stability's Stable Audio and Google's music tools are other options, and Soundraw suits background music.
