AlphaFold tackled a problem that biologists worked on for fifty years: predicting how a chain of amino acids folds into a three-dimensional protein. Shape determines function, so knowing the structure helps researchers understand diseases, design drugs, and engineer enzymes. Before AlphaFold, determining a structure took expensive experiments such as X-ray crystallography that could take months or years. AlphaFold predicts many structures with accuracy that is often close to experimental results.

The AlphaFold Protein Structure Database, run with the European Bioinformatics Institute, offers predicted structures for a vast number of proteins, free for researchers worldwide. Later versions predict how proteins interact with other molecules such as DNA, RNA, and small drug-like compounds. The work earned its creators a Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and the code and models have been made available for research use.

AlphaFold is free to use for research through the database and open code, with some limits on commercial use of the newest models. Predictions are models, not experiments, and confidence varies across a protein, especially for flexible or disordered regions. Related tools include RoseTTAFold, ESMFold, Boltz, and Isomorphic Labs' drug discovery work. AlphaFold is a foundational tool for structural biology, and appears in many scientific platforms.
