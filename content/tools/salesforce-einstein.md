Einstein brought AI to Salesforce customers well before generative AI became popular. Predictive features score leads and opportunities, forecast revenue, recommend next best actions, and flag at-risk accounts, using each company's own CRM data. Einstein Discovery finds patterns and suggests what drives outcomes, and bots and analytics features extend the same ideas to service and marketing.

With generative AI, Einstein has expanded to write emails, summarize records and cases, and generate service replies, grounded in customer data and protected by the Einstein Trust Layer, which masks sensitive information and prevents customer data from being used to train outside models. The conversational assistant evolved into Agentforce, which builds autonomous agents on the same foundation, so Einstein now refers to a mix of older predictive features and the generative AI infrastructure.

Einstein capabilities are included in some Salesforce editions and sold as add-ons or by consumption in others, which can make pricing hard to follow. They require good CRM data to work well. Competitors include Microsoft Dynamics 365 Copilot, HubSpot Breeze, Zoho Zia, and ServiceNow's AI. Salesforce Einstein suits companies already on Salesforce who want built-in predictions and generative features.
