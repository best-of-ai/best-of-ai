Capture One began as software for tethered shooting, where a camera connects to a computer and photos appear on screen immediately, and it's still a leader there. Studio and commercial photographers like its fast, reliable tethering, strong RAW processing, and color tools, including detailed color editing, skin tone adjustment, and support for many camera profiles. It offers a catalog system and sessions for organizing shoots.

Editing features include layers and masks, advanced color grading, and tools for sharpening and noise reduction. AI is used for masking, such as selecting subjects and backgrounds, and for other tasks that speed up workflow. A mobile app and live collaboration features let clients and teams review images during a shoot, and it works across Mac and Windows.

Capture One is sold by subscription or perpetual license, at a higher price than some competitors, with trials available. It has a learning curve and a different layout from Lightroom, which many photographers know. Alternatives include Adobe Lightroom, which is stronger for general organization and mobile editing, DxO PhotoLab, ON1 Photo RAW, and Luminar Neo. Capture One suits professional photographers who prioritize color quality and tethering.
