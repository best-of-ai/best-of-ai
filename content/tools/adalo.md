Adalo lets people without programming skills build mobile apps. You design screens with a drag-and-drop editor, add components such as lists, forms, and buttons, set up a database for your data, and define what happens when users interact. It handles user accounts, payments, and push notifications, and produces apps that can be published to the Apple App Store and Google Play, as well as web apps.

AI features speed up the first draft: you can describe an app and have the builder generate screens and data structures, then refine them manually. A marketplace of components and integrations extends functionality, and connections to external databases and APIs allow more complex apps. Typical projects include community directories, booking apps, marketplaces, internal tools, and simple social apps.

Adalo has a free plan for experimenting and paid plans that add publishing, more records, and custom features. No-code apps can hit limits on performance and customization as they grow, and publishing to app stores has its own review and cost requirements. FlutterFlow offers more flexibility and exports code, Bubble is stronger for complex web apps, Glide builds apps from spreadsheets, and Softr and Thunkable are other options. Adalo suits founders prototyping mobile apps.
