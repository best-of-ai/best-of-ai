Google Maps is the default map for much of the world. It provides turn-by-turn navigation with live traffic and rerouting, public transit schedules, walking and cycling directions, and detailed maps with business hours, photos, reviews, and popular times. Street View lets you look at places at ground level, Live View uses your phone's camera to show directions as arrows in the real world, and offline maps work without a connection.

Machine learning drives much of what it does. It predicts travel times from live and historical traffic, suggests more fuel-efficient routes, estimates how busy a place is, and keeps business information current by analyzing imagery and user contributions. Newer Gemini-powered features let you ask conversational questions, such as finding a restaurant that fits several requirements, and get answers drawn from reviews and place data. Immersive views combine imagery to preview routes and neighborhoods.

The consumer app is free. Businesses can manage a free Business Profile, and developers pay for the Maps Platform APIs, which power the maps in countless other apps. The service collects location data, which Google lets users manage through settings and history controls. Apple Maps is the main competitor on iPhones, Waze focuses on live community driving reports, and Mapbox, HERE, and OpenStreetMap-based apps serve developers and users who prefer alternatives.
