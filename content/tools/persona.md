Persona lets companies verify who their customers and business partners are. A business builds a flow, choosing which checks to run: collecting and verifying a government ID, matching a selfie to the photo with liveness detection, checking databases and watchlists, verifying a business and its owners, or confirming an address. The flow can adapt based on risk, asking for more evidence only when something looks suspicious.

Configurability sets it apart. Companies can design the user experience, set rules for approvals, and route uncertain cases to human reviewers in a case management tool. Additional products detect fraud patterns across accounts, run ongoing monitoring, and verify employees and workforce identities. APIs and no-code builders connect the flows to existing systems, and the platform covers documents from many countries.

Persona is sold to businesses, with pricing based on the number of verifications and products used, and a path for startups to begin. Biometric checks carry privacy duties and legal requirements, especially around consent and storage in different regions. Accuracy and fairness across demographics should be tested. Competitors include Jumio, Onfido, Veriff, Socure, Sumsub, and Stripe Identity. It's valued for flexibility and developer-friendly tools.
