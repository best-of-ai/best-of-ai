Pachama works on the trust problem in carbon markets. Credits from forest projects are only valuable if the forest is actually protected or restored, and checking this is hard. Pachama's models analyze satellite and lidar data to estimate carbon stored in forests and to detect deforestation or degradation over time.

Buyers use its ratings and monitoring to choose credits and track them after purchase, and project developers use the data to support their claims. The company has also worked on tools for measuring forest carbon more cheaply than field surveys alone.

Reports indicate Carbon Direct acquired Pachama, so its products may be folded into that company's offerings. Carbon credit markets remain contested, with debate about the accuracy of measurements and the effect of credits. Alternatives include Sylvera, BeZero Carbon, Renoster, and Verra's own standards. Pachama suits organizations assessing nature-based carbon projects.
