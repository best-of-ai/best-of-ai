Stable Diffusion is the only major image generation model you can run entirely on your own hardware. A mid-range GPU with 8GB of VRAM is enough to generate images locally, which means your prompts, your images, and your workflow stay completely private. The weights are published under a permissive licence, and the community has spent three years building tooling around them — AUTOMATIC1111 and ComfyUI are the most popular local interfaces, offering fine-grained control over every generation parameter.

The open-weight nature of Stable Diffusion has produced an ecosystem that no proprietary model can match in breadth. Community sites like Civitai host thousands of fine-tuned models specialised for specific styles: photorealism, anime, architectural visualisation, product renders, watercolour illustration. Techniques like ControlNet let you use a sketch, pose, or depth map to control the composition of generated images — something that isn't possible through the interfaces of closed models. LoRA fine-tuning lets studios train the model on their own visual style or product photography in hours.

Stable Diffusion is free in the sense that the model weights are free to download. Running it locally requires a GPU; cloud inference through services like DreamStudio or Leonardo AI costs per image. The practical choice is between local control (free, private, full access to the ecosystem) and cloud convenience (pay-per-image, no setup). For developers building image generation into products, Stable Diffusion's API accessibility via Stability AI or third-party providers makes it the most flexible foundation available.
