Coze lets non-programmers build conversational agents. In its editor, you describe the bot's role and behavior, choose a language model, and attach tools: plugins for web search, image generation, and other services, plus knowledge bases that hold your documents. Visual workflows chain steps together for more complex tasks, and memory features let a bot recall details about users. A store lets creators share and discover bots built by others.

Publishing is flexible. A finished agent can be deployed to messaging apps and chat platforms, embedded on a website, or exposed through an API, which makes it practical for customer support, community bots, personal assistants, and internal tools. ByteDance has also released parts of the platform as open source for those who want to run it themselves, and offers separate editions for different regions.

Coze is free to start, with paid plans for higher usage and advanced model access. Because it is operated by ByteDance, some organizations weigh data governance and regional availability before using it with sensitive information. The quality of a bot depends on how well you write its instructions and prepare its knowledge. Dify and Botpress are open-source rivals, OpenAI's GPTs and Agent Builder are close alternatives, and Zapier Agents and n8n focus on automation.
