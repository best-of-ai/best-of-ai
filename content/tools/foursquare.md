Foursquare began as an app for checking in at places and discovering venues, but its main business today is location data. The company maintains a large database of points of interest, including businesses, landmarks, and venues, with details like categories, hours, and popularity. Developers use its Places API to add search, recommendations, and place information to maps and apps, and brands use its data to understand where people go.

Beyond place data, Foursquare provides tools for analyzing movement and location behavior. Panels of opted-in users allow measurement of foot traffic to stores, audience analysis, and attribution for advertising, and mapping and geospatial products let companies visualize and process location data at scale. Machine learning keeps place information up to date and helps match records from different sources.

The consumer side has narrowed: the Foursquare City Guide app was shut down, and the company focuses its consumer product on Swarm, which lets people check in and keep a record of places. For developers, Foursquare offers a free tier for the API and paid plans for higher volumes and richer data. Competitors in place data include Google Places, Mapbox, HERE, TomTom, and Overture Maps, an open data effort. Privacy rules around location data are strict and vary by region, so companies should confirm how data is collected.
