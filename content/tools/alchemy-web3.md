Alchemy gives developers a reliable way to talk to blockchains. Running your own Ethereum node takes expertise and hardware, so most teams rent access instead. Alchemy offers that through APIs that read data, send transactions, and subscribe to events, supporting Ethereum and many other networks, including layer 2 chains. Its infrastructure is built for uptime and speed, which matters when an app depends on real-time chain data.

On top of raw node access are higher-level tools. Enhanced APIs return token balances, NFT metadata, transaction history, and transfers in a form that is easier to use than raw blockchain data. Smart wallet and account abstraction tools let apps create wallets and sponsor gas fees for users, rollup services help teams launch their own chains, and webhooks and subgraphs deliver data to applications. Dashboards show request volume, errors, and latency.

Alchemy has a free tier with a monthly allowance, and paid plans that raise throughput and add support, with enterprise terms for larger teams. Applications built on its infrastructure depend on its availability, so teams sometimes add a backup provider. The Web3 market has swung widely, and developer demand tracks that. Infura, QuickNode, and Moralis are the closest competitors, and teams that need full control run their own nodes.
