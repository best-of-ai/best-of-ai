PlanetScale brought a workflow familiar from Git to relational databases. Developers create a branch of their database schema, make changes, and merge them through a deploy request, with checks that avoid downtime. Behind that is Vitess, the sharding system created at YouTube, which lets MySQL scale horizontally across many servers. This allows growth beyond the limits of a single database machine.

Features focus on safe changes and performance. Online schema changes let teams alter large tables without locking, insights show slow queries and suggest improvements, and connection handling supports large numbers of clients. Recent products emphasize high-performance storage and have extended the platform to Postgres. AI-assisted insights help identify problematic queries, and the service integrates with popular frameworks and hosting platforms.

PlanetScale is paid, having ended its free hobby tier, with pricing based on resources and usage and enterprise options. It is aimed at production workloads that benefit from sharding and zero-downtime changes, so very small projects may find simpler hosts more economical. Neon and Supabase offer serverless Postgres, Amazon Aurora and Google Cloud SQL are managed alternatives, and CockroachDB and TiDB serve distributed needs. PlanetScale suits teams that run large, busy databases.
