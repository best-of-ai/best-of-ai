Bonterra was created by combining several nonprofit software companies, including EveryAction, Social Solutions, Network for Good, and CyberGrants. That heritage explains the breadth: the company sells tools for fundraising and donor management, case management for human services programs, volunteer and employee giving programs, grants administration, and online giving pages. Nonprofits can use pieces of it or adopt a larger suite.

The pitch is to connect the work of raising money with the work of delivering programs. A charity can track a donor's giving next to the services it funds, and a case manager can record outcomes that feed into reports for funders. AI features appear in tasks like drafting fundraising messages, segmenting supporters, and summarizing case notes, with attention to privacy because the data can be sensitive.

Bonterra is sold on custom contracts, and implementation can take time since it covers many modules. Some customers find that combining separate products into one experience is a work in progress. It targets mid-sized and larger nonprofits, government-funded programs, and corporate social responsibility teams. Competitors include Blackbaud, the largest company in the field, plus Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud, Neon One, Virtuous, and Bloomerang for smaller organizations.
