ElevenLabs is best known for text-to-speech that sounds like a person rather than a robot. You paste in a script, choose a voice from a large library or make your own, and the tool reads it with natural pacing and emotion. The same voice can speak dozens of languages, which makes it a popular option for audiobooks, YouTube narration, e-learning, game characters, and accessibility. Voice cloning builds a copy of a voice from a recording, and there are safeguards around it: you are meant to have the speaker's permission, and some features require identity checks.

The platform has grown into a wider set of audio products. Dubbing translates a video into other languages while keeping the original speaker's voice. Scribe transcribes speech, with speaker labels and timestamps. There are tools for sound effects and music generation, and a Studio editor for long projects like audiobooks and podcasts. For developers, the API supports streaming speech with low latency, and the agents product lets companies build voice assistants that handle phone calls and customer conversations with a chosen voice, knowledge base, and set of tools.

The free plan includes a small monthly allowance of audio and is meant for testing, and paid plans add more usage, commercial rights, and higher-quality cloning. Usage is measured in credits, so long projects can get expensive and it is worth generating drafts with a cheaper model first. Quality varies by language and voice, and it can occasionally mispronounce names or unusual terms, which you can fix with pronunciation controls. Competitors include OpenAI's text-to-speech, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, and Cartesia.
