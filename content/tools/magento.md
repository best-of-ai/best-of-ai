Magento was created as an open-source e-commerce framework and became one of the most customizable platforms for online stores. Merchants with complex needs use it to manage large catalogs, multiple storefronts and currencies, custom pricing and checkout, and integrations with ERP and other systems. A huge ecosystem of extensions, agencies, and developers supports it.

Adobe acquired Magento and sells a commercial version, Adobe Commerce, with additional features for B2B selling, merchandising, and cloud hosting and support, plus integration with Adobe's marketing and analytics products. Adobe Sensei AI powers features such as product recommendations and search. The free Magento Open Source edition continues for those who want to host and manage their own stores.

Magento is powerful but demands technical skill: running and upgrading a store takes developers, and hosting, extensions, and maintenance add to the cost. Many smaller merchants have moved to hosted platforms. Alternatives include Shopify and Shopify Plus, BigCommerce, WooCommerce, Salesforce Commerce Cloud, and commercetools for headless setups. Magento suits enterprises with in-house developers and unusual requirements.
