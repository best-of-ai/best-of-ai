Kin positions itself as a private personal AI. Users talk with an assistant that remembers their circumstances and offers guidance across areas of life, with specialized advisors that focus on different topics. The conversation builds a profile over time so advice reflects what the user has shared.

Privacy is the main selling point. The company states that personal data is stored locally on the device and encrypted, rather than in a central cloud, so sensitive details are less exposed. This appeals to people who want to discuss personal matters without handing data to a large platform.

Kin has a free tier and paid options. AI advisors are not substitutes for professionals in health, finance, or mental health, and users should verify advice. Some processing still requires model providers, so read the privacy policy. Alternatives include Pi, ChatGPT with memory, Claude, Replika, and Rewind-style tools. Kin suits people who want a personal AI with a privacy focus.
