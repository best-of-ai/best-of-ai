ZipRecruiter works on both sides of hiring. An employer writes a job once, and ZipRecruiter posts it across a network of job boards and sends it to potential candidates through email and notifications. Job seekers create a profile or upload a resume, browse listings, set alerts, and apply with a click, and the platform recommends jobs based on their interests and activity.

Matching technology is central. The system learns from employer and candidate behavior to recommend candidates to employers and jobs to seekers, and employers can invite candidates to apply. Tools help screen applicants, schedule interviews, and communicate in one place. AI features assist with writing job descriptions and surfacing strong matches, and an employer dashboard tracks responses.

ZipRecruiter is free for job seekers. Employers pay subscriptions to post and promote jobs, with plans based on features and how many candidates they want to reach. Results vary by industry and location, and some employers report getting many unqualified applicants. Indeed is the largest competitor, LinkedIn is stronger for professional roles, Glassdoor combines reviews with listings, and niche boards serve particular industries. ZipRecruiter suits small and mid-sized employers who want broad reach quickly.
