Captions is built for creators who film themselves speaking. You record or upload a video, and it transcribes the speech and adds stylish animated captions, which is important because many people watch social video with the sound off. It can remove filler words and silences, cut the video into a tighter version, and add zooms and effects that keep viewers watching. It works well on a phone, which suits creators who edit as they go.

AI features go beyond captions. Eye contact correction makes you appear to look at the camera even if you are reading a script, and dubbing translates your speech into other languages with a matching voice. AI avatars and a studio tool generate videos of a presenter from text or audio, which businesses use for marketing and training content. Other tools create b-roll and quickly adapt a video for different platforms.

The company behind Captions has rebranded as Mirage and says it is working on its own video foundation models, but the app continues under the Captions name. There is a free tier, with paid plans for more exports, longer videos, and advanced AI features. AI-generated avatars and faces raise concerns about authenticity, so disclose them where audiences would expect it. CapCut, Descript, Opus Clip, and HeyGen are the main alternatives.
