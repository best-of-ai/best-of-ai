D-ID became known for animating still photos. You upload a portrait and add a script or audio, and the platform produces a video in which the person appears to speak, with lip movement and facial expressions. That idea grew into a full avatar platform: a studio where you create presenters, choose voices and languages, and generate videos for training, marketing, and customer communication.

Beyond videos, D-ID provides tools for interactive use. An API lets developers generate talking avatars in their own applications, and real-time agents combine an avatar, a language model, and a voice so that a digital person can hold a conversation on a website or kiosk. The company also works on privacy technology, with its origins in tools that protect faces from recognition systems.

D-ID is paid, with a free trial and plans based on video minutes, and enterprise options. Synthetic presenters raise questions of consent and misuse, so using someone's likeness without permission is a legal and ethical problem, and audiences may expect disclosure. Quality is good for simple talking-head formats but less natural than filmed video. HeyGen and Synthesia are the main competitors, Colossyan serves corporate training, and Hedra focuses on character animation.
