Storybird approaches writing through pictures. Writers choose artwork from a library of illustrations and build a story around it, which lowers the barrier for young children who find a blank page daunting. The platform offers story types such as picture books, chapter books, and poetry, along with prompts and challenges to get ideas flowing, and finished stories can be published, shared, and read.

Education has been a major focus. Teachers use Storybird to run writing assignments, give feedback, and manage classes, with lessons, quizzes, and activities that support literacy skills. Students can write and publish in a safe, teacher-moderated environment. Newer features introduce AI assistance for prompts and feedback, with a focus on supporting rather than replacing the student's writing.

Storybird is free to start, with premium plans for classrooms and families offering more tools and content. The artwork library shapes the look of stories, and the platform's updates have been modest compared with larger educational technology products. Alternatives include Book Creator, Canva for Education, Wattpad for older writers, Epic for reading, and general AI assistants used with teacher guidance. Storybird suits teachers and families encouraging young writers.
