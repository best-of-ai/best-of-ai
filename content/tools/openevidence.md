OpenEvidence works like a medical search engine that writes answers. A clinician asks a question, such as the first-line treatment for a condition or how to manage a drug interaction, and the system searches the medical literature and produces a summary with references to the underlying studies and guidelines.

It has partnerships with major medical publishers and journals, which gives it access to authoritative content. Clinicians use it at the point of care, and it has become widely used among US physicians. The company funds it through advertising aimed at medical professionals.

AI summaries can be wrong or miss context, so doctors verify with the cited sources and use clinical judgment. It is not for patient self-diagnosis. Alternatives include UpToDate with AI, Doximity GPT, Glass Health, Elsevier's ClinicalKey AI, and Perplexity. OpenEvidence suits clinicians looking for fast, cited evidence.
