Kira was one of the first widely adopted machine learning tools in legal work. It reads contracts and identifies provisions such as change of control, termination rights, assignment, and indemnification, then extracts the relevant text into a structured summary. Lawyers use it in mergers and acquisitions due diligence, where hundreds or thousands of agreements must be reviewed quickly, and in lease, finance, and compliance reviews.

Litera acquired Kira Systems and continues to develop it. The newer version adds generative AI to the original extraction models, with features like rapid clause analysis that organizes clauses across many documents. Users can start from built-in models for common provisions, train their own for specific needs, and review results in a side-by-side view that links back to the source text. Integration with Litera's other tools connects review to drafting and transaction workflows.

Kira is sold to law firms and corporate legal teams on enterprise contracts at a premium price, with training and support. Accuracy is high for standard clauses but not perfect, so lawyers review the output. Competitors include Luminance, Eigen, Harvey, Thomson Reuters CoCounsel, Evisort, and LexisNexis tools. The shift to generative AI has made some newer entrants more flexible, while Kira's advantage is its long track record and trusted extraction models.
