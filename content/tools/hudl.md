Hudl made video review standard for coaching. Teams upload game and practice footage, coaches tag plays, and players watch clips on their phones, which replaces the old routine of sitting in a film room with a stack of tapes. The platform supports many sports, including football, basketball, volleyball, and soccer, and it lets coaches create playlists, draw on video, and share feedback with individuals or groups.

The product now includes hardware and AI. Hudl's Focus cameras automatically follow the action and record games without a camera operator, which makes video affordable for schools and clubs. AI features tag events, cut highlights, and generate statistics, and a recruiting side of Hudl helps athletes share highlights with college coaches. Separate products serve professional teams with advanced analytics and scouting data, such as through its Wyscout and Sportscode lines.

Hudl is sold by subscription, with tiers for schools, clubs, and professional organizations, and some free features for athletes. Costs vary by team size and equipment, and a school may need approval for recording and sharing footage of minors. Competitors include Veo, Pixellot, Trace, and Catapult for tracking data, and many teams still use basic tools like YouTube. Hudl dominates in school and club sports in many regions.
