Spire builds and operates a large fleet of small satellites known as nanosatellites. Instead of taking photographs, they listen: they pick up radio signals from ships, aircraft, and GPS signals bouncing off the Earth, and measure atmospheric conditions. The resulting data feeds products that track vessels around the world, monitor air traffic, and improve weather forecasts.

The business sells data and analytics as a service. Maritime customers use it to follow ships, estimate arrival times, and detect suspicious behavior, aviation customers use it for flight tracking, and meteorological agencies use radio occultation data to enhance forecasts. Spire also provides space services, building and operating satellites for customers. Machine learning supports forecasting and anomaly detection in the data, and APIs deliver it to customers' systems.

Spire is a public company selling to governments, shippers, insurers, and weather and climate groups on contracts, with pricing that depends on the data and coverage. Its business has shifted as it focused on certain lines. Competitors include Planet Labs for imagery, HawkEye 360 for radio frequency data, exactEarth products now under other owners, and national weather services. It's relevant mainly to organizations that need global tracking and weather data.
