Crisalix builds simulation tools for aesthetic medicine. A surgeon takes photos of a patient, the software builds a 3D model of the face or body, and the surgeon adjusts parameters to show what a procedure might look like. Patients can see and discuss options before deciding.

The aim is to improve communication and set realistic expectations. A visual helps patients and surgeons align on goals, and consultations can be more productive. The company sells to plastic surgeons, dermatologists, and clinics, and has tools for marketing and patient engagement.

Simulations are illustrations, not guarantees, and clinics must be careful to explain that results vary. Crisalix is sold by subscription to professionals. Alternatives include Canfield Scientific VECTRA, Vectra systems, Arc, and Axis Three. Crisalix suits cosmetic practices that want to show patients possible outcomes.
