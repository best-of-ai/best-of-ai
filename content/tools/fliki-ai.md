Fliki turns written content into video. A user pastes a script, links an article, or writes a prompt, and the tool splits it into scenes, picks visuals from a stock library or generated media, and adds narration with a synthetic voice. The editor allows swapping clips, adjusting text, and changing voices.

A big draw is the range of voices and languages, which lets creators produce the same video in several markets. Avatars and voice cloning are available, and text to speech is offered as a standalone product for narration and audio. Marketers, educators, and YouTubers use it to repurpose content.

Fliki has a free plan with limits and paid tiers by minutes. Videos made from stock footage can look generic, so adding original visuals helps. Alternatives include Pictory, InVideo, Lumen5, HeyGen, and Synthesia. Fliki suits creators who want fast narrated videos from text.
