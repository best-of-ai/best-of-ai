ABBYY has worked with optical character recognition for decades. Its FineReader software converts scanned pages and PDFs into editable text, and its enterprise products read invoices, contracts, IDs, and forms at scale. Machine learning classifies the type of document, locates key fields such as invoice totals and dates, and extracts the data in a structured form that other systems can use.

The platform has expanded into intelligent document processing. Pre-trained skills handle common document types without training, while companies can build custom models for their own forms. Validation tools route low-confidence results to human review, and integrations connect with robotic process automation, ERP, and content management systems. ABBYY has added generative AI features and tools for making documents ready for use by language models, as companies seek to feed documents into AI workflows.

ABBYY is sold to enterprises through licenses and subscriptions with custom pricing, and a consumer FineReader product is available for desktop users. Accuracy depends on scan quality and document variety. Competitors include Google Document AI, Amazon Textract, Microsoft Azure Document Intelligence, Kofax, UiPath Document Understanding, and Rossum for invoices. ABBYY suits organizations processing large volumes of paper and PDF documents.
