Embark's test starts with a cheek swab at home. You activate the kit, collect a sample from your dog, and mail it to the lab, and results arrive online in a few weeks. The breed report shows a percentage breakdown across hundreds of breeds and how far back each breed appears in the family tree, which owners of mixed-breed dogs often find revealing. It also estimates traits such as coat color and weight.

The health screening is the main selling point. The test checks for a large number of genetic variants linked to inherited conditions, and reports which ones your dog carries or is at risk of developing. Owners can share findings with their veterinarian to guide checkups and preventive care, and breeders use it to avoid pairing dogs that carry the same recessive conditions. The company works with university researchers, and customers can opt in to share data for studies. Relative-finding connects you with other owners of related dogs.

Embark is a paid product, sold as test kits with optional upgraded panels, and results depend on the company's database and algorithms. A genetic result indicates risk, not a diagnosis, and a carrier of a condition may never show symptoms. Interpreting findings with a veterinarian is wise. Wisdom Panel is the main competitor, with similar tests, and results can differ between companies because they use different reference data.
