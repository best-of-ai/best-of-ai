Casetext began as a community site where lawyers annotated and shared case law, then grew into a legal research service with tools such as a brief-checking feature called Parallel Search, which found relevant cases using natural language. It was among the first legal technology companies to build on large language models, and its CoCounsel assistant could review documents, summarize depositions, draft research memos, and help prepare contract analyses with citations to sources.

Thomson Reuters acquired Casetext, folding CoCounsel into its portfolio alongside Westlaw and Practical Law. The standalone Casetext platform was later retired, and visitors to its old address are now sent to Thomson Reuters. CoCounsel continues as a product under Thomson Reuters, offered to law firms, corporate legal departments, and government agencies, often through Westlaw subscriptions.

For legal professionals looking for what Casetext offered, CoCounsel is the direct successor. Harvey, Lexis+ AI from LexisNexis, and vLex's Vincent are competing legal AI tools. AI can produce fabricated citations, which has led to sanctions against lawyers who filed unchecked output, so any AI-assisted legal work must be verified against primary sources. This entry stays for reference because older articles and links still point to Casetext.
