Loom replaces many meetings and long emails with a short video. You click record, show your screen while talking, and Loom uploads the video instantly and gives you a link to share. Viewers can watch at their own pace, react with emojis, comment at specific timestamps, and get an automatic transcript. It's widely used for product walkthroughs, bug reports, onboarding, feedback, and updates to distributed teams.

AI features cut the work around videos. Loom creates titles and summaries, chapters for easy navigation, and action items, and can remove filler words and silences or turn a recording into a written document or ticket. Integrations connect with Slack, Jira, Notion, and Gmail, and since Atlassian acquired Loom, it fits more closely with tools like Jira and Confluence.

Loom has a free plan with limits on length and number of videos and paid plans with unlimited videos, editing, and AI. Videos that run too long or contain sensitive screens need care, and some colleagues prefer text. Alternatives include Zoom Clips, Claap, Tella, Descript, Screen Studio, and the built-in screen recording in Teams and Slack. Loom suits teams that want to communicate asynchronously.
