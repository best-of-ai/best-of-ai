ContractPodAi was built for corporate legal departments that handle large volumes of contracts. Its platform covers the lifecycle: drafting from templates, routing for approval, negotiation, signature, storage in a searchable repository, and tracking of obligations and renewals. It connects with systems such as Salesforce, Microsoft 365, and ERP tools so contracts sit within the business processes they relate to.

The company has rebranded as Leah, after the name of its AI product, and now describes itself as an agentic AI platform. Its AI agents review contracts against playbooks, flag risks and deviations, extract key terms, answer questions about a contract portfolio, and handle repetitive tasks across legal, procurement, and finance. The change reflects a wider move in legal technology from software that stores documents to software that does the first pass of the work.

Leah is sold to enterprises through custom quotes, with implementation projects that take time. Accuracy matters in legal work, so AI-extracted terms and suggested edits should be reviewed by lawyers. Competitors include Ironclad, Icertis, DocuSign CLM, Agiloft, and Evisort (now part of Workday), along with newer AI-native tools like Spellbook and Harvey for drafting. Companies choosing a system should weigh integration with existing tools and the maturity of the AI features.
