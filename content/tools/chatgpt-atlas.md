Atlas puts ChatGPT inside the browser. A sidebar assistant can see the page you are on, so you can ask it to summarize an article, explain a table, compare products across tabs, or draft a reply in context. The address bar can also act as a chat prompt, and the browser can remember context from your browsing if you turn that on.

Agent mode goes further. The assistant can take actions on your behalf, such as navigating to a site, filling in a form, or doing research across several pages, while you watch and can take over. This capability is powerful and also risky, since web pages can contain instructions meant to trick an agent, a problem known as prompt injection.

Atlas is free to use with ChatGPT accounts, and agent features depend on plan. Browser memories and page access raise privacy considerations that deserve a look at settings. Alternatives include Perplexity's Comet, Dia, Microsoft Edge with Copilot, Chrome with Gemini, and Brave with Leo. Atlas suits people who want ChatGPT deeply integrated into browsing.
