Cerebras took an unusual approach to AI hardware. Instead of cutting a silicon wafer into many small chips, it builds a single enormous chip from the whole wafer, which holds an enormous amount of compute and on-chip memory. That design reduces the data movement between chips that slows down large models, and lets it generate tokens at very high speeds.

The company sells its systems and operates a cloud inference service where developers call open models through an API, with fast responses that suit agents, coding tools, and real-time applications. It has partnerships with AI labs and cloud providers, and supports training as well as inference. Developers can try the API with a free tier and then pay by usage, often at competitive per-token rates.

Cerebras competes in a field dominated by NVIDIA, and its model catalog is limited compared with general cloud providers. Capacity and supported models change, and large customers dominate its business. Alternatives for fast inference include Groq, SambaNova, Together AI, and Fireworks, which run on different hardware, along with NVIDIA-based clouds. Cerebras suits developers who need very low latency and teams interested in alternatives to GPUs.
