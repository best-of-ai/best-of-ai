Coda reimagines the document as something that can behave like an app. A Coda doc can contain text, tables, buttons, and automations, so a team can build a project tracker, a wiki, a CRM, or a planning tool in the same document where they write. Formulas, similar to those in spreadsheets, connect data across the doc, and Packs integrate with outside services such as Slack, Jira, and Google Calendar.

AI features run through the platform. Coda AI can summarize documents, draft text, fill table columns with generated content, categorize and extract information, and answer questions about the contents of a doc. AI blocks and agents can be placed in workflows to automate tasks. The company joined Grammarly, which has taken the name Superhuman, and its tools are being combined with Grammarly's writing assistant and the Superhuman email app.

Coda has a free plan with limits and paid plans per doc maker, with viewers free, which is a different pricing model than most tools. Its flexibility has a learning curve, and as the product is integrated into a larger suite, its direction may evolve. Notion is the main competitor with a similar block-based approach, Airtable offers stronger database features, and Google Docs and Microsoft Loop are simpler collaborative documents. Coda suits teams building custom workflows in documents.
