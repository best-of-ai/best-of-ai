Dynamics 365 combines CRM and ERP in a modular set of cloud applications. The sales and customer service apps manage leads, opportunities, cases, and customer communications, while finance, supply chain, and Business Central handle accounting, inventory, purchasing, and operations. Because the modules share a data platform, a company can start with one and add others over time, and they connect closely with Microsoft 365, Teams, and Power BI.

Copilot is built throughout. In sales, it summarizes accounts and meetings, drafts emails, and suggests next steps. In service, it helps agents find answers and summarize cases, and in finance and supply chain, it supports tasks like reconciliations, collections, and spotting disruptions. AI agents take on routine tasks, such as qualifying leads or processing orders, and Copilot Studio lets companies build their own.

Dynamics 365 is sold per user and per app, usually through Microsoft partners who handle implementation, and costs can be substantial. It is powerful but takes planning and customization. Salesforce is the main competitor in CRM, SAP, Oracle NetSuite, and Workday compete in ERP, and HubSpot and Zoho suit smaller businesses. It fits mid-sized and large organizations that already rely on Microsoft technology.
