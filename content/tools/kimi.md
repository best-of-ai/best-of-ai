Kimi first gained attention for handling very long documents. Users can upload large PDFs, books, or collections of files and ask questions, and the assistant keeps track of the whole text, which suited tasks like summarizing reports and analyzing contracts. It also searches the web, writes and reasons about code, and has agent features that carry out multi-step tasks.

Moonshot AI releases powerful models, including open-weight versions of its Kimi K2 family, which are among the larger open models and strong at coding and agentic tasks. Developers use them through Moonshot's API or run them through third-party providers, and they are often used inside coding tools. The company competes with other Chinese labs such as DeepSeek, Alibaba, and Zhipu.

Kimi's chat app is free with usage limits, and the API is priced by tokens. As a service from a Chinese company, it follows Chinese rules on content, which affects some answers, and its privacy practices are governed accordingly. Open-weight models can be run elsewhere to avoid that concern. Alternatives include DeepSeek, Qwen, ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. Kimi suits users who want long-context analysis and developers looking for strong open models.
