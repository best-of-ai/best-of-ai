Obviously AI was built to make machine learning accessible to business users. You upload a CSV or connect a data source, choose the thing you want to predict, such as customer churn, sales, or lead conversion, and the platform tests multiple algorithms, picks the best one, and shows the results in plain language. It highlights which columns most influence the prediction and lets you try what-if scenarios.

Predictions can be exported or sent to other tools, and the platform offers dashboards for sharing insights with teammates. It positions itself for marketing, sales, finance, and operations analysts who have data but no data scientists, and for consultants who need quick models for clients.

Obviously AI is paid, with plans based on usage and a trial. Automated modeling is only as good as the data and the question, and users can be misled by correlations or poor data quality, so understanding the basics of data matters. Competitors and alternatives include DataRobot, Akkio, Julius AI, Google's Vertex AI AutoML, and Excel or Sheets with built-in forecasting. Obviously AI suits business teams that want predictions without writing code.
