Cohere sells AI to businesses and keeps its focus there. Its flagship Command models handle generation, summarization, and tool use, while Embed turns text into vectors for semantic search, and Rerank reorders search results by relevance. Together those pieces form the backbone of retrieval-augmented generation, where a model answers from a company's own documents instead of from memory. Cohere also builds models for translation, document parsing, and speech transcription.

What differentiates it from OpenAI and Anthropic is how it is deployed. Cohere models can run on major clouds, in a customer's private cloud, or fully on-premises, which suits banks, governments, and healthcare organizations that cannot send data to a public service. North is its workplace platform for AI agents that search company data and complete tasks, and Compass provides enterprise search. The company's research arm also releases open-weight models for non-commercial use.

Developers can use a trial API key for free, with production access billed by usage or through enterprise agreements. It is not trying to win a consumer chatbot race, so most people encounter it indirectly through products built on top of it. Competitors include OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, and AI21 for models, and Google Vertex AI and Amazon Bedrock for enterprise deployment. It is a strong fit for search-heavy business applications with strict data requirements.
