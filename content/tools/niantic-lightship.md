Lightship began as a set of tools for building location-based AR experiences. The ARDK SDK for Unity offered features such as depth sensing, occlusion, and meshing, and the Visual Positioning System let apps determine a device's precise location and orientation by matching camera images to a map of the real world. The technology came from the infrastructure behind Pokémon GO and drew on scans collected from players.

Niantic's geospatial work later moved into a separate company, Niantic Spatial, after the games business was sold. The developer platform was renamed the Niantic Spatial Platform, with an SDK for Unity, tools for scanning places through Scaniverse, and mapping services. The original Lightship website was shut down, and visitors are redirected, with guidance for migrating projects.

Developers should check Niantic Spatial's documentation for current features and pricing, which include free tiers and paid plans for commercial use. Large-scale AR remains niche, and coverage of visual positioning depends on the mapped area. Alternatives include Google's ARCore Geospatial API, Apple's ARKit with location anchors, 8th Wall, which has been discontinued, Snap's Camera Kit, and Immersal and Vuforia for positioning and tracking. This suits AR developers building location-based experiences.
