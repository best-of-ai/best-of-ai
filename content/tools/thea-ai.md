Thea focuses on learning over answer-getting. A student uploads notes or a textbook section, and Thea creates questions, hints, and explanations that walk through the material step by step, asking the student to think rather than handing over the solution.

Features include quizzes, flashcards, and summaries built from the student's own materials, which keeps study aligned with the course. The approach aligns with research showing that retrieval practice improves memory.

Independent information is limited. Thea has a free tier and paid plans. Alternatives include Quizlet, Anki, Khanmigo, NotebookLM, and StudyFetch. Thea suits students who want guided practice.
