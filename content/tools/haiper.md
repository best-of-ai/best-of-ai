Haiper launched as a fast, accessible video generator. Users typed a prompt or uploaded an image and received a short clip, with tools to extend videos, repaint areas, and animate stills. The company emphasized speed and iteration, aiming to let creators try many ideas quickly, and attracted attention as one of the more promising independent challengers to larger video model makers.

It could not keep pace with the rapid advances and heavy computing costs of video models. The company closed its consumer platform, and its founders moved to other roles, while its video models were acquired by another company that offers them through an API for enterprises. User projects hosted on the original platform were no longer accessible.

People looking for an alternative can use Kling, Hailuo, Runway, Luma, Pika, Google's Veo, and open models such as Wan. The story is a reminder that independent AI video startups face difficult economics, so exporting your work is wise. This entry stays for reference because older articles still list Haiper among AI video tools.
