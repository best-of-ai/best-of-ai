Litmaps helps researchers see a field instead of reading a list. You start with one or more papers you know, and the tool plots them on a map where one axis shows publication date and the other shows how much a paper is cited. Lines between the nodes show citation links. From the seeds, it suggests related papers that cite or are cited by your set, so you can quickly find missing foundational works and recent developments.

The monitoring features are a big part of the appeal. You can save a map as a collection and Litmaps will watch for new papers that match, sending alerts so your literature review stays current. Maps can be shared with collaborators, and you can import and export references with common managers like Zotero. Searching can use keywords or paper identifiers, and the tool is used by graduate students, academics, and R&D teams.

Litmaps has a free plan with a limited number of maps and paid plans that raise the limits and add features. It relies on public bibliographic data, so coverage varies by field. It does not summarize papers or answer questions. Connected Papers, ResearchRabbit, and Inciteful offer similar visual exploration, Semantic Scholar and Google Scholar are search engines, and Elicit and Consensus add AI answers. It is well suited to systematic and ongoing reviews.
