Tinder popularized the swipe. You see a profile with photos and a short bio, swipe right to like or left to pass, and if the other person also likes you, you match and can message. The format is fast and casual, which fits its large, young user base across many countries. People use it for dating, relationships, and meeting new people, and settings let you filter by distance, age, and gender preferences.

The app has added features around the swipe. Profiles can show prompts, interests, and verified photos, and options like Passport let paying users swipe in other cities. AI shows up in a few places: Photo Selector uses a face scan to choose the profile photos that are likely to perform best, and other features suggest conversation starters and use signals to improve who you see. Safety tools include photo verification and reporting.

Tinder is free to use with limits on daily likes, and paid tiers sell extras such as unlimited likes, seeing who liked you, boosts, and more control. Prices can vary by age and region, which has drawn criticism. Like all dating apps, it carries risks: fake profiles, scams, and privacy concerns around sharing photos and location. Many users report swipe fatigue. Hinge and Bumble are the main rivals, and Hinge in particular is positioned toward longer-term relationships.
