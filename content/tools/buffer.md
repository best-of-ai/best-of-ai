Buffer is built for people who want to run their social accounts without living in each app. You connect your profiles, write a post once, adjust it per platform, and schedule it into a queue that publishes at set times. A calendar view shows what is planned, and an idea library keeps drafts and inspiration in one place. The interface is deliberately plain, which is a big part of why freelancers, creators, and small businesses like it.

Beyond publishing, it covers the surrounding tasks. Analytics show which posts earned engagement and when your audience is most active. A community inbox collects comments and replies so you can respond in one place, and approval workflows let a team review posts before they go live. Start Page builds a simple link-in-bio page. The AI assistant drafts captions from a prompt, rewrites a post for a different platform, and turns one idea into several variations.

Buffer has a free plan with a small number of channels and scheduled posts, and paid plans priced per connected channel that add more capacity, analytics, and team features. It is lighter than enterprise suites such as Sprout Social or Hootsuite, with fewer listening and reporting tools. That is a plus if you want something simple and a limit if you manage dozens of accounts for clients. Later and Metricool are closer competitors.
