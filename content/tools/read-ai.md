Read AI began as a meeting analytics tool. It joins calls on Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and other platforms, then produces a transcript, summary, action items, and chapters. What set it apart was measurement: reports on engagement, speaker time, and sentiment, giving meeting organizers a sense of how a conversation went and who participated.

The product has expanded across communication channels. Read AI can summarize emails and messages, and its search and Q&A features answer questions across meetings, email, and chat, so you can ask what was decided about a project or what a client said last month. Integrations send summaries to Slack, Notion, Salesforce, and HubSpot. A copilot can prepare you for upcoming meetings by pulling relevant history.

Read AI has a free plan with limited use and paid plans with more history, features, and team controls. Engagement scores are estimates and may be uncomfortable for participants who prefer not to be measured, so use them with care. As with all recorders, get consent. Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, and MeetGeek are the main alternatives, while Zoom, Teams, and Google Meet have built-in AI notes. Read AI suits people who want analytics along with notes.
