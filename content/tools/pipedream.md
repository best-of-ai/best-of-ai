Pipedream sits between no-code automation and writing everything yourself. You build workflows from triggers, such as a webhook or a new row in a sheet, and steps that either call a prebuilt action for an app or run your own code, with access to npm and PyPI packages. That mix lets developers move faster than in tools like Zapier while still handling logic that needs real programming, and it suits integrations and internal automations.

The platform has a large library of app connectors, with managed authentication so developers don't deal with OAuth for each service. Pipedream Connect lets software companies add integrations to their own products, and its tools expose thousands of actions to AI agents through the Model Context Protocol, which makes it popular for giving agents access to apps. Event sources, scheduling, and a free tier lower the barrier to experimenting.

Pipedream has a generous free plan and paid plans based on credits for workflow runs. Workday announced an agreement to acquire the company to extend its AI agent integrations, so packaging may change after the deal closes. Alternatives include Zapier, Make, n8n, Activepieces, Temporal for code-first workflows, and Windmill. Pipedream suits developers who want code-friendly automation with many integrations.
