Zoho CRM covers the normal work of sales: tracking leads and contacts, managing deals through a pipeline, logging calls and emails, and forecasting revenue. It supports multiple channels, including phone, email, live chat, and social media, and lets you build custom modules and fields so it can reflect how a business actually sells. Workflow rules, assignment rules, and blueprints enforce a consistent sales process without manual follow-up.

Zia is the AI layer. It scores leads by how likely they are to convert, predicts which deals will close, spots unusual activity, and recommends the best time to contact a prospect. It can answer questions in plain language, summarize records, and help draft emails. Because Zoho builds its own models, it positions this as a cost-effective alternative to enterprise AI add-ons. Integrations connect with Zoho's other products such as Campaigns, Books, and Desk, and with outside tools through a marketplace.

Zoho CRM has a free plan for very small teams, and paid editions that add automation, analytics, and customization, with enterprise options. Prices are lower than Salesforce, which is its main draw, and the product is flexible but can feel cluttered. Adopting more of the Zoho suite brings the most benefit, while teams using non-Zoho tools may find integration less polished. HubSpot is easier to start with, Pipedrive is simpler, and Salesforce is more powerful.
