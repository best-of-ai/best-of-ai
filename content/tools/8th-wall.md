8th Wall made AR available through a link. Developers built experiences using web technologies, and users opened them in a phone browser by scanning a QR code or tapping a link, with no app download. Brands used it for marketing campaigns, product try-ons, games, and events, and agencies liked that it worked across iOS and Android. Features included world tracking, image targets, face effects, and, later, location-based AR.

Niantic, the company behind Pokémon Go, acquired 8th Wall, and its technology was used alongside Niantic's mapping and spatial work. Niantic Spatial, the spinoff focused on geospatial AI, then ended access to the 8th Wall platform. Hosted projects were given time to keep running, and the engine was released as open source, so developers can still use its code.

Developers needing similar web AR have other options. Zappar, Snap's Lens Studio and Camera Kit, Adobe Aero's successors, and the WebXR standard in modern browsers support various AR use cases, while Unity and Unreal serve native apps. This entry stays for reference, since many past campaigns and articles mention 8th Wall.
