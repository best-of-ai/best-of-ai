ClickUp tries to be a single home for everything a team does. At its center are tasks that can be shown as lists, boards, calendars, Gantt charts, or timelines, and each task can have subtasks, custom fields, dependencies, and checklists. Around that sit docs, wikis, whiteboards, forms, dashboards, goals, and built-in chat. Teams use it for software sprints, marketing calendars, client projects, and operations, and the same workspace can be shaped differently for each department.

Flexibility is the product's main selling point and its main complaint. There are hundreds of settings and templates, which lets a team build exactly the workflow it wants, but a new workspace can feel crowded and take time to configure. ClickUp Brain adds AI across the workspace: it drafts task descriptions and documents, summarizes comment threads, turns notes into action items, and answers questions by searching your tasks and docs. Automations and AI agents handle routine steps, like assigning work or posting updates.

ClickUp has a free plan and paid tiers that add storage, automations, reporting, and admin features, with AI sold as an add-on or included in higher tiers depending on the plan. The depth of features can overwhelm small teams, who may prefer the simplicity of Trello or Asana. It tends to suit teams that want to replace several tools with one and are willing to spend time setting it up.
