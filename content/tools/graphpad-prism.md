Users enter data in tables, choose analyses such as t-tests, ANOVA, survival curves, or nonlinear regression, and get publication-style graphs. It guides non-statisticians with explanations of results. Dose-response and kinetics fitting are common uses.

It is a paid license, often provided through universities. It is less flexible than R or Python for custom work.

R, Python, and JMP are alternatives. Prism suits bench scientists who want a quick path from data to figure.
