Apple Maps comes installed on every Apple device and has improved a great deal since its rocky start. It provides turn-by-turn driving directions, walking, cycling, and public transit routes, plus lane guidance and speed limit information. Features such as Look Around give a street-level view of many cities, detailed maps show buildings and landmarks in 3D, and downloadable offline maps work without a connection. Guides collect recommended places in a city, and you can share your arrival time with friends.

Machine learning supports many of the details. It estimates arrival times and traffic, suggests places you may want to visit, and learns your commonly used routes and destinations. Search understands natural phrases like coffee near the airport, and Siri can start navigation by voice. On Apple Watch and in CarPlay, directions appear on the wrist or the car display, which is a major reason iPhone owners use it.

Apple Maps is free and ties into Apple's privacy approach, which avoids tying location history to your Apple account. Business data and coverage vary by country, and it generally lags Google Maps in the number of listings, reviews, and real-time updates for small businesses. A web version exists, but Android has no native app. Google Maps and Waze are the main alternatives, and Citymapper covers transit in some cities.
