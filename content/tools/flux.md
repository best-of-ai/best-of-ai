FLUX comes from Black Forest Labs, a company founded by researchers who previously worked on Stable Diffusion. Its models became popular because they produce sharp, detailed images that follow prompts closely, including difficult cases like readable text in a picture and complex scenes with several subjects. For many people, FLUX became the best open alternative to closed services such as Midjourney, because the weights for several versions can be downloaded and used locally.

The lineup has several roles. Larger models are aimed at top quality through the API, smaller ones are designed for speed and local use, and editing-focused models let you change an existing image with a text instruction while keeping the subject consistent. FLUX Tools add features like inpainting, outpainting, and using a reference image or depth map for control. You can use the models through Black Forest Labs' own playground and API, through third-party platforms such as Replicate, fal, and Hugging Face, and in local tools like ComfyUI.

Licensing differs by model. Some open-weight versions are free for non-commercial use, while commercial use requires a license or going through the paid API. Running the larger models locally takes a powerful GPU, and quality varies with the prompt and version. Alternatives include Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, OpenAI's image models, and Google's Imagen and Gemini image models. Fine-tuning a FLUX model with LoRAs for a particular style or subject is a common practice in its community.
