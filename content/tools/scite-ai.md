Scite changes how citation counts are read. A paper with many citations may be well supported or heavily disputed, and a raw count does not tell you which. Scite analyzes the text around each citation and labels it as supporting, contrasting, or mentioning, with a snippet of the citing sentence, so you can see how others have actually used a finding. It helps researchers judge reliability and spot controversial claims.

The platform now includes AI features. An assistant answers research questions using the scientific literature and shows sources with Smart Citations, and tools for reference checking can scan a manuscript or reading list to see whether cited works have been retracted or disputed. A browser extension shows citation context on publisher sites and PubMed, and dashboards help institutions understand their research's impact. Scite is part of Research Solutions.

Scite has a free tier with limited features and paid plans for individuals and institutions. Classification of citations is automated and not perfect, and coverage depends on access to full-text articles. It supports, but does not replace, reading and judgment. Consensus and Elicit offer AI answers from papers, Semantic Scholar provides influence indicators, and Web of Science and Scopus have traditional citation databases. Scite is useful for evaluating claims in the literature.
