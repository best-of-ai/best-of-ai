Harvey was built for lawyers and has become one of the best-known legal AI companies. Attorneys use its assistant to ask legal questions, draft and revise contracts and memos, summarize documents, and analyze large sets of files, such as the documents in a due diligence review. It runs on leading language models adapted for legal work and is designed to provide citations so lawyers can verify the answers.

Beyond a chat assistant, Harvey offers structured features. Vault stores and analyzes large document collections, workflows automate multi-step tasks such as contract review against a playbook, and integrations connect with tools law firms already use, including document management and legal research providers. Firms can customize the system with their own templates and precedents, and security controls support the confidentiality requirements of legal practice.

Harvey is sold to firms and corporate legal teams on enterprise contracts, not to individuals, with onboarding and training. As with any AI in law, output can contain errors or invented citations, and lawyers remain responsible for checking everything before it is used. Competitors include Thomson Reuters CoCounsel, LexisNexis Lexis+ AI, Legora, Spellbook, and Clio's legal AI. Large firms often test several. Harvey stands out for the breadth of its adoption among top-tier firms.
