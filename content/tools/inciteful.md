Inciteful takes a paper or a list of papers and builds a network of the research that surrounds them. Using citation data, it identifies the papers that are most connected, which tend to be foundational works, review articles, and recent developments. You enter a DOI, title, or several seed papers, and the tool shows a ranked list of papers, authors, and journals, with visual graphs of how they connect.

It is most useful early in a literature review. Starting from a few papers you know, you can discover similar and influential work you might have missed, track the most cited authors in the area, and spot new papers that cite your seeds. A Paper Discovery view finds the most important papers connected to your starting point, and a Literature Connector finds papers that link two different topics. Results can be exported to reference managers.

Inciteful is free and runs in the browser, built by an independent developer, and it uses open scholarly data. Coverage depends on those sources, so very new papers and some fields may have gaps. It does not summarize papers or answer questions, which is the job of tools such as Elicit, Consensus, and SciSpace. Connected Papers and ResearchRabbit offer similar visual exploration, and Semantic Scholar and Google Scholar are general search tools. It suits researchers who want a transparent, free citation explorer.
