Wordware treats prompts as code. Users write workflows in an editor that feels like a document, mixing instructions in plain language with structure for loops, branches, and tool calls. The aim is to let people who understand a problem, not only engineers, build AI apps.

Workflows can be published as apps or APIs, and the platform supports several model providers and integrations. Teams use it for research agents, content workflows, and internal tools.

Wordware has a free tier and paid plans. Alternatives include Dify, Flowise, Relevance AI, Vellum, and n8n. Wordware suits builders who want to program in natural language.
