Lumo comes from Proton, the company behind Proton Mail and Proton VPN, and it carries the same privacy pitch. Prompts are handled by open-source language models running on Proton's own servers in Europe, so your text is not passed to OpenAI or Google. Proton says it keeps no logs of conversations and does not use them to train models. The models it has used include Mistral and OLMo variants, and the lineup can change.

Chats you choose to save are protected with zero-access encryption, which means Proton cannot read them. You can use Lumo without an account, though you need one to save history. It handles the usual chat tasks such as questions, drafting and summaries, and you can upload files. A ghost mode makes a conversation disappear when you close it. Web search is optional and off by default, and Proton says it only uses privacy-friendly search sources when you turn it on.

The trade-off is model quality. Open models on Proton's hardware are generally behind the newest flagship systems from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, so hard reasoning and coding tasks may come out weaker. The free plan limits the number of questions and uploads, and Lumo Plus raises those limits. If you already use Proton's other products, Lumo fits in without a new vendor.
