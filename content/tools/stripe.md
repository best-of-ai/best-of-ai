Stripe began as a way for developers to accept card payments with a few lines of code and has become the payment layer for much of the internet. Businesses use it to take cards and local payment methods online and in person, bill customers on subscription plans, send invoices, and pay out sellers on marketplaces. Its documentation and libraries are widely admired, which is why startups tend to adopt it first. It operates in many countries and currencies.

The platform includes products for the rest of the money workflow. Billing handles recurring charges, Connect supports platforms and marketplaces, Tax calculates and collects sales tax, and Issuing creates virtual and physical cards. Radar uses machine learning trained on its payment network to flag fraud, and Terminal handles in-person payments. Stripe has also moved into AI commerce, with tools that let AI agents make purchases and let merchants sell through chat assistants, and many AI companies use Stripe to charge for their own products.

Stripe has no monthly fee for standard use. It charges a percentage plus a fixed amount per transaction, with extra fees for some products and international cards, and custom pricing for large volume. Account holds and sudden reviews can be disruptive for small businesses, a common complaint. PayPal, Adyen, Square, and Braintree are the main competitors, and Paddle and Lemon Squeezy act as merchant of record for software sellers who want tax handled for them.
