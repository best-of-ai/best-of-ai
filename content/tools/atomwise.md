Atomwise applies machine learning to the early stage of drug discovery. Its AtomNet technology uses deep learning to predict how well a small molecule would bind to a protein, which lets researchers screen enormous virtual libraries of compounds before running physical experiments. The idea is to cut down the number of molecules a lab has to test, so teams find promising candidates faster and spend less on dead ends.

The company works through partnerships with pharmaceutical firms, biotech startups, and academic labs, covering many disease targets, including some that have been hard to address with conventional methods. Beyond screening, it has developed methods to search ultra-large chemical spaces and tools for designing and optimizing compounds. It also runs internal programs, so it is both a technology provider and a drug developer.

Atomwise has rebranded as Numerion Labs, and the site now operates under that name. It sells through collaborations and custom agreements, not self-serve software. As with any computational prediction, results need lab confirmation, and success in virtual screening does not guarantee a working drug. Competitors and peers include Schrödinger, Recursion, Isomorphic Labs, and Genesis Therapeutics, each with different strengths in structure-based design and biology.
