Brilliant teaches by making you work through problems. Instead of watching a lecture, you manipulate diagrams, solve puzzles, and answer questions that build up from basic ideas, with immediate feedback. Lessons are short, so it fits into a daily routine. Courses cover topics such as logic, algebra, calculus, probability, statistics, physics, computer science, programming, and, more recently, AI and machine learning concepts.

The learning path is designed to develop intuition before formal rules. It breaks big topics into small steps, uses visuals heavily, and reviews earlier material to help it stick. Adaptive features adjust difficulty based on how you do, and AI features offer hints and explanations when you get stuck. Courses are written by educators and professionals, and they are polished in a way that appeals to curious adults as well as students.

Brilliant has limited free content and a subscription for full access. It is designed to build understanding, not to prepare for a particular exam, so it does not replace a full course or textbook when you need depth. Some learners find advanced topics too shallow. Khan Academy is free and more complete for school subjects, Coursera and edX offer university-style courses, and Duolingo-like gamification is common here. Brilliant works well for building habit and intuition.
