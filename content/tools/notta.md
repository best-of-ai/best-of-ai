Notta turns speech into text for people who sit through many conversations. You can record in the mobile or web app, upload audio and video files, or let its assistant join Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams calls. The transcript appears in the app with speaker labels and timestamps, and you can search it, edit it, and share it. It supports a large number of languages, which suits international teams, journalists, and researchers.

AI features add structure after the recording. Notta produces summaries, action items, and key points, and it can translate transcripts into other languages. Integrations send notes to tools like Notion, Slack, and Google Drive, and meeting templates tailor the summary to the type of meeting. A Chrome extension captures audio from browser-based calls, and some plans support a separate recorder device.

Notta has a free plan with limited minutes and paid plans for longer recordings, more AI features, and team use. Accuracy depends on audio quality and accents, so important quotes should be checked. Otter, Fireflies, and Fathom are common alternatives for meeting notes, Happy Scribe is stronger for subtitles, and Whisper is a free open model for those who can run it. Notta is a reasonable choice if you work in several languages.
