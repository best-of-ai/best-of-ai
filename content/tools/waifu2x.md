Waifu2x was one of the first widely used AI image upscalers. Created for anime and illustration, it uses convolutional neural networks to double the size of an image while reducing noise and compression artifacts. A free web demo made it easy to try, and downloadable versions let people run it locally, including ports that work on many graphics cards through Vulkan. It became a standard tool for enlarging fan art, wallpapers, and screenshots.

Variants support photos as well as artwork, and tools built on it handle batches of images and some video frames. The model's approach influenced a generation of upscalers. It works best on clean, flat-colored illustration and less well on photographs, where it can look smoothed.

Waifu2x is free and open source, and its web demo has had limits on image size and availability. Newer upscalers such as Real-ESRGAN and the app Upscayl produce better results for most photos and detailed art, and commercial tools like Topaz Gigapixel offer extra polish. Waifu2x remains a useful, lightweight choice for anime-style images and a historical milestone in AI image enhancement.
