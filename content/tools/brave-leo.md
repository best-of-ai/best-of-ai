Leo sits in the browser's sidebar and works with whatever page you are viewing. You can ask it to summarize an article or a long video transcript, explain a technical passage, extract key points from a PDF, or answer questions about the content, without copying text into a separate chatbot. It can also help write or rewrite text, translate, brainstorm, and answer general questions, with the option of using the web for current information.

Privacy is its main selling point. Brave states that conversations with Leo are not stored or used to train models, that requests are not linked to your identity, and that no account is needed for basic use. Users can choose among several models, including open-source options and models from other providers, and can connect their own local model, which keeps data on their machine. It is integrated with Brave's browsing, so no extension is required.

Leo has a free tier with limits and a paid subscription that raises the limits and offers access to stronger models. Because it only knows what is on the page or in the chat, it is less capable than a dedicated assistant for long projects. Alternatives built into other browsers include Microsoft Copilot in Edge, Gemini in Chrome, and Perplexity's Comet. For people already using Brave, it is a convenient and privacy-conscious option.
