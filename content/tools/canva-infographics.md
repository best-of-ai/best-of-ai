For infographics, Canva works as a template-first editor. You choose a layout for a process, timeline, comparison, statistic, or report, then replace the placeholder text and swap in your own colors and fonts. The element library supplies icons, illustrations, shapes, and photos, and chart tools let you paste or upload numbers to draw bar charts, pie charts, and other graphs that update when the data changes. Because it is a drag-and-drop tool, people without design training can produce something tidy in under an hour.

AI features reduce the drafting time. Magic Design can build a layout from a short description, Magic Write can draft the copy, and image generation can produce custom illustrations to match a topic. Brand Kits keep your colors, logos, and fonts consistent across graphics, and shared folders let a team reuse templates. Finished designs can be downloaded as images or PDFs, or published as a link.

The free plan includes many infographic templates, while the paid plan adds premium elements, brand controls, and more AI use. Canva is best for marketing, education, and internal reports where speed matters more than a custom visual style. Its charts are basic, so complex or interactive data work is better done in Datawrapper, Flourish, or Tableau. Piktochart and Visme are specialist infographic tools, and Adobe Express is the closest general competitor.
