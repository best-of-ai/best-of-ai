OneTrust helps large organizations manage the rules about data. Its best-known product handles consent: the cookie banners and preference centers that appear on websites and apps and record what visitors agree to. Privacy teams use other modules to respond to data subject requests such as access and deletion, map where personal data is stored, run privacy impact assessments, and show regulators that they follow laws like GDPR and CCPA.

The platform has expanded into broader governance, risk, and compliance. Tools cover third-party and vendor risk, security and IT risk, ethics and policy management, and environmental and social reporting. A growing area is AI governance: inventorying the AI systems an organization uses, assessing their risks, and tracking compliance with emerging rules such as the EU AI Act. AI features in the platform help classify data and automate assessments.

OneTrust is sold to enterprises on custom contracts, with modules priced separately, and implementation can take months. Its breadth can be a drawback for smaller organizations, which may find lighter tools easier. Competitors include Osano, TrustArc, Securiti, BigID, and Usercentrics. Choosing among them often depends on how many regions and regulations you must cover and how much integration with existing systems you need.
