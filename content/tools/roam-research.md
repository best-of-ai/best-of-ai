Roam helped popularize networked note-taking. Notes are organized as outlines of bullet blocks, and you link to other pages by typing double brackets. Every link creates a backlink on the page it points to, so you can see everywhere a concept is mentioned. Daily notes serve as a default home for new thoughts, and over time, links build a web of connected ideas without a rigid folder structure.

Its power comes from working at the block level. Any bullet can be referenced or embedded elsewhere, queries pull together blocks that match conditions, and attributes add structured data to notes. A graph view and a plugin ecosystem add visualization and automation, and keyboard-driven editing makes it fast for those who master it. Researchers, writers, and knowledge workers have used it for long-term thinking.

Roam is a paid subscription, with options such as a lower-priced plan and a one-time purchase for lifetime access in the past. It remains influential, though many users moved to Obsidian, Logseq, and others that offer local storage or free tiers, and its pace of change has been slower than newer competitors. Notion offers a more mainstream structure, Reflect and Capacities provide polished modern designs, and Tana builds on structured nodes. Roam suits people who like outliners and block-level linking.
