Opendoor popularized the iBuyer model. A homeowner enters an address and details, receives an offer within days based on the company's pricing model and an inspection, and can close on a timeline they choose. The company then repairs and resells the home. The process appeals to sellers who value speed and certainty over getting the highest price.

Technology is central to the business. Machine learning models estimate what a home is worth and how long it will take to sell, using data on the property and the local market, and these estimates determine offers and risk. Opendoor also offers listing options for sellers who prefer to sell traditionally, along with buyer services such as touring homes with self-guided access in some markets.

Opendoor charges a service fee, and offers usually come in below what a patient seller might get on the open market, so compare with a traditional listing. The model carries risk for the company when housing markets shift, and Opendoor has experienced financial difficulties and restructurings. Availability is limited to certain cities. Competitors include Offerpad, Zillow's earlier iBuying, which was discontinued, and Redfin or Compass for traditional sales. Opendoor suits sellers who prioritize convenience.
