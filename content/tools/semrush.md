Semrush is among the most widely used toolkits for search marketers. Its core is a big database of keywords and domains, so you can see which terms a competitor ranks for, how much traffic a page gets, who links to it, and where your own site stands. Keyword research, position tracking, site audits, and backlink analysis cover the regular work of search optimization. Separate tools handle paid search, content planning, local listings, and social media.

AI features reach into both the product and the changing search landscape. A writing assistant helps draft and optimize content, and an AI visibility toolkit shows how brands appear in answers from tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google's AI results. Agencies use the reporting tools to build client dashboards, and in-house teams use them to plan content and track competitors.

Semrush is sold by subscription in tiers that limit the number of projects, tracked keywords, and reports, and some modules are add-ons. It is not cheap, and the feature breadth can feel overwhelming to beginners. Adobe completed its acquisition of Semrush, which continues to operate under its own name, and Adobe plans to connect it with its experience and marketing products. Ahrefs is the closest rival and is often favored for backlink data, Moz and SE Ranking are cheaper alternatives, and Google Search Console is free.
