Explainpaper targets a familiar frustration: academic papers are dense and full of jargon. You upload a PDF, select the sentence, equation, or paragraph you do not understand, and the tool gives a plain-language explanation. You can adjust the level, from a lay audience to an expert, and ask follow-up questions about the paper. It was popular with students and curious readers who wanted to follow a paper outside their own field.

The tool is simple on purpose. It does not try to search the literature, manage references, or write summaries of dozens of papers. It stays in the paper you are reading and tries to make that paper clearer. Explanations are generated by a language model using the highlighted text and the surrounding context.

The service has a free tier with limited use and paid plans for more. The risks are the same as with any AI explainer: simplified or incorrect explanations, especially for equations and subtle methods, so anything important should be checked against the paper and its authors. Newer assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini can read an uploaded PDF and explain it, which overlaps with Explainpaper. SciSpace, ChatPDF, and Elicit offer similar or broader features. Check that the service is still active before building a workflow on it.
