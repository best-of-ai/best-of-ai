Catapult makes the small wearable devices that professional teams use to measure what athletes do. A tracker worn in a vest sits against the athlete's upper back and records movement with GPS and inertial sensors, capturing distance, speed, accelerations, impacts, and the intensity of effort. Coaches and sports scientists use the data to see how hard each player is working, compare training with match demands, and adjust loads to reduce the risk of injury.

Software turns the raw measurements into usable insights. Dashboards show individual and team workloads over time, flag athletes who are overworked or undertrained, and support return-to-play planning after injury. Video analysis tools and integrations with other data sources, including heart rate and medical records, build a broader picture. Machine learning models help detect patterns, estimate fatigue, and identify athletes at risk.

Catapult is sold to teams, leagues, universities, and national programs, with pricing that depends on the number of athletes and products, and it is expensive. It is used across football, rugby, basketball, soccer, and many other sports. Predictions about injury remain uncertain, so staff use the data to inform decisions instead of dictating them. Competitors include STATSports, Polar Team Pro, and Zebra Technologies, along with video-based tracking from companies like Second Spectrum.
