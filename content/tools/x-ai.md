x.ai took a distinctive approach to scheduling. Instead of an app or a booking link, you copied its assistant, Amy or Andrew, on an email thread, and it handled the back-and-forth of finding a time with the other people. It read your calendar, proposed times in natural language, replied to the guests, and sent calendar invitations. Because everything happened by email, the other person did not need to install or log in to anything.

The service was an early example of a conversational AI assistant doing a practical task, and it attracted attention for how natural the exchanges seemed. Over time, tools such as scheduling links and calendar features in email clients made the problem easier to solve without an assistant, and the company, which was acquired, wound down the standalone assistants.

For similar tasks today, Calendly and Cal.com offer booking links, Motion and Reclaim schedule tasks automatically around your meetings, and email assistants in Gmail and Outlook suggest times. Newer AI assistants such as Google's Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and ChatGPT can propose meeting times from your calendar, and Zoom and Teams handle scheduling in their own tools. This entry stays for reference because older articles still describe x.ai as a pioneer in AI scheduling.
