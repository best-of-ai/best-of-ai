Doximity is often described as LinkedIn for doctors. Physicians use it as a free directory and communication tool: they can find colleagues, send secure messages and faxes, and call patients using a number that shows the practice's caller ID, all under a verified identity. Many of the country's doctors have profiles, which makes it a convenient way to reach other clinicians for referrals and coordination.

The platform has added tools for clinical work. It provides medical news and journal feeds tailored to specialties, continuing education credits, telehealth video visits, and, increasingly, AI assistants for drafting letters, prior authorization appeals, and notes. A clinical reference tool answers questions drawn from medical sources. Doximity earns most of its revenue from pharmaceutical marketing and recruiting.

Doximity is free for verified clinicians, and the business model depends on advertisers. It is limited to the healthcare professional audience and mainly relevant in the United States. Competitors and alternatives include Sermo, Medscape and UpToDate for reference, OpenEvidence for AI-powered medical search, and general networks such as LinkedIn. Doximity suits clinicians who want communication, news, and AI writing help in one place.
