Acrobat AI Assistant adds a chat panel to the PDF reader most offices already use. Open a contract, report, or research paper, and you can ask for a summary, a list of action items, or an answer to a specific question. Each answer links back to the passage it came from, which makes it practical to check the work instead of trusting it blindly. It handles long files that would take an hour to skim.

It also reaches past a single file. You can point it at a group of PDFs and ask it to compare terms, find differences between versions, or pull the same fact out of each one. Other tools help draft emails or presentations based on a document, and the generated summaries can be shared as a short brief. Because it lives inside Acrobat, the usual PDF tools such as editing, e-signature, and redaction are in the same place.

Adobe sells AI Assistant as an add-on to Acrobat plans rather than as a separate app, and the free version of Reader has limited access. Adobe says it does not use your documents to train its models. Compared with uploading a PDF to ChatGPT or Claude, the draw is convenience and tighter handling of scanned and complex files; the drawback is that you pay for it on top of Acrobat. People who only need an occasional summary can get a similar result from a general chatbot at lower cost.
