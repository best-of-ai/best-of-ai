Okta is one of the best-known providers of cloud identity. Companies use it as the central place where employees sign in to their applications: one login gives access to hundreds of apps through single sign-on, and multi-factor authentication adds protection against stolen passwords. The Universal Directory stores identities and groups, and lifecycle management automates giving people the right access when they join, change roles, or leave.

The platform covers more of the identity problem over time. Adaptive policies check device and context before allowing access, governance tools handle access reviews and requests, and privileged access tools protect administrator accounts. Okta also owns Auth0 for customer-facing apps. As companies deploy AI agents that act on behalf of users, Okta has added features for giving these agents their own identities and managing what they may do.

Okta is sold on subscription, priced per user per month by product, and used by organizations from mid-sized to the largest enterprises. Because identity is a prime target for attackers, Okta itself has been attacked, which is a reminder to use strong controls and monitoring. Microsoft Entra ID is the main competitor, especially for Microsoft-centric companies, while Ping Identity, JumpCloud, and Google Cloud Identity are alternatives. Okta stands out for its neutrality across many vendors' apps.
