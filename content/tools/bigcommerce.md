BigCommerce provides the pieces needed to sell online. A merchant sets up a storefront with themes, manages products and inventory, accepts payments, calculates shipping and tax, and sells across channels such as Amazon, eBay, Google, social platforms, and marketplaces. Many features that other platforms handle through apps, such as product variants, reviews, and discounts, are built in, which keeps costs more predictable.

It stands out in two areas. It supports B2B commerce with features like customer-specific pricing, quotes, and bulk ordering, and it's designed for headless setups in which the storefront is built separately and connected through APIs, which suits brands wanting a custom front end. AI tools help generate product descriptions, support merchandising, and optimize search, and an ecosystem of apps and partners extends the platform.

BigCommerce is paid, with monthly plans that depend on annual sales volume, and no transaction fees of its own on top of payment processing. Compared with Shopify, it has a smaller app and theme ecosystem and community, though it can be cost effective for larger merchants. Alternatives include Shopify, WooCommerce for WordPress, Adobe Commerce, Salesforce Commerce Cloud, and Wix or Squarespace for simpler stores. BigCommerce suits growing and mid-market merchants.
