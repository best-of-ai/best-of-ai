Pl@ntNet lets anyone identify a plant by taking a photo. You photograph a leaf, flower, fruit, or bark, and the app compares it with a large reference collection and returns a list of likely species with confidence scores and similar images. It covers wild plants and many cultivated ones, with regional floras for different parts of the world, and works best when you provide several views of the same plant.

The project is a collaboration among French research organizations and is built on contributions from a global community. Users share observations, other users help confirm identifications, and the data supports scientific research on biodiversity and species distributions. Machine learning models are trained on this growing, validated set of photos, and the data feeds open databases.

Pl@ntNet is free, with optional paid features for extra identifications and no ads, and supports research through its open approach. Identification can be wrong for similar species or poor photos, so edible and medicinal uses should be confirmed by experts. Alternatives include iNaturalist, which covers all living things, PictureThis, Google Lens, and Seek by iNaturalist. Pl@ntNet suits hikers, gardeners, and students interested in plants.
