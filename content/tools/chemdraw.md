Users draw structures, predict properties like NMR shifts, and name compounds from drawings. It integrates with Revvity Signals tools and Microsoft Office. A web version supports sharing.

Licenses are paid, and many universities provide them.

MarvinSketch and Ketcher are alternatives. ChemDraw suits synthetic and medicinal chemists.
