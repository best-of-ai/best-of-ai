Planet Labs built its business on frequency. Rather than a few large satellites, it operates a large number of small ones, so it can photograph almost every part of the land surface regularly. That consistency allows users to watch change over time: crops growing, forests being cleared, ice melting, construction progressing, or ships moving. The imagery has a lower resolution than the sharpest commercial satellites but far greater frequency, and some higher-resolution satellites supplement it.

Beyond raw pictures, Planet offers analytic products. Machine learning detects objects such as buildings, roads, and vessels, tracks land use, and creates measurements of vegetation health and other variables. A platform and APIs let customers access imagery and data, and integrations with GIS tools such as ArcGIS and QGIS make it easy to work with the results. Users include defense and intelligence agencies, agriculture and forestry companies, insurers, mapping firms, and climate researchers.

Planet is a public company that sells through subscriptions and contracts, with pricing depending on area and frequency, and a limited program for researchers and nonprofits. Satellite data analysis needs expertise to interpret, and clouds can obscure images. Competitors include Maxar's successor Vantor, Airbus, BlackSky, Satellogic, and free sources like Sentinel from the European Space Agency. Planet is a leading provider of daily global monitoring.
