Happy Scribe converts speech into text. You upload an audio or video file, or connect a source like Zoom or YouTube, and receive a transcript in minutes with speaker labels and timestamps. It supports many languages and dialects, which suits journalists, researchers, podcasters, and companies that work in several markets. A browser editor lets you listen while you correct the text.

Subtitling is a major use. The service can generate subtitles and captions for videos, translate them into other languages, and export in standard formats for YouTube, Vimeo, and video editors. For cases where accuracy matters more, such as legal or broadcast work, you can order human-made transcripts and subtitles, which cost more and take longer. An API and integrations connect it to workflows.

Happy Scribe is paid, billed by the minute or through subscriptions, with a free trial. Automatic transcription is fast and good for clear audio but degrades with accents, crosstalk, and noise, so important content needs review. Otter and Fireflies focus on meeting notes, Rev offers a similar mix of AI and human transcription, Descript adds editing, and Whisper is a free open model for those who can run it themselves. Happy Scribe is a good fit for multilingual subtitle work.
