Deepdub works with studios, streamers, and media companies that need content in many languages. Its technology translates the dialogue and generates new speech in the target language, in a voice that matches the original performer, with timing that fits the picture. The goal is dubbing that carries over emotion and character, not the flat voice of a basic text-to-speech engine. It supports over a hundred languages and uses licensed voices, which matters for rights and union considerations.

For production use, the company adds controls for professional workflows. Terminology glossaries keep names and phrases consistent, human reviewers can edit scripts and performances, and the infrastructure is built to meet privacy requirements such as GDPR. Speech-to-speech and voice cloning let one performance be converted into another voice, and an API provides text-to-speech for voice agents and other products.

Deepdub is sold to enterprises on custom contracts. Customers have included studios and distributors of films and television. AI dubbing reduces cost and speeds up localization, but it raises concerns among voice actors about work and consent, and many productions still use human review to protect quality. ElevenLabs offers dubbing for creators and companies, Papercup, HeyGen, and Rask serve other segments, and traditional dubbing studios remain common for premium content.
