Inworld AI began by giving non-player characters in games personality, memory, and the ability to converse. Developers define a character's background, goals, and knowledge, and the platform handles dialogue, emotion, and behavior using language models, so players can talk to characters freely instead of choosing from scripted options. Integrations with Unity and Unreal Engine make it easy to add to games.

The company has evolved toward infrastructure for real-time AI applications. Its runtime helps developers build and scale voice and conversational experiences with low latency, and it offers text-to-speech models and tools for combining speech recognition, language models, and voice into agents. Use cases extend from games to training simulations, companions, and consumer apps with talking characters.

Inworld is paid, with developer tiers and enterprise plans, and a free allowance for experimentation. Putting AI characters in games raises costs for model inference, content moderation, and quality control, and designers must keep conversations on track. Alternatives include Convai, Charisma.ai, Character.AI's tools, NVIDIA ACE for game characters, and ElevenLabs or Cartesia for voice. Inworld suits game developers and teams building conversational characters.
