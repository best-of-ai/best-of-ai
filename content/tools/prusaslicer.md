PrusaSlicer converts 3D models into the instructions a printer follows. You load a model, arrange it on the bed, choose a print profile for your printer and material, and the slicer generates the toolpaths, previewing each layer. It started as a fork of Slic3r and is developed by Prusa Research, which makes well-known 3D printers, and it is open source under the AGPL.

It's valued for depth and control. Features include variable layer height, tree and organic supports, multi-material and multi-color printing, modifiers and custom settings for parts of a model, and a calibration toolkit. It supports printers from many manufacturers through profiles and a plugin-like configuration system, and has a mode that hides advanced settings for beginners. Community forks such as OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio build on it.

PrusaSlicer is free, with regular updates and strong documentation. The number of settings can overwhelm new users, and AI features are not a focus. Alternatives include Ultimaker Cura, OrcaSlicer, Bambu Studio for Bambu printers, Simplify3D as a paid option, and SuperSlicer. PrusaSlicer suits makers who want a capable, open slicer for many printers.
