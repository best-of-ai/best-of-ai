Rosetta Stone teaches by immersion. Instead of translating from your own language, lessons pair images with words and phrases in the target language, so you learn by association, the way children pick up a first language. Lessons build from simple vocabulary to conversation, with exercises on listening, reading, writing, and speaking. It has been a recognized name in language learning for decades and sells courses in about two dozen languages.

Speech recognition is a distinctive feature. It listens to your pronunciation and gives feedback, which helps with accent and rhythm, and live tutoring sessions are available in some plans. Mobile apps let you study anywhere and download lessons. The company owned by IXL Learning also provides programs for schools, businesses, and government, and has added newer interactive and AI-driven practice.

Rosetta Stone is paid, with subscriptions of varying lengths and a lifetime option that grants access to all languages. Immersion without explanations frustrates some adult learners who want grammar rules spelled out, and the content can feel slow compared with newer apps. Babbel gives more explicit instruction, Duolingo is free and game-like, Pimsleur focuses on audio, and Busuu includes community feedback. Rosetta Stone suits learners who enjoy the immersive method.
