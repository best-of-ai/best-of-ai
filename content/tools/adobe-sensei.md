Sensei is not an app you open. It is the label Adobe uses for the AI and machine learning that runs inside its products, from Photoshop and Premiere Pro to Lightroom, Acrobat, and the Experience Cloud marketing tools. When Photoshop selects a subject in one click, Premiere reframes a video for vertical, or Lightroom masks a sky automatically, that is Sensei doing the work. Many of these features arrived before generative AI became popular, and they handle analysis and automation more than image creation.

Newer generative tools come from Adobe's Firefly models, which sit beside Sensei in the same apps. The split is mostly about purpose: Sensei covers things like detecting objects, matching colors, tagging assets, and predicting customer behavior, while Firefly generates new images, text effects, and video. In Adobe's business products, Sensei-based features handle audience segmentation, journey analytics, and content recommendations for marketing teams.

You cannot buy Sensei on its own. It comes with the Creative Cloud, Document Cloud, and Experience Cloud products that use it, and it is covered by their subscription. Adobe states that features run with permission and clear rules on data use, and processing happens in the cloud or on the device depending on the feature. For anyone comparing AI tools, the useful question is not whether a product uses Sensei but which specific feature in Photoshop, Premiere, or Lightroom saves you time.
