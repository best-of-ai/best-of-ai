Zillow is where many people begin looking for a home. Listings for sale and rent appear on maps and lists with photos, price history, taxes, and school details, and users can filter by price, size, and features, save searches, and get alerts when matching homes appear. Agents and landlords pay to advertise or manage listings.

Its signature feature is the Zestimate, an automated estimate of a home's value based on public records, market data, and a machine learning model. It is useful as a rough guide but can be off for individual homes, and sellers should not treat it as an appraisal. Zillow also offers mortgage and agent referral services, rental tools, and an AI-powered search that accepts natural language descriptions.

Zillow is free for consumers, and makes money from advertising and referrals. Listing data comes from many sources and can lag or contain errors, so confirm details with an agent or the listing broker. Alternatives include Redfin, Realtor.com, Trulia (owned by Zillow), Homes.com, and local MLS sites. Zillow suits buyers, renters, and sellers researching a market.
