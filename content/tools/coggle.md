Coggle keeps mind mapping simple. In the browser, you start with a central idea and add branches by clicking, using colors, images, and text, and the diagram grows into a tidy tree. It can also draw flowcharts, with free-form connections between nodes, so the same tool covers brainstorming, note taking, planning, and basic process diagrams. The look is clean, with auto-arranged branches that make diagrams easy to read.

Collaboration is a core feature. Several people can edit the same diagram at once and see each other's changes, and a full history lets you review and restore earlier versions. Diagrams can include notes and images, be shared by link, and be exported as PDF, image, or text outlines. Students, teachers, and small teams use it for study notes, lesson plans, and project outlines.

Coggle has a free plan with a limited number of private diagrams and paid plans that add unlimited private diagrams, downloads, and sharing controls. It has fewer advanced features and no strong AI capabilities compared with larger rivals. MindMeister offers more presentation and task features, XMind has more diagram styles, Miro and Whimsical are broader visual tools, and Obsidian canvases suit note makers. Coggle suits people who want an easy shared mind map.
