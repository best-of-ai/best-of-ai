Polly takes text and returns audio, which developers call from their apps to add speech. It offers dozens of voices across many languages and accents, built on several engines. The neural voices sound considerably more natural than the older standard ones, and newer generative voices push realism further. It is used for reading articles aloud, voicing phone systems and assistants, creating e-learning narration, and adding accessibility features to apps.

Control over how things are said is a strength. SSML tags let you adjust pauses, emphasis, pitch, speed, and the way numbers, dates, and abbreviations are spoken. Custom lexicons teach Polly the right pronunciation of brand names and jargon. It can also return speech marks that tell your app when each word is spoken, which is useful for highlighting text in sync with audio. Output can be streamed or saved as files in common formats.

Pricing is per character converted, with different rates for different voice types, and there is a free allowance for the first year of use. At scale this is cheap, and it fits easily into other AWS services. The voices are good but less expressive than those from newer specialists such as ElevenLabs, and Polly has no voice cloning of the kind many creators want. It is a practical, dependable choice for applications that need a lot of speech at predictable cost.
