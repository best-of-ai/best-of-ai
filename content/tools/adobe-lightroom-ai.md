Lightroom is built for photographers who handle many images. It organizes a library, lets you rate and filter, and edits photos non-destructively with sliders for exposure, color, tone, and detail. Edits can be copied across a whole shoot, which saves hours after a wedding or event. Lightroom syncs between desktop, phone, and the web, while Lightroom Classic is the desktop-only version with a more traditional file-based workflow.

AI has changed the way people edit in it. Masking tools select the subject, sky, background, or people with a click, so you can adjust one part of a photo without painting a selection. Denoise cleans up high-ISO images while preserving detail, Generative Remove deletes distractions, and Lens Blur adds realistic background blur. Adaptive presets adjust to the content of each photo, and auto-edit settings give a starting point.

Lightroom is sold through Adobe's Photography plan and other Creative Cloud subscriptions, with cloud storage included, and the mobile app has a free tier. Some AI features use cloud processing or generative credits. Capture One is the main professional rival, known for color and tethered shooting, and Darktable is a free open-source option. For people who also need Photoshop, the bundle is a common starting point.
