Databricks started with Apache Spark and grew into a unified platform for data work. Companies store data in open formats in their cloud, then use Databricks for pipelines, SQL analytics, notebooks, and machine learning, with a governance layer called Unity Catalog that controls access and tracks lineage across it all.

AI has become central to the pitch. The platform supports training and fine-tuning models, serving them through endpoints, vector search for retrieval, and a framework for building agents grounded in enterprise data. The company acquired MosaicML to strengthen model training, and offers tools to monitor and evaluate AI systems. An assistant helps write code and queries.

Databricks is usage-based and works on AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud. It is powerful but complex, and costs need monitoring. Alternatives include Snowflake, Google BigQuery, Microsoft Fabric, Amazon Redshift, and Dataiku. Databricks suits data and AI teams working with large data.
