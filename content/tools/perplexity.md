Perplexity works more like a search engine than a chatbot. You ask a question, it searches the web in real time, and it writes a short answer with numbered citations you can click to check. That makes it good for current events, product comparisons, and fact-finding where you need to see where the information came from. Follow-up questions keep the context, and you can narrow searches to sources like academic papers or forums.

For bigger questions there is Deep Research, which runs many searches, reads the results, and compiles a structured report in a few minutes. Spaces let you group related searches, files, and custom instructions into a project, and Labs can produce reports, spreadsheets, and simple apps. Comet is Perplexity's browser, with an assistant built in that can summarize pages and take actions for you across tabs. Pro users can pick among several models from different providers, including Perplexity's own Sonar models.

The free plan covers basic searches, and paid plans raise the limits on deeper research and advanced models. A developer API gives access to the same search-grounded models. Citations are the main strength, but they are not a guarantee: the answer can still misread a source or lean on a weak one, so it is worth opening the links for anything important. Publishers have also criticized how Perplexity uses their content, which is an ongoing dispute. For quick, sourced research it is one of the better tools available.
