Turnitin compares student submissions against a large database of papers, publications, and web content, producing a similarity report for instructors. It integrates with systems such as Canvas, Blackboard, and Moodle, so checking fits into grading.

An AI writing indicator estimates how much of a submission may be AI generated. AI detection is imperfect: false positives and negatives occur, and results are best used as a prompt for conversation, not proof. Feedback and grading tools add other functions.

Turnitin is sold to institutions, not individuals. Student privacy and the retention of submitted papers raise questions that institutions address in policy. Alternatives include Copyleaks, GPTZero, Grammarly's checker, Scribbr, and Originality.ai. Turnitin suits schools and universities.
