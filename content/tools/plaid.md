Plaid connects apps to bank accounts. When you link an account in a budgeting app, investment platform, or payments service, Plaid's interface asks you to sign in to your bank, and the app then receives only the data you permit. Developers use Plaid's APIs to verify account ownership, check balances, read transaction history, confirm identity, assess income and assets for lending, and initiate transfers, without building separate integrations with thousands of banks.

The company has expanded beyond account linking. Products cover payments, fraud and identity checks, credit underwriting data, and one-click sign-up flows that reuse a user's verified information. Machine learning categorizes transactions and detects patterns, and it works with banks to move connections to secure, tokenized APIs in place of screen scraping. Open banking rules in various countries shape how it operates.

Plaid charges developers by product and usage, with a limited free tier for development, and enterprise terms for large customers. Users should understand what data they share and can manage connections through Plaid's portal. Privacy practices and past legal settlements make transparency important. Competitors include MX, Finicity from Mastercard, Yodlee, Tink in Europe, and TrueLayer. Plaid suits fintech developers building apps that need access to bank data.
