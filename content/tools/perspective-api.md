Perspective used machine learning to help moderate online conversations. A site sent a comment to the API and received scores for attributes such as toxicity, insult, profanity, and threat, which publishers and platforms used to flag comments for review, warn commenters before posting, or sort discussions. News organizations and forums used it to cope with large numbers of comments, and researchers used it to study online abuse.

The tool also sparked debate. Studies found that its scores could be biased, for example by rating comments mentioning certain identity terms as more toxic, and it was not a substitute for human moderators. Jigsaw acknowledged limitations and worked to reduce bias, and the model was widely used in research as a benchmark for toxicity.

Jigsaw stopped accepting new requests and announced that it will end the service. Operators that rely on it need to migrate to other moderation tools, such as OpenAI's moderation endpoint, Azure AI Content Safety, Hive, Sightengine, or open models for toxicity classification, along with human review. This entry stays for reference because many moderation guides and research papers mention Perspective.
