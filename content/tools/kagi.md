Kagi takes a different business model from other search engines: users pay a subscription, and in return there are no ads, no tracking-based profiles, and no incentive to favor advertisers. Results are meant to be clean and relevant, and the company says it does not sell data. The service uses several sources, including its own index of smaller sites, and puts effort into reducing low-quality content and SEO spam.

Control is a defining feature. Users can raise, lower, or block domains, so a site that is always useless for you disappears, and lenses restrict a search to a set of sources, such as forums or academic sites. AI tools include summaries of pages and videos, answers with citations, and an assistant that gives access to multiple language models. Kagi has also launched Orion, a privacy-focused browser, and other products around the search experience.

Kagi has a limited trial and then requires a paid plan, with tiers based on the number of searches or an unlimited option. That cost is the main barrier compared with free alternatives. Its index is smaller than Google's, so it sometimes misses niche results, though it can pull in other sources. Alternatives include Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Brave Search, and AI-first options like Perplexity. Kagi appeals to people who want control and are willing to pay for quality.
