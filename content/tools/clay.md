Clay works like a spreadsheet for sales and marketing data. You start with a list of companies or people, and Clay adds columns that look up information, such as emails, job titles, funding, technologies used, and recent news, pulling from many data providers. Its waterfall enrichment tries one source after another until it finds a result, which improves coverage over relying on a single vendor.

The AI part is a web research agent that can visit sites and answer questions you define for each row, for example whether a company has a mobile app or what it sells. Results feed into AI-written emails and messages tailored to each prospect, and workflows push the finished leads into CRMs and outreach tools like HubSpot, Salesforce, Smartlead, or Outreach. Growth teams use it to build very specific lists and trigger outreach when signals appear.

Clay is paid, with plans based on credits that each enrichment and AI action consumes, and costs scale with how much data you pull. It has a learning curve, and the flexibility can lead to complex tables. Sending high volumes of cold messages carries deliverability and compliance risks that the tool does not remove. Apollo and ZoomInfo are data-first alternatives, Amplemarket combines data and outreach, and Lusha offers simpler contact lookup.
