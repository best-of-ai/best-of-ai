Capacities rethinks how a notes app is structured. Instead of one kind of page, everything you capture is an object with a type, such as a book, a person, a meeting, an article, or a project, and each type can have its own properties and template. You link objects together, so a book can connect to the people who recommended it and the ideas it inspired. The result is a graph of your knowledge that is easier to browse than a pile of untitled notes.

Daily notes are a central place to capture thoughts, with the option to link to other objects as you write. Backlinks, block references, tags, and a graph view show how information relates, and a visual layout helps with browsing. The AI assistant can answer questions about your notes, summarize content, and help find connections, using information from your own workspace.

The free plan includes unlimited spaces, objects, and blocks, and a paid Pro plan adds extra storage, AI features, and more advanced options. Capacities is newer and smaller than Notion or Obsidian, so it has fewer integrations and plugins, and the object model takes time to learn. Obsidian stores local Markdown files, Notion offers databases and team features, Logseq is open source, and Tana takes a similar object-based approach. It suits individuals who value structure in their personal knowledge.
