Arc reimagined the browser. Instead of a row of tabs across the top, it uses a vertical sidebar where tabs are grouped into spaces, such as work and personal, each with its own pinned items and theme. Tabs that you do not use are archived automatically, and features like split view, boosts for customizing websites, and easels for collecting ideas made it a favorite of power users and designers. It is built on Chromium, so most Chrome extensions work.

Some AI features were added around search and browsing: summarizing pages, renaming downloads, and Arc Search on iPhone, which compiles an answer from several pages. The company then turned its attention to Dia, a browser with an AI assistant at its center, and moved Arc to maintenance mode. That means security and compatibility updates continue, but major new features are not planned.

The Browser Company is now owned by Atlassian, which plans to develop Dia as an AI browser for work. Arc remains free to use and still works well, though its long-term future depends on how Atlassian proceeds. Alternatives with similar ideas include Zen, Vivaldi, and Opera, while Dia, Perplexity's Comet, and ChatGPT's Atlas are AI-centered browsers. Anyone starting fresh with a browser built around vertical tabs and spaces has other active choices.
