Gretel helps teams get usable data when real data is sensitive or scarce. Developers connect a dataset, such as customer records or medical tables, and Gretel trains a model that produces new records with the same statistical patterns but no direct link to real individuals. Teams use the results to test software, share data across departments, and train machine learning models without waiting for privacy approvals. It also generates text and tabular data for fine-tuning language models.

The platform pairs generation with checks. Quality reports compare the synthetic data with the original, and privacy tools such as differential privacy and filters reduce the risk that the output leaks real information. Transformation features can detect and mask personal data in existing records. APIs and open-source libraries let developers add these steps to their pipelines.

According to press reports, NVIDIA acquired Gretel to strengthen its tools for generating training data, and the technology is being folded into NVIDIA's services.
