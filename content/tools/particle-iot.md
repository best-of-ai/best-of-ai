Particle gives hardware teams a full stack for connected devices. It sells development boards and production-ready modules with cellular, Wi-Fi, or Bluetooth connectivity, runs its own device operating system, and provides a cloud console to monitor devices, send commands, and update firmware over the air. Because connectivity, SIM management, and cloud services come bundled, a small team can move from prototype to production without assembling the parts separately.

The platform emphasizes fleet management at scale. Dashboards show device health and data usage, alerts flag problems, and tools support provisioning, remote diagnostics, and secure updates. Webhooks and integrations send data to cloud services and applications, and newer products add more powerful edge computing, including boards that run Android and machine learning workloads at the edge.

Particle has a free tier for small experiments and paid plans based on the number of devices and data usage, plus the cost of hardware. It competes with building directly on microcontrollers and cloud services from AWS or Azure, which offers more flexibility but more work. Alternatives include Hologram and Soracom for connectivity, Blues for cellular modules, and Golioth and Memfault for device management. Particle suits startups and product teams building connected hardware.
