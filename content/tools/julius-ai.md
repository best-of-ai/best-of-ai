Julius lets people explore data by asking questions. After uploading a CSV or Excel file, or connecting to a database or Google Sheets, you can ask for things like the average sales by region, a trend over time, or a correlation between two columns. Julius writes and runs code behind the scenes, then shows the result as a chart, a table, or a written explanation, and you can view the code to check what it did.

It supports typical analyst tasks: cleaning messy data, running statistical tests, building forecasts, and creating visualizations that can be exported. Notebook-style features save analyses so they can be repeated or shared, and scheduled reports update when the data changes. It's aimed at people who understand their data but don't want to write code, as well as analysts who want to move faster.

Julius has a free tier with limited usage and paid plans with more compute and features. AI can misinterpret questions or make statistical errors, so results should be validated, especially for important decisions, and uploading sensitive data requires trust in the vendor. Alternatives include ChatGPT's data analysis feature, Claude with analysis tools, Hex, Power BI Copilot, Tableau Agent, and Excel Copilot. Julius suits non-programmers who want quick data answers.
