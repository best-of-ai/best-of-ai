Edge Impulse brings machine learning to small devices. An engineer collects data from sensors such as accelerometers, microphones, and cameras, labels it, designs a processing pipeline, trains a model in the browser, and exports an optimized version that runs on a microcontroller or edge processor. Applications include detecting equipment faults, recognizing keywords, and spotting objects.

The platform supports a wide range of hardware and shows estimates of memory use and latency before deployment, which matters when resources are tiny. Tools help with data collection and testing, and integrations connect to major chip vendors. Qualcomm acquired the company to strengthen its edge AI offering.

Edge Impulse has a free tier for developers and paid plans for enterprises. Embedded ML requires domain knowledge about sensors and signal processing. Alternatives include TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers, Roboflow for vision, Nota AI, and chip vendors' own toolchains. Edge Impulse suits teams building intelligent edge devices.
