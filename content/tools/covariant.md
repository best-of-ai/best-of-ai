Covariant started in robotics research with a goal of building a general model that could control robots in the physical world. Its first big application was robotic picking, in which a robot arm takes products from bins and places them into orders. That task is hard because warehouses contain thousands of different items with varied shapes, materials, and packaging. Covariant Brain, its AI system, was trained on data from many deployments to deal with that variety, instead of being programmed for each product.

The company worked with fulfillment and distribution customers, deploying robots that learned from shared experience, and it published research on robotics foundation models that combine vision, language, and action. It positioned itself as an AI company that could power robots from various manufacturers rather than as a hardware maker.

Amazon hired several of Covariant's founders and a portion of its staff and took a license to its technology, and Covariant said it would continue serving customers under new leadership. The arrangement was described as a form of acqui-hire, and it has attracted scrutiny. Anyone considering Covariant today should confirm the company's current offerings and support directly. Competitors in AI-driven picking include RightHand Robotics, Berkshire Grey, Dexterity, Plus One Robotics, and Ocado's systems.
