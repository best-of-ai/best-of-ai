Ada guides users through a structured conversation about their symptoms. It asks about what you feel, when it started, your medical history, and related factors, adapting each question based on earlier answers, and then shows possible conditions with likelihoods and recommendations such as self-care, seeing a doctor, or seeking urgent help. The app was developed with doctors and is available in several languages.

The consumer app is free to use, and Ada also licenses its technology to health systems and insurers, who embed it in their own services for triage and navigation. The aim is to help people understand their symptoms, avoid unnecessary visits, and know when something needs prompt attention. Users can share a summary with their clinician.

Ada is not a diagnosis tool and doesn't replace a medical professional. Accuracy in symptom checkers varies and studies have found mixed results, so use it as one input and seek care when in doubt or in an emergency. Privacy settings and data use should be reviewed since health information is sensitive. Alternatives include Symptomate, K Health, WebMD's symptom checker, and general AI assistants, which can be less structured and reliable. Ada suits people who want a guided starting point.
