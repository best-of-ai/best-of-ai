Wordtune is built around rewriting. Highlight a sentence in your draft and it offers several alternatives: shorter, longer, more casual, or more formal. You pick the one that sounds right and keep your own voice instead of pasting in a block of generated text. That makes it more of an editor's tool than a text generator, which suits people who already know what they want to say and need help phrasing it.

Other features support the same flow. A summarizer condenses articles, PDFs, and YouTube videos into key points. A grammar and spelling check catches mistakes, and a Spices feature suggests extra material to strengthen a point, such as a statistic, a question, or an analogy. A browser extension brings these options into Gmail, LinkedIn, and other sites, and it works in Microsoft Word and Google Docs. Wordtune is aimed at professionals writing emails, reports, and posts, including people who write in English as a second language.

There is a free plan with a limited number of rewrites per day and a paid plan that removes the limits and adds longer summaries and more tone options. Rewrites can flatten distinctive writing or change nuance, so they should be read carefully. Grammarly and QuillBot are the closest alternatives, and general chatbots such as ChatGPT can do the same job with more flexibility. Wordtune is a good choice if you want quick suggestions with no prompts.
