Piktochart is aimed at people who need to present information visually but are not designers. You choose a template for an infographic, report, presentation, or poster, edit the text and images, and add charts and maps that you can link to data. Visual blocks, icons, illustrations, and a library of graphics make it quick to produce clean results, and the tool exports to image and PDF formats or shares online.

AI features reduce the work of getting started. An assistant can create a draft infographic or presentation from a prompt or a document, suggest layouts, and rewrite text, and image generation tools produce custom visuals. Brand kits keep colors, fonts, and logos consistent, and team plans allow collaboration and shared templates. Educators, nonprofits, marketers, and communicators are typical users.

Piktochart has a free plan with limits and paid plans for more features and brand controls, with discounts for education and nonprofits. It is less flexible for custom design than Canva or Adobe Express, and the template look can feel familiar. Alternatives include Canva, Visme, Venngage, Infogram, and Adobe Express. Piktochart suits people who want fast, tidy infographics and reports.
