Ahrefs is best known for its backlink data. Its crawler maps links across the web, so you can look at any site and see who links to it, which pages draw the most traffic, and what keywords bring visitors. Site Explorer is the tool most people open first, and it is often used to study a competitor's strategy. Keywords Explorer suggests search terms and estimates how hard each one would be to rank for.

The rest of the toolset covers the regular work of SEO. Site Audit crawls your own pages and flags technical problems such as broken links, slow pages, and duplicate content. Rank Tracker follows your positions over time, and Content Explorer finds popular articles in a topic. Brand Radar is a newer addition that tracks how often a brand is mentioned in AI-generated answers from tools such as ChatGPT and Google's AI results. That matters as more people ask chatbots instead of using search.

Ahrefs sells subscription plans in tiers based on usage limits and the number of users, plus an enterprise tier with extra security. Free webmaster tools let site owners check their own site for basic issues. It is considered one of the most accurate SEO platforms, but it is not cheap, and smaller sites may find the paid plans hard to justify. Semrush is its closest competitor, and Moz and Screaming Frog cover narrower needs.
