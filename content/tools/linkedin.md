LinkedIn is the main place for professional identity online. Members create profiles that work as living resumes, connect with colleagues and contacts, follow companies, and share posts and articles. It is also a major channel for hiring and job hunting, with job listings, recruiter messaging, and tools that show how you compare with other applicants. Companies maintain pages and advertise to professional audiences.

AI features run through the product. Job search can be done in plain language, writing assistance helps draft posts and profile sections, and an AI hiring assistant helps recruiters find and screen candidates. Premium subscriptions add insights, more messages, and learning courses through LinkedIn Learning. Skills assessments, recommendations, and endorsements are used to signal ability. The feed is ranked by algorithms that favor engagement.

LinkedIn is free to use with paid tiers for job seekers, recruiters, and sales professionals. Its value depends on network size and activity, and some people dislike the performative tone of its feed. Data privacy has drawn scrutiny, including how member content is used for AI training, and settings let users limit that. Scams and fake profiles are a continuing problem, so be careful with unsolicited offers. Indeed, Glassdoor, and Wellfound are alternatives for jobs, and X and Reddit serve other kinds of professional conversation.
