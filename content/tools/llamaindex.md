LlamaIndex started as an open-source library for connecting language models to private data, and that remains its core job. It helps load information from files, databases, APIs, and apps such as Notion and Slack, split it into pieces, index it, and retrieve the relevant parts when a user asks a question. This retrieval-augmented generation pattern is how many company chatbots answer from internal documents instead of from the model's general knowledge.

The company's attention has moved toward document processing and agents. LlamaParse is a hosted service that turns messy PDFs, tables, slides, and scans into clean text and structured data, with tools to extract fields, split and classify documents, and build an index. A local, open-source parser is also available. Workflows is a framework for building agents and multi-step processes, and LlamaCloud packages the managed services for teams that do not want to run everything themselves.

The open-source framework is free. The cloud services use credits, with a free monthly allowance and paid plans, plus enterprise options with private cloud deployment and support. Compared with LangChain, LlamaIndex is more focused on data ingestion and retrieval, though the two overlap. Accuracy on messy documents depends on parsing quality, which is where LlamaIndex puts its effort. Unstructured, Haystack, and Azure Document Intelligence are the other options for document-heavy AI work.
