Iris.ai aims to reduce the time scientists spend finding and reading relevant papers. Instead of keyword searches, a researcher can input a problem description or an abstract, and the system finds related papers by meaning. It then helps explore the results with a visual map of topics, filters based on content, and tools that extract data and summarize findings across many documents. Research teams use it to scan a field, check for prior work, and decide where to focus.

The platform is oriented toward enterprise research workflows. It can search internal documents alongside public databases, which lets a company combine its own reports and patents with the published literature. Tools for reading and extraction pull out key facts, methods, and results, and workflow features help teams document their search process and share findings. The company has described its technology as suited to specialized scientific language.

Iris.ai is sold to organizations on custom contracts, with no broad consumer tier, and it is aimed at R&D-heavy companies and research institutions. Results depend on the quality of the underlying document collections, and automatic summaries should be checked against the papers. Elicit, Consensus, and SciSpace serve individual researchers more directly, Semantic Scholar offers free search, and enterprise alternatives include Elsevier's tools and Clarivate. It suits large teams managing a lot of literature.
