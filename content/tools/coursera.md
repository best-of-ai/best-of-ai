Coursera partners with universities and companies to offer online learning at scale. Learners can take individual courses, follow multi-course specializations and professional certificates from names like Google, IBM, and Meta, or enroll in full online degrees. Material combines video lectures, readings, quizzes, and graded assignments, and many courses can be audited for free. Completed programs earn certificates that people add to resumes and LinkedIn.

AI has become part of the platform. A learning assistant answers questions about course content, gives feedback, and helps with practice, and tools help instructors and organizations build and translate courses faster. Skills-based recommendations suggest what to learn next based on your goals. The platform also offers business and government offerings, where employers assign programs and track progress.

Coursera has merged with Udemy, the course marketplace, which now operates as part of the company. Coursera has free access to many course materials and paid options for certificates, graded work, and degrees, including a subscription that covers a large catalog. The quality varies from course to course, and completion rates for online courses tend to be low unless learners have a clear goal. A certificate does not guarantee a job. edX, Udacity, Udemy, and LinkedIn Learning are alternatives, with Udemy leaning toward practical, lower-priced courses and edX toward university content. Coursera suits people who want structure and recognized credentials.
