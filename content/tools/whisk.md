Whisk started as an independent app for collecting recipes. You could save dishes from any food site into one library, group them into collections, plan meals for the week, and turn the ingredients into a shopping list that could be sent to grocery delivery services. A browser extension made saving a recipe a single click.

Samsung bought the company and renamed the app Samsung Food, and the whisk.com address now leads to the new brand. The app keeps the same core features and adds AI help, such as adjusting a recipe to dietary needs, suggesting meals from what you have, and generating plans. It also connects with Samsung's smart appliances, though it works without them on phones and the web.

Samsung Food is free to use, with an optional paid tier for extra features. There is an unrelated Google Labs experiment also called Whisk, which is for image generation, so do not mix the two up. Alternatives include Paprika, Mealime, Yummly, SideChef, and NYT Cooking. Whisk, in its current form as Samsung Food, suits people who want a free recipe organizer and meal planner.
