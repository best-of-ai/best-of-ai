Glean addresses the problem that company knowledge lives in dozens of separate tools. It connects to those systems through hundreds of connectors, builds an index of documents, messages, tickets, and other content, and lets employees search across everything from one box. Crucially, it mirrors the permissions of the source systems, so a person only sees results they would already have access to.

On top of search, Glean provides an AI assistant that answers questions in natural language using company information, with citations to the underlying sources. Employees ask things like how a process works, what was decided in a project, or what the latest customer feedback says, and receive a synthesized answer. Glean Agents let teams build automations and agents that take actions in connected apps, and a builder allows non-engineers to create them. Companies can choose among language models from several providers.

Glean is sold to enterprises on custom contracts, typically per user, and deployment involves security review and connector setup. It works best in organizations with enough content that finding information is a real cost. Microsoft 365 Copilot, Google Gemini Enterprise, and ChatGPT and Claude with enterprise connectors are the main alternatives, and tools like Guru and Coveo cover narrower knowledge needs. The quality of answers depends on the quality and organization of the underlying content.
