Dream Machine stands out in the AI video space for how well it handles lighting. Generated clips have specular highlights, soft shadows, and ambient occlusion that makes them look like they were filmed rather than synthesised — a quality that immediately reads as higher budget when compared to clips from generators that skip those physical details. The motion is smooth, with natural camera movement options including slow push-ins, orbital shots, and handheld styles.

The image-to-video mode is particularly useful for creative work. Upload any image — a photograph, a rendering, an illustration — and describe how you want it to move. A portrait photograph can become a subtle breath-and-blink clip. A product render can be put on a rotating turntable. A landscape photo can have clouds moving across it. This makes Dream Machine practical for bringing still assets to life without re-creating them in a 3D environment.

The free tier provides five video generations per day with watermarks and a queue wait time. Standard plans at $9.99/month and Pro at $29.99/month increase the monthly generation limit, remove watermarks, and give priority queue access. Clips can be up to five seconds on the free tier and up to ten seconds on paid plans. Luma is used heavily in advertising concept work, music videos, social media content, and anywhere that cinematic-quality short video clips are needed without the cost of a camera crew and location shoot.
