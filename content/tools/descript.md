Descript turns a video or audio recording into a text document and lets you edit the media by editing the transcript. Delete a sentence from the text and the corresponding audio/video is cut. Rearrange paragraphs and the clip rearranges. This single idea dramatically speeds up the editing process for spoken-word content — instead of scrubbing through a timeline, you find the words you want to cut and just delete them.

Studio Sound removes background noise, equalises the audio, and produces a noticeably cleaner recording from a laptop mic. Overdub uses your voice clone to fix mispronounced words or insert new sentences into an existing recording — useful for correcting mistakes after the fact without re-recording. The filler word remover finds every "um," "uh," and "you know" in the transcript and offers to remove them all at once. Screen recording, teleprompter mode, and multi-track audio editing are all built into the same application.

Descript is free for up to one hour of transcription per month. The Creator plan at $15/month removes that cap and unlocks Overdub voice cloning and Studio Sound. Pro adds watermark-free export, collaboration tools, and higher quality AI features. Podcasters, video essayists, educators, and remote-first teams use Descript most heavily — essentially anyone producing a lot of spoken-word content where editing speed matters. The text-based editing paradigm doesn't suit every workflow, but for people who adopt it, going back to a traditional timeline editor feels slow.
