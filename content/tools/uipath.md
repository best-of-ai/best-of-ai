UiPath popularized robotic process automation, in which software robots mimic what a person does on a screen. Developers build automations in a studio by recording and arranging steps such as clicking, copying data between systems, and filling forms, and run them on servers or desktops. This is valuable for companies with legacy systems that lack APIs, since bots can work through the same interfaces people use.

The platform has expanded into AI. Document understanding extracts data from invoices and forms, process mining reveals which workflows are worth automating, and agent tools let AI agents plan and carry out tasks, orchestrated alongside robots and people. The Maestro orchestration layer coordinates agents, robots, and human steps, and governance features control access and auditing.

UiPath is sold to enterprises on subscription, with a free community edition for learning and small use. Large deployments need investment in design and maintenance, because bots break when interfaces change. Competitors include Microsoft Power Automate, Automation Anywhere, Blue Prism, ServiceNow, and Appian. UiPath suits large organizations automating back-office work.
