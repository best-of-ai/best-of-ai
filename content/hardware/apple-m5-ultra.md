Apple introduced the M5 Ultra on August 25, 2026, in the new Mac Studio, built the same way as its predecessors: two M5 Max dies fused together over UltraFusion, now running at more than 4.4TB/s of inter-die bandwidth. The 80-core GPU keeps the same core count as the M3 Ultra but adds a Neural Accelerator inside every core, and Apple configures it with 96GB, 256GB, or 512GB of unified memory at up to 1.2TB/s of bandwidth, alongside a 36-core CPU and 32-core Neural Engine.

That top-end 512GB configuration is what makes it interesting outside the usual video-editing and 3D crowd: it's enough unified memory to load large open-weight models locally without the multi-GPU setup a comparable amount of VRAM would otherwise require.
